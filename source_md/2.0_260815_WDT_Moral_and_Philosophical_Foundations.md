# Abstract {.unnumbered .unlisted}

The WDT whitepaper demonstrates that an accrual-basis tax on annual wealth deltas can be made mechanically coherent. This paper addresses the prior question: what the WDT is for, what values it expresses, and why those values lead to this design rather than another.

The argument proceeds from a single foundational axiom: individual human beings are the only legitimate moral subjects of a tax system. Legal instruments are vehicles through which human wealth is organised, not entities with welfare of their own. Everything that follows derives from that axiom applied consistently.

The paper argues that extraordinary private wealth is partly a collective product, dependent on the labour, consumption, and tax contributions of the broader population. This grounds the WDT's claim that proportional contribution to maintaining the conditions enabling accumulation is morally appropriate, not merely politically convenient. At sufficient scale, wealth is also better understood as durable economic and institutional power than as deferred consumption, which is the basis for rejecting the consumption-tax framework at the upper end of the distribution.

The terminal goal is democratic flourishing: maintaining the conditions under which ordinary people retain meaningful participation in the societies their labour and consumption sustain. This sets a harder success criterion than revenue generation alone. An implementation that collected WDT revenue but directed it away from reducing the burden on labour income would satisfy the fiscal mechanics while failing on its own terms.

The cooperative architecture, including the symmetric loss-refund mechanism, pre-funded obligations, and governance participation, follows from the same foundational axiom rather than being a political concession attached after the fact. A state that participates in gains while offering nothing in loss years is a selective extractor. The WDT is designed to move away from that.

The paper concludes by naming six points at which theoretical purity and practical design part company. These are presented as compromises, not as natural extensions of the theory.

# Glossary {.unnumbered .unlisted}

**Collective production argument:** The claim that extraordinary private wealth accumulation depends on the labour, consumption, and tax contributions of the broader population, and that the systems enabling accumulation therefore require proportional reinvestment in return.

**Cooperative architecture:** The set of design features, including the symmetric loss-refund mechanism, pre-funded obligations, and governance participation, that treat taxpayers as participants in a reciprocal arrangement rather than subjects of an extractive one.

**Democratic flourishing:** The terminal goal of the WDT. The preservation of conditions under which democratic institutions remain functional and ordinary people retain meaningful participation in the societies their labour and consumption sustain.

**Exemption threshold:** The net-worth level above which an individual falls within the WDT system. Set high enough to exclude the substantial majority of households, on both administrative-cost grounds and a moral judgment about differential capacity to bear bad years.

**Labour tax relief dividend:** The progressive reduction in taxes on wages and consumption that WDT revenue is intended to finance. The mechanism through which the terminal goal is actually pursued rather than merely stated.

**Morally survivable equilibrium:** The condition the WDT aims to maintain: wealth concentration constrained to a degree compatible with the continued functioning of democratic institutions, rather than eliminated or ignored.

**Named compromises:** The six points ((MF §9.1) through (MF (MF §9).)6) at which the WDT's theoretical foundations and its practical design part company, stated as trade-offs under constraint rather than as natural extensions of the underlying theory.

**Prospective participation:** The moral basis for taxing future wealth deltas rather than existing wealth stocks. New wealth created after the WDT's reciprocal terms are publicly known is accumulated within a framework whose obligations are understood in advance. Taxing future deltas is therefore participation in a mutually understood arrangement, not retrospective imposition.

**Reciprocal partnership:** The working characterisation of the state/taxpayer relationship under the WDT, adopted in (GOV.A (MF (MF §E)).) The state accepts downside exposure alongside the taxpayer through the symmetric refund, rather than participating only in gains.

**Wealth as power:** The characterisation of large net worth as durable economic and institutional power that operates in the present, rather than as deferred consumption awaiting a future spending event. The basis for the WDT's departure from consumption-tax logic at the upper end of the distribution.

# 1. Introduction

The WDT whitepaper establishes that an accrual-basis tax on annual wealth deltas, combined with a symmetric loss-refund mechanism and a pre-funded Sovereign Wealth Fund, can be made internally consistent, administratively viable, and theoretically grounded in the Haig-Simons tradition. This paper addresses the prior question: what the WDT is for, what values it expresses, and why those values lead to this design rather than another.

The WDT did not originate from a moral theory and work downward to a mechanism. It originated from dissatisfaction with the political slogan "tax the rich" — widely held, rarely examined, almost never specified. Building a mechanism that could actually do what the slogan gestured at required cooperative features to survive contact with its targets, constitutional features to protect its commitments, and governance features to prevent capture. This paper is retrospective articulation: it explains what the design implies about ownership, fairness, and the relationship between concentrated wealth and democratic society. The philosophy followed the mechanism.

The argument proceeds from a single foundational axiom: individual human beings are the only legitimate moral subjects of a tax system. Corporations, trusts, funds, and other legal instruments are vehicles through which human wealth is organised, not entities with welfare of their own. The choice of tax base, the cooperative architecture, the refund mechanism, the fund structure — all of it derives from that axiom applied consistently.

The WDT is not a proposal to eliminate wealth concentration or dismantle capitalist accumulation. Extreme concentration of wealth is a structural feature of advanced capitalist economies. The WDT's claim is narrower: that unconstrained concentration eventually destabilises the democratic institutions and social conditions on which capitalist economies themselves depend, and that a mechanism is therefore required to maintain a morally survivable equilibrium. The goal is constrained coexistence, not abolition.

This paper is also not an exercise in ideal theory. It is written for the world as it is: one in which concentrated wealth commands real institutional power, capital is mobile, and a tax system that cannot survive contact with its targets achieves nothing. At several points the cleanest moral argument and the workable design argument point in different directions. These are compromises reached where theory meets practice, not presented as if the theory had always required them.

# 2. The Individual as the Only Legitimate Moral Subject

Tax systems operate on people. This seems obvious until a corporation, a fund, or a trust becomes the unit of assessment, at which point the human beings behind those instruments can disappear from view. The WDT begins by refusing that disappearance.

The experience of wealth accumulation — the security it provides, the optionality it creates, the influence it confers, the capacity to transmit advantage across generations — belongs to people rather than to legal structures. A person whose equity appreciates by a significant amount has experienced something real: reduced material anxiety, expanded freedom, greater leverage over their own circumstances and those of others. A corporation whose market capitalisation increases has experienced nothing. Its shareholders may have experienced something, and the WDT is interested in them. The corporation is an instrument.

A natural objection is that modern institutions produce effects and exercise power that cannot be reduced to the intentions or actions of any single individual. That is true as a description of causal complexity. It is not a reason to dissolve moral accountability. Human beings deliberately design, exploit, and benefit from institutional complexity. The opacity of a structure does not neutralise the responsibility of the people who constructed and operate it. Causal complexity explains how outcomes arise; it does not determine who bears responsibility for them.

This distinction runs through the whole design. It is why the WDT taxes annual changes in individual net worth rather than corporate profits. It is why the refund mechanism is fully symmetric for individuals: humans experience losses as well as gains, and a state that participates only in upside is a selective extractor rather than a reciprocal partner. The corporate instrument, where one is required at all, is justified on coverage grounds rather than on the moral logic driving the rest of the system. The WDT does not tax corporations because corporations deserve to be taxed. It reaches corporate structures, where it does, because that is sometimes the only practical route to the human beings whose enrichment is the actual subject of concern.

The human experience argument runs deeper than any single mechanism: it is the reason the system needs to work at all, why it requires democratic legitimacy, and why its benefits must flow visibly to ordinary people rather than disappearing into administrative structures.

There is a further foundation for the individual as the right unit of taxation, one that runs through property rather than welfare. Ownership is the authority to make consequential decisions over an asset, coupled with responsibility for the consequences those decisions impose on others and on oneself. A founder who controls a company is the decision-maker. The company is the instrument of their authority. The authority attaches to the person, not the vehicle; the responsibility follows. The WDT does not reduce the authority side of that relationship. A taxpayer within the system retains every right to invest, sell, restructure, or liquidate. What the WDT insists is that as the power created by ownership increases, the reciprocal responsibilities increase proportionally. Rights and responsibilities scale together. This property-theory foundation converges on the same individual-as-subject conclusion as the welfare argument, through a different route. Where decision-making authority is dispersed across thousands of shareholders, the responsibility disperses with it, and the corporate instrument is the practical mechanism for aggregating those dispersed obligations into a collectible form.

# 3. Where Wealth Comes From

Extraordinary private wealth accumulation is the product of ordinary economic activity on a vast scale: workers exchanging labour for wages, households purchasing goods and services, consumers generating the revenues from which corporate profits and equity appreciation flow. Existing structures of capital ownership mean that value generated broadly across the economy concentrates upward through ownership patterns already in place.

A person holding equity in a company serving millions of consumers is, in a meaningful sense, capturing part of the economic activity of those consumers. Their wealth grows not solely through personal effort or ingenuity, but through their position within systems sustained by the participation of everyone below them. The non-ultra wealthy majority are not peripheral to this system. They are its economic foundation. Their labour generates the value that flows upward. Their consumption sustains the revenues driving equity appreciation. Their taxes fund the legal systems, infrastructure, education, healthcare, and contract enforcement on which concentrated private wealth depends.

This is not an argument against private ownership or accumulation through productive enterprise. Legal ownership is not disputed here. What is disputed is the mythology that extreme wealth is entirely the product of individual effort and ingenuity. At the level of ordinary entrepreneurship and productive investment, the connection between individual effort and financial return is real. At the level of extreme wealth concentration, it weakens. Large fortunes emerge heavily from inheritance, timing, structural positioning within ownership networks, and the compounding of advantages already held. At sufficient scale, the individual becomes somewhat incidental to the accumulation structure.

When the WDT asks the wealthiest individuals to contribute proportionally to maintaining the systems enabling their accumulation, it is recognising that extraordinary wealth is partly a collective product, and that the systems enabling its accumulation require proportional reinvestment in return (Murphy & Nagel, 2002).

This has a further implication for where taxation is most efficiently applied. Current systems tax value early in the chain before it has fully concentrated: when wages are earned, goods consumed, transactions completed. This creates friction where reduced pressure would most benefit ordinary households, while slowing the economic activity that ultimately drives wealth accumulation at the top. In its full form, the WDT aims to shift as much of the fiscal burden as possible toward direct taxation of accumulation at the point of concentration. Ordinary households would pay little or nothing in direct tax. Their increased disposable income would generate the economic activity whose gains flow upward through existing ownership structures and are taxed at the point of arrival.

## 3.1 Why the Delta

The collective production argument establishes that contributing proportionally to the systems enabling accumulation is morally appropriate. It does not, by itself, explain why the tax base should be the annual change in wealth rather than the existing stock.

Existing wealth was accumulated under a different social contract. The people who hold it could not have known, at the time of accumulation, that future appreciation would carry reciprocal obligations of the kind the WDT establishes. Imposing those obligations retrospectively would contradict the cooperative principles on which the system is built. A mechanism premised on mutually understood terms cannot fairly apply those terms to wealth accumulated before the terms existed.

New wealth is a different case. Wealth created after the WDT is in operation is accumulated within a framework whose reciprocal obligations are publicly known in advance. Every investment decision, every acquisition, every retained gain occurs after those terms have been announced and enacted. Taxing future deltas is prospective participation in an arrangement whose terms were understood when the wealth was created, not retrospective confiscation.

This also explains grandfathering, which appears in (VAL) as a mechanical design choice. Under the prospective participation principle, grandfathering is not a concession to existing wealth but a requirement of the moral framework. Imposing WDT obligations on wealth accumulated before the system existed would be inconsistent with the same cooperative logic that justifies the system in the first place.

The prospective participation principle holds cleanest in steady state, once the system has been operating long enough that anyone accumulating significant new wealth has done so with full knowledge of the framework. During the implementation phase, the picture is messier. Individuals mid-career at the point of introduction will find the system's terms changing relative to the expectations under which their earlier accumulation occurred. That tension is real and acknowledged as such in (MF (MF §9.6).) (ENV (MF §2)) identifies a further alignment between the delta base and the prospective participation principle: the mechanism's incentive structure generates a population-level preference for mild overstatement rather than minimisation, meaning the rational equilibrium under the delta base nudges toward contribution rather than evasion — an alignment between mechanism design and moral purpose that most tax systems do not produce.

# 4. Wealth as Power, Not Deferred Consumption

Above a threshold of basic sufficiency, wealth does not merely represent future consumption. It compounds into advantages that exist regardless of whether any spending ever occurs.

Significant net worth provides real, present-tense advantages: better healthcare access and outcomes, greater psychological security, expanded geographic freedom, disproportionate political influence, stronger legal protection, and the capacity to transmit accumulated advantage to the next generation. These effects do not depend on liquidation or expenditure. A person with substantial illiquid assets is materially better positioned than someone with none, before a single asset is sold.

Beyond a certain scale, wealth undergoes a qualitative change in character. The distinction between liquid and illiquid holdings, which matters considerably at moderate wealth levels, weakens substantially at extreme ones. Very large asset positions generate their own effective liquidity through leverage against appreciating collateral, through institutional credit access unavailable to ordinary borrowers, through the ability to monetise influence itself. A founder whose entire net worth is nominally locked in private equity is not liquidity-constrained in the way the term implies for ordinary households. The constraints that make illiquid wealth feel different from liquid wealth dissolve at sufficient scale.

The consumption-tax tradition, most rigorously expressed in Bradford's X Tax (Bradford, 1986), treats wealth as fiscally irrelevant until converted into spending. The normative case for this — that saving merely defers consumption rather than extracting resources from society at that moment — was developed most fully by Kaldor (1955). The WDT rejects this framing, not because the consumption-tax literature is wrong on its own terms, but because its terms become increasingly inapplicable at the upper end of the wealth distribution. Where holdings are so large they may never be meaningfully consumed, and are instead continually compounded, transferred at death, placed into trust structures, or directed toward philanthropy, wealth-as-deferred-consumption is not a convincing description. The wealth is durable economic and institutional power that exists and operates in the present.

Taxing wealth accumulation as it occurs reflects the view that economic power becomes fiscally relevant when it exists, not when it is eventually spent.

# 5. The Cognitive Moral Hazard of the Current System

Current tax systems obscure the mechanisms by which wealth concentrates. This is not incidental to how those systems were designed and have been maintained.

Ordinary households pay income tax, payroll tax, and consumption taxes throughout their working lives. The transactions are visible, the deductions itemised, the burden felt directly. The processes by which large fortunes compound operate largely outside this visible register. Unrealised appreciation accrues without a tax event. Ownership structures intermediate between individual and asset. The connection between ordinary economic activity and the equity appreciation flowing to those at the top is largely invisible within the structure of the system itself.

This matters because democratic deliberation about taxation depends on the information available to participants. A system that makes one set of fiscal relationships highly visible while obscuring another does not produce neutral political outcomes. The ordinary worker can observe extreme wealth concentration. What they cannot easily observe is the structural process by which it occurs: that their labour generates value flowing upward through ownership structures, that their taxes fund the infrastructure on which large-scale private wealth depends.

The WDT makes the relationship visible and explicit. By taxing wealth accumulation directly and transparently, linking taxation to reciprocal obligations, and routing revenue through institutional structures with meaningful governance participation, the system makes the connection between concentrated wealth and the society enabling it observable rather than obscured.

Making the mechanisms visible does not determine what conclusions people should draw from them. Reasonable people, looking clearly at how wealth concentrates, can legitimately disagree about what fiscal response is appropriate. The WDT's case depends on claiming that democratic deliberation about these questions is better conducted when the underlying mechanisms are observable, not that informed people would inevitably support this particular response.

The political consequences of the current system's opacity are damaging in ways that cut across usual distributional arguments. Even without understanding the mechanisms clearly, ordinary people have rational grounds for resentment when labour and consumption are taxed continuously while unrealised wealth compounds at comparatively low effective rates. That resentment is structurally accurate even when mechanically uninformed. The political responses this generates — populism, punitive redistribution, and institutional erosion — can damage the conditions democratic capitalist economies depend on, including conditions relied upon by ordinary people themselves. The obscurity of the current system does not protect against these responses. It may accelerate them.

# 6. Democratic Flourishing as the Terminal Goal

The WDT is an instrument, not an end in itself.

The legitimacy of concentrated private power depends on its continued compatibility with broad democratic human flourishing. This sets a harder success criterion than revenue generation alone. If the mechanical design succeeds — the tax is collected, the fund capitalised, the refunds paid, the administration functioning — but ordinary people remain materially constrained, politically marginalised, and structurally subordinate to concentrated private power, then the WDT has failed on its own terms.

The system's benefits must therefore flow visibly and meaningfully to those who are not its primary taxpayers. The labour tax relief dividend — the progressive reduction in taxes on wages and consumption that the WDT is intended to finance — is not a secondary political consideration. It is the mechanism through which the terminal goal is actually pursued. An implementation that taxed wealth deltas successfully but used the revenue to fund activities unconnected to the welfare of ordinary households would satisfy the fiscal logic while missing the moral point entirely.

The terminal goal is democratic flourishing, not equality of outcome. The WDT does not aim to eliminate wealth concentration or flatten the distribution of economic advantage. It aims to maintain the conditions under which democratic institutions remain functional and ordinary people retain meaningful participation in the societies their labour and consumption sustain. The WDT tolerates, and in some respects depends upon, the continued existence of significant private wealth accumulation. What it does not tolerate is wealth concentration so extreme and unconstrained that it captures the institutional conditions democratic legitimacy depends on, including legal systems, political processes, and regulatory frameworks. The empirical basis for this concern is substantial: Dahl (1985) argued that economic inequality of sufficient magnitude is incompatible with political democracy; Bartels (2008) and Gilens (2012) provide evidence that elected representatives are systematically more responsive to the preferences of wealthy constituents than to those of ordinary voters, and that policy outcomes align with high-income preferences at rates substantially above chance when preferences diverge. (ENV (MF §4)) develops the transmission channel through which the labour relief dividend actually pursues this goal: bilateral removal of employer and employee National Insurance Contributions simultaneously dissolves the standard incidence ambiguity and reduces the employment-relationship cost for both parties, making the connection between WDT revenue and the welfare of ordinary households mechanically traceable rather than diffuse.

# 7. The Cooperative Architecture

The cooperative design of the WDT — the symmetric loss-refund mechanism, pre-funded obligations, and governance participation — is sometimes interpreted as a political concession: an otherwise extractive system softened to secure acceptance from those subject to it. That interpretation misses how the architecture developed.

The moral and strategic cases for cooperation are not two separate arguments that happen to converge. They are grounded in the same observation about the population the WDT must reach. The individuals most affected by the tax are also those with the greatest capacity to restructure, relocate, and resist. A purely extractive system gives that population strong rational incentives to destroy it, and the history of progressive fiscal reform offers enough examples of technically sound proposals that did not survive that contact. The cooperative architecture is a response to that fact. But it is also what follows from the moral framework itself: a system that participates in upside gains while offering nothing in downside years is a selective extractor — which is more or less what the WDT is designed to move away from.

The specific mechanisms are worth describing plainly. The symmetric refund means the state participates in wealth losses as well as gains. The pre-funded Sovereign Wealth Fund means the refund commitment is credible even during downturns, when it would otherwise be most tempting to suspend. Governance participation means those bearing the primary fiscal obligation have a meaningful institutional voice in how the fund is managed. Not control, but presence. The cooperative architecture does not require transparency as a condition of participation: (BEHAV (MF (MF §6).)4) establishes that full privacy for any individual taxpayer is compatible with the mechanism functioning identically across revenue, refund symmetry, and deterrence. The state does not demand disclosure as the price of reciprocal partnership; the partnership holds regardless of how the taxpayer elects to participate.

Whether these features materially change behaviour, and whether some taxpayers remain within the system who would otherwise restructure or relocate, is an empirical question the framework cannot answer in advance. The claim is more limited: a system that offers reciprocal protections is likely to be more durable than one that does not, both because it alters incentives and because it is harder to characterise as purely adversarial in political debate.

The working characterisation of the state/taxpayer relationship under the cooperative architecture has since been settled. This paper identified three candidate framings — reciprocal partnership, institutional legitimacy exchange, and democratic co-investment — and declined to choose between them. (GOV.A (MF §E)) resolves the question and adopts reciprocal partnership as the governing characterisation, on the grounds that the architecture across (MF §2) through (MF (MF §10)) most fully embodies that framing. The symmetric refund is the operational expression of the state accepting downside exposure alongside the taxpayer. Governance participation is the structural expression of that co-participation at the institutional level. The three-way analytical distinction this paper draws stands on its own terms; (GOV.A (MF §E)) is the settlement of which framing the mechanism most fully embodies. A further observation follows from (POL (MF (MF §5).)7): the cooperative architecture's political durability is a consequence of the same derivation as its moral correctness, not a separate political calculation layered on top — which is what one would expect of an architecture derived from the foundational axiom rather than designed to survive it.

# 8. Tolerance for Imperfect Compliance

The WDT operates in a world where some wealthy individuals will restructure their holdings to reduce assessed value, some will explore jurisdictions with more favourable treatment, and some will devote substantial professional resources to finding the edges of whatever rules apply. This is rational behaviour in any system where significant sums are at stake and legal avoidance options exist, not a prediction about bad character.

The WDT must therefore be able to function — collecting meaningful revenue, maintaining its refund commitments, preserving institutional integrity — even when compliance is imperfect. A system that depends on near-universal compliance has not solved the avoidance problem. It has assumed it away.

Several features address this. The high exemption threshold concentrates administrative resources on a small population where revenue per unit of compliance effort is highest. The valuation infrastructure, with certified professionals and legal effect across multiple contexts, raises the cost of maintaining inconsistent valuations. The symmetric refund mechanism creates partial incentives toward accurate reporting: understatement in a gain year also reduces the refund base in a loss year, so underreporting is not unambiguously beneficial. The cooperative architecture may reduce avoidance motivation at the margin for those who remain in the system for reasons beyond pure cost minimisation.

None of this makes avoidance disappear. How much avoidance occurs, the forms it takes, and the resulting revenue cost can only be understood through incremental implementation and observation. Tolerance for imperfect compliance is a first-order design requirement, not a concession to political reality. A further dimension is identified in (BEHAV (MF (MF §8.1))1): the WDT's enforcement posture is passive collection rather than active detection, because the mechanism's own architecture makes the most common hiding strategies either self-defeating or self-punishing. A system that claims reciprocal partnership while enforcing through adversarial detection would be philosophically incoherent; the mechanism avoids that incoherence by design, not by assumption.

# 9. Limitations and Required Further Work

Intellectual honesty requires naming the points at which the WDT's theoretical foundations and its practical design part company. Presenting these as natural extensions of the theory, rather than as compromises reached under practical constraint, would weaken both.

## 9.1 Formal modelling gaps

The cooperative compliance literature establishes that perceived procedural fairness improves compliance outcomes, but this result is derived from studies of individuals as direct decision-makers. At the wealth levels where WDT revenue concentrates, the compliance decision is made within institutional networks of advisers, family offices, and trustees. Whether cooperative design features alter the compliance posture of professional intermediaries — and through what mechanism — is a question the existing literature has not asked. The WDT's claim that the cooperative architecture changes the rational calculus of the population best positioned to resist the system is directionally well-grounded; direct evidence does not exist.

## 9.2 Phase One empirical unknowns

Whether the cooperative architecture actually produces the behavioural effects the three convergent arguments in (MF (MF §4)) predict — moral, incentive-based, and political-durability — can only be established through observation of a live system. Phase One is the resolution path. The working assumption is that the cooperative architecture reduces departure and restructuring relative to a purely adversarial system; Phase One will confirm, revise, or contradict it.

## 9.3 Jurisdiction-specific legal and implementation work

The transition period's approximation of prospective consent involves grandfathering of pre-existing wealth, which creates jurisdiction-specific complications around valuation basis, deemed-disposal rules, and treatment of pre-existing offshore structures. The interaction of these mechanics with domestic law requires legal analysis outside this paper's scope.

## 9.4 Structural and irreducible limits of the design

The named compromises in this section are not open questions with resolution paths. They are honest statements of where the WDT's theoretical foundations and practical design part company, collected here so the reader can see the full shape of the trade-offs made.

**The corporate instrument** ((MF §9.1)). The individual-centred moral logic is theoretically clean: tax the humans, not the instruments. For dispersed listed ownership, the individual-level mechanism cannot reliably reach the relevant humans, and the corporate instrument is the only practical way to aggregate the resulting obligations. This is a pragmatic departure from the moral core, justified on coverage grounds.

**The asymmetric treatment of corporate loss years** ((MF §9.2)). The individual WDT is fully symmetric. The corporate instrument is not. Corporations do not experience losses in any humanly meaningful sense; their shareholders do, and those losses are captured through individual assessments. The asymmetry is principled, not a concession. It should be stated as such rather than left to appear administrative.

**The exemption threshold** ((MF §9.3)). A perfectly individual-centred system would assess all humans. The threshold exists partly on administrative-cost grounds and partly on a moral judgment: everyone within the system has sufficient resources to manage the obligations it creates, including in bad years. Some wealth appreciation below the threshold falls outside the system as a result. That is a feature of the design rather than a natural consequence of the theory.

**International mobility and exit** ((MF §9.4)). The moral logic applies to all individuals accumulating wealth within democratic capitalist economies regardless of where they subsequently live. Some individuals will relocate to lower-tax jurisdictions, and the WDT cannot reach them without international coordination that may not be achievable in the near term. The system is designed to function with some emigration rather than to prevent it.

**The assumption of functional democratic institutions** ((MF §9.5)). The WDT depends on a state capable of administering the system neutrally. The structural protections in GOV are real but limited: they create resistance to discrete capture, not immunity from slow drift. The hard problem is a state that honours the mechanics while abandoning the purpose — collecting the tax, paying the refunds, but directing revenue away from the labour tax relief dividend or allowing valuation methodology to drift. The drift form is irreducible. The honest response is residue: a public record durable enough to give a future legitimate effort something to rebuild from.

**The transition period** ((MF §9.6)). The prospective participation principle holds cleanest in steady state. For individuals mid-accumulation-career when the system is introduced, earlier wealth is grandfathered but future appreciation is governed by terms that change. That is an approximation of prospective consent, not the ideal. The voluntary opt-in during Phase One is the institutional expression of the consent logic during transition.

**The institutional tiers the mechanism produces** ((MF §9.7)). A WDT that works produces visible institutional distinctions between people based on their relationship to the mechanism — between participants and non-participants, long-tenure and new entrants, dividend recipients and the general public. None of this was designed to create tiers of citizenship; it emerges from the mechanism's own transactions. The WDT does not introduce tiers to a non-tiered society; it makes existing invisible, unobligated tiers visible, obligated, and subject to democratic constraint. Whether that is sufficient — whether the visible, obligated tiers remain manageable under democratic institutions — is a wager rather than an established claim.

**The individual as unit of taxation and the household boundary** ((MF §9.8)) The individual is the only legitimate moral subject of the tax system — the foundational axiom from which the WDT's design derives. This commitment produces a known and accepted boundary condition: household-level wealth management that distributes assets between individuals is consistent with the mechanism's logic and is not treated as avoidance.

The clearest instance is spousal or partnership wealth transfer below the exemption threshold. A transfer at market value from an above-threshold individual to a below-threshold partner is a real transfer of economic control. The WDT correctly observes less wealth in the transferring individual's assessment because there is less wealth there. Preventing this would require either joint household assessment — abandoning the individual-as-moral-subject axiom — or deemed transfer rules treating real transfers as fictional, which is both philosophically inconsistent and administratively unworkable.

The boundary is self-limiting in practice. It is accessible only to individuals whose wealth is close to the exemption threshold, and at those levels the rate schedule is relatively flat. The tax saving is small relative to the economic cost of relinquishing real control. The strategy does not scale to the wealth levels where the WDT's revenue concentrates. The individual unit of taxation is the right design choice and the right moral commitment; its boundary conditions are accepted as a consequence of taking the foundational axiom seriously, not as a gap to be closed.

**The generational loyalty property of the lifetime envelope** ((MF §9.9)) The lifetime contribution envelope was designed as a compliance mechanism — bounding refund entitlement by prior contribution and preventing strategic cycling. It has a second property: it accumulates cooperative stake across time.

A TP member who has participated through multiple assessment cycles, including loss years where refunds were received, has progressively deeper financial interest in the SWF's solvency and the mechanism's political durability. This is the cooperative architecture operating across time rather than within a single assessment cycle.

The distributional consequence is directionally correct on the project's own moral terms. Entrepreneurial wealth trajectories — volatile, with genuine loss years, built rather than inherited — accumulate larger envelope balances than inherited stable wealth trajectories holding appreciating assets without real downturns. The mechanism is more generous to the person who built something and lost it than to the person who inherited something and held it. This was not designed in explicitly; it follows from the symmetric refund's interaction with the envelope constraint and is consistent with the project's account of where wealth comes from.

The inheritance basis reset should be read in this light. It acknowledges a new participant entering the system fresh while the family's accumulated contribution history persists in the envelope. Whether multi-generational participation reinforces the cooperative architecture's durability or hardens the institutional tiers named in (MF (MF §9.7)) is a question the mechanism's operation will answer, not one the design can settle in advance.

# 10. Conclusion

This paper began with a single foundational axiom: individual human beings are the only legitimate moral subjects of a tax system. Everything that followed — the collective production argument, the departure from consumption-tax logic, the cooperative architecture, and the named compromises — derives from that axiom applied consistently across the design.

The WDT does not resolve the tension between capital accumulation and democratic legitimacy. Accumulation is structurally inevitable under advanced capitalism and often productive. Unconstrained accumulation is structurally destabilising to the democratic institutions on which capitalism itself depends. Both statements are true simultaneously. The WDT accepts this and designs around it rather than pretending it away.

What the WDT attempts is the management of a permanent tension toward a morally survivable equilibrium. The goal is the preservation of the conditions — democratic accountability, institutional integrity, and meaningful participation by ordinary people in the societies their labour and consumption sustain — under which capitalism retains its legitimacy and democratic institutions retain their function. That is a constrained objective reached through constrained means.

The named compromises in (MF (MF §9)) are not failures of the theory. They are the theory meeting the world it must operate within. A corporate instrument that cannot be grounded in the individual-centred moral logic but is required for coverage. An exemption threshold that excludes some accumulation by design. An international mobility gap the system manages rather than closes. A dependence on functional democratic institutions the system cannot itself guarantee. A transition period during which the prospective participation ideal is approximated rather than achieved. And the institutional tiers the mechanism produces by operating correctly: visible, obligated, and democratically constrained, but tiers nonetheless, whose compatibility with democratic flourishing is a wager rather than a guarantee. These are the points at which theoretical purity and practical survivability part company, and naming them honestly is part of what makes the framework credible rather than aspirational.

Readers of the full project sometimes observe that the WDT looks less like a tax proposal and more like a constitutional settlement between capital, democratic government, and citizens. That observation is accurate, but the direction of travel is the reverse of what it implies. The constitutional features were not designed in. They emerged because successive mechanical requirements needed them: cooperative architecture because extraction fails operationally; pre-funding because symmetric refunds create contingent liabilities; governance participation because a pre-funded institution needs protection from capture. Each feature exists because something else required it. That the aggregate looks constitutional may say more about the shape of the underlying problem than about the author's ambitions.

The tension the WDT manages will not be resolved. It will require managing again, in every generation, under whatever conditions advanced capitalist democracies produce. The WDT is one attempt to build an institution capable of doing that work durably.
