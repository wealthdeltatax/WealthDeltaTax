## Where to start

The argument has a logical spine. These five papers establish it in order. Reading them gives a complete picture of mechanism, foundations, valuation, governance, and fiscal viability — everything else builds on or extends this core.

1. (WP) — mechanism design, principal objections, implementation pathway
2. (MF) — why the delta base, and the cooperative architecture
3. (VAL) — how the four valuation routes work and why valuation accuracy is not required
4. (GOV) — how the SWF is governed and protected from capture
5. (RATES) — what the WDT raises and under what assumptions

> If you have 1–2 hours, read these five in order. Everything else is extension, detail, or robustness.

## Why each domain exists

The paper domains follow from the proposition. Each is necessary, not optional.

| If… | Then… | Domain |
|---|---|---|
| WDT taxes annual changes in net wealth | the change must be measurable | Valuation |
| It taxes gains and refunds losses | the refund must be funded | Revenue |
| Capital accumulates in a public fund | someone must govern it | Governance |
| People respond behaviourally | responses must be understood and designed around | Behavioural |
| This is to become law | a credible transition path must exist | Implementation |

## What the project does not yet know

Every open question across all papers is collected and maintained in two places: the [Project Map](Project-Map.html), which carries the full register with status and assignment, and (SCOPE), which distinguishes questions outside the project's scope from those inside it but unresolved.

> The open questions are stated because intellectual honesty requires it — not despite it. A proposal that cannot name what would change its conclusions is not a serious proposal.
