title: "Glossary"
description: "Definitions of key terms used across the Wealth Delta Tax research programme."

---

This glossary defines terms that recur across the WDT paper series. Each entry links to the paper where the concept is developed in detail. Terms are listed alphabetically.

---

**Assessment window**
The period over which the delta is measured for tax purposes. The taxpayer may choose among available windows (e.g. calendar year, fiscal year, or a voluntary settlement date). The choice affects timing but not the lifetime tax liability, which is path-invariant under honest declaration. → (VAL §3)

**Cooperative architecture**
The design principle that the WDT should function as a bargain rather than a unilateral extraction: taxpayers participate in governance through the Taxpayer Chamber, receive symmetric loss refunds, and hold units in the SWF. The architecture is designed to make voluntary compliance the rational response. → (MF §7), (WP §1.1)

**Corporate Delta Levy (CDL)**
The mechanism by which listed companies collect and remit WDT liability on behalf of shareholders, analogous to PAYE withholding. The CDL is applied at a flat rate to the annual change in market capitalisation per share, with settlement accounts for individual reconciliation. → (CORP §5)

**Delta**
The change in an individual's net wealth between two assessment dates. The delta is the WDT tax base. A positive delta (wealth increase) generates a tax liability; a negative delta (wealth decrease) generates a refund. → (WP §3.1)

**Delta base**
The conceptual foundation of the WDT: taxing the *change* in wealth rather than its stock level. This contrasts with a conventional net wealth tax (which taxes the stock) and an income tax (which taxes flows but excludes unrealised gains). → (WP §2)

**Dividend Recipient Chamber (DR)**
One of the three chambers of the Governing Council. Composed of citizens selected by lottery who hold SWF units and receive the labour-tax-relief dividend. The DR provides a constituency for long-run fiscal stability that has no interest in short-term political manipulation of the fund. → (GOV §3), (GOV.B §A.3)

**Enumerated structural clauses**
A set of constitutional constraints on the Governing Council that cannot be overridden by any chamber vote. They define what the WDT is — including the symmetric refund, the SWF structure, and the public valuation register — and prevent the mechanism from being hollowed out through incremental rule changes. → (GOV §5.2)

**Fiscal Sovereign Chamber (FS)**
One of the three chambers of the Governing Council. Composed of holders of senior fiscal offices (Chancellor, OBR Chair, etc.). Provides the state's interest in fiscal stability and long-run solvency. → (GOV §3), (GOV.B §A.2)

**Governing Council**
The constitutional body that governs the WDT mechanism and the SWF. Composed of three chambers: Taxpayer Chamber (TP), Fiscal Sovereign Chamber (FS), and Dividend Recipient Chamber (DR). Decisions require a dual threshold across chambers. → (GOV)

**Grandfathering baseline**
The asset value at the point a taxpayer enters the WDT system, which becomes their initial cost basis. The delta in the first assessment period is measured from this baseline. → (VAL §5.1)

**Labour-tax-relief dividend (LRR)**
One of the two fiscal milestones the WDT is designed to reach. The LRR is the point at which SWF returns are sufficient to fund a meaningful reduction in National Insurance contributions or equivalent labour taxes, providing a direct and visible benefit to working-age citizens. → (RATES §6.2)

**Membrane**
The quality of the relationship between taxpayers and the tax authority — specifically, the degree to which that relationship is characterised by clarity, reciprocity, fairness, responsiveness, and accessibility. A healthy membrane produces cooperative compliance; a degraded membrane produces evasion and resistance. The term is developed in the behavioural paper. → (BEHAV §6)

**Must-transfer rule**
Under Route C (self-declaration of fungible assets), if the declared value is challenged by the tax authority, the taxpayer must sell the asset at the declared price. This creates a self-balancing incentive against understatement: understatement exposes the taxpayer to forced sale below market value. → (VAL §5.2)

**Non-interference constraint**
The design principle that the WDT mechanism should not distort the investment or allocation decisions of taxpayers beyond what is required to collect the tax. The purchase option (Route D) satisfies this constraint because it does not require the taxpayer to sell; it gives the state the *option* to buy at the declared price. → (MF)

**Position closure**
The event at which a taxpayer's WDT account is settled in full — on death, jurisdictional exit, threshold fall-through, or bankruptcy. Position closure triggers a final delta calculation and settles any outstanding liability or refund. → (CLOSE)

**Public Valuation Register**
A register of all declared asset values, accessible to the public. Transparency serves as a deterrent to systematic understatement: a taxpayer who declares a low value for an asset provides a public signal that may attract buyers at that price. → (VAL §9)

**Purchase option (Route D)**
For non-fungible assets under self-declaration, the tax authority retains the option to purchase the asset at the declared value. This is the WDT's adaptation of the Harberger self-assessment mechanism. It does not require compulsory sale; it creates a credible threat that disciplines the declared value. → (VAL §4.4), (VAL §11)

**Route A / B / C / D**
The four valuation routes that handle different asset and declaration types: A = professional valuation of fungible assets; B = professional valuation of non-fungible assets; C = self-declaration of fungible assets; D = self-declaration of non-fungible assets (subject to the purchase option). → (VAL §4)

**Solvency Reserve Requirement (SRR)**
The first of the two fiscal milestones: the point at which the SWF holds sufficient assets to cover one full year of WDT loss-refund obligations. Until the SRR is met, the WDT operates in Phase One with constrained drawdown rights. → (RATES §6.1)

**Sovereign Wealth Fund (SWF)**
The fund into which WDT receipts flow. Citizens hold units in the SWF and receive the labour-tax-relief dividend as returns accumulate. The SWF is the structural reason the WDT is politically durable: it creates a large, diffuse constituency with a direct financial interest in the mechanism's survival. → (WP §4), (GOV.B §E)

**Symmetric loss-refund mechanism**
The WDT's defining feature: losses (negative deltas) generate cash refunds at the same rate as gains generate tax liabilities. Symmetry eliminates the lock-in effect of conventional capital gains taxation, removes the asymmetric treatment of risk that distorts investment, and is the mechanism's principal claim to being a Domar-Musgrave risk-sharing instrument. → (WP §3.5)

**Taxpayer Chamber (TP)**
One of the three chambers of the Governing Council. Composed of WDT taxpayers, with vote shares weighted by tax paid. Provides an organised taxpayer interest in the mechanism's integrity and a structural check on short-term political interference. → (GOV §3), (GOV.B §A.1)

**Voluntary settlement**
A provision allowing taxpayers to crystallise their delta at a date of their choosing, within rules, rather than waiting for the end of the formal assessment window. Voluntary settlement provides liquidity management flexibility without enabling systematic avoidance. → (VAL §6.4)
