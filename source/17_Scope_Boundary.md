# 1. Purpose

The consolidated open questions register (0.0 §26) lists every question the companion paper series raises but does not close. All 30 items are now classified by group: confirmed literature gaps requiring formal modelling (26.1), Phase One empirical questions unanswerable before a live system (26.2), items assigned to MACRO as a Phase One successor (26.3), Governing Council calibration parameters settled in kind but open in value (26.4), and jurisdiction-specific and institutional preconditions that are not design gaps (26.5).

This document records which of those 30 items are outside this project's scope and why. The categorisation matters: different reasons for deferral carry different implications for what a reader or subsequent researcher should do with each item.

# 2. Formal Modelling Tasks

These are analytical tasks this project deliberately does not attempt. They require formal economic modelling expertise and peer-review infrastructure that a single-author policy design project does not carry. All three are confirmed literature gaps in 26.1.

**#1 — Domar-Musgrave formal extension.** The variance-reduction results established by Domar and Musgrave (1944) and extended by Sandmo (1977), Stiglitz (1969), and King (1977) have not been formally generalised to a progressive, delta-based wealth tax — specifically, the multi-period case with the three complications a delta base introduces. That generalisation is a modelling task for researchers with the relevant technical toolkit. LR.A §2.1 characterises the gap precisely. The WDT's design rests on the directional argument that the symmetric refund mechanism produces the Domar-Musgrave effect; the formal proof is not supplied here.

**#2 — Formal welfare comparison.** The welfare comparison between a delta-based wealth tax and a consumption tax alternative — extending Guvenen et al. (2023) from a stock base to a flow base, at current concentration levels — has not been done in the literature and is not done here. LR.A §2.2 characterises the gap. The WDT's design takes a position on this question (WP §8.4); the formal welfare derivation supporting that position is outside this project's scope.

**#3 — Distributional arithmetic of the delta base.** The distributional arithmetic of the delta base under persistent return heterogeneity — how concentration of the delta base evolves over time when returns are systematically higher for larger portfolios — has not been formally derived. LR.A §2.3 characterises the gap. The project's distributional claims rest on the structural argument; the formal derivation requires modelling tools this project does not deploy.

# 3. Comparative Political Economy and International Coordination

These items require either comparative political economy research across multiple jurisdictions or bilateral and multilateral agreement between states. Neither can be supplied by a single-jurisdiction policy design project. Items #7, #8, and #9 are confirmed literature gaps in 26.1.

**#7 — Causal framework validation.** The three-mechanism account of wealth tax abolition derived in POL §3 (legitimacy collapse, organised opposition advantage, institutional brittleness) is an analytical construction built from comparative historical cases. It has not been independently validated using formal comparative political science methods. LR.A §4.1 confirms it as a literature gap. POL §3 states the framework as the project's working explanation; it does not claim independent validation. Validation requires comparative case-study depth outside this project's scope.

**#8 — International competitive dynamic at the political level.** How sustained downward pressure from jurisdictions competing for the mobile WDT population transmits through domestic political institutions to produce political outcomes is underdeveloped in the project. LR.A §4.2 confirms it as a literature gap. POL §7 characterises the pressure; the comparative political economy of how it is absorbed or resisted requires cross-jurisdictional research this project cannot supply.

**#9 — Minimum-tax floor interaction.** Whether a negative-tax year under the symmetric loss-refund mechanism constitutes a breach of an international minimum-tax floor obligation, and how multi-year averaging interacts with that floor, is a legal and treaty question. No refund-based wealth tax has previously existed as a coordination reference point, so the question is novel in the treaty law sense. LR.A §4.3 confirms it as a literature gap. Resolution requires the international legal and tax treaty community.

# 4. Phase One Empirical Questions

These ten items cannot be resolved by further desk research. The conditions under which each can be answered only come into existence once a WDT is operating. PHASE1 specifies evaluation designs for items #4–6, #13–16; items #18, #29, and #30 are flagged as Phase One tasks in ENV, VAL.A, and POL respectively.

## 4.1 Compliance and Behavioural Questions

**#4 — Cooperative compliance at ultra-high-net-worth level.** How professional intermediaries mediate the compliance psychology of ultra-high-net-worth taxpayers under the WDT's cooperative architecture — and whether the administrative-layer design assumptions in BEHAV §4 hold at scale — requires a live system. BEHAV §4.1 assigns this to Phase One cluster 1.

**#5 — Cross-base migration externality magnitude.** Agrawal et al. (2025) identify a six-to-one ratio between within-country and cross-country migration responses for high-net-worth individuals. Whether the WDT population's migration response falls within, above, or below that ratio cannot be determined without observational data from a live system. BEHAV §9.2 assigns this to Phase One cluster 3.

**#6 — Administrative-layer intervention effects on compliance psychology.** The effectiveness of the taxpayer history record and related administrative-layer interventions on compliance psychology is a behavioural question that requires live observation. BEHAV §7 assigns this to Phase One cluster 5, with the taxpayer history record identified as the highest-priority instrument to evaluate.

**#18 — SRR floor calibration under mild-overstatement equilibrium.** If mild overstatement is the stable equilibrium in the self-assessment mechanism (ENV §2), the SRR floor calibration needs to reflect the refund liability implied by that equilibrium rather than by a neutral assessment distribution. What the calibration correction should be depends on observing the equilibrium distribution. ENV §2 assigns this to Phase One.

**#29 — Monitoring instrument for population distribution of α.** The mild-overstatement parameter α (VAL.A §A.6) cannot be observed directly; it must be inferred from patterns in assessment data over time. What monitoring instruments are adequate for tracking the population distribution of α, and at what reporting frequency, are implementation questions. VAL.A §A.6 and BEHAV §10.2 assign this to Phase One.

## 4.2 Institutional and Operational Questions

**#13 — Valuation route adoption distribution and flexibility levy calibration.** The proportions in which taxpayers elect Routes A through D, and the assessment window adoption rate under Route A, determine the flexibility levy calibration. PHASE1 §4.4 assigns this to Phase One cluster 4.

**#14 — OBR independence assessment as mandate-guardian.** Whether the Office for Budget Responsibility can function as the WDT's mandate-guardian without structural modifications to its independence framework is an institutional question answered only by operating the mandate-guardian role. PHASE1 §4.6 assigns this to Phase One cluster 6.

**#15 — Corporate instrument transition conditions.** The conditions under which the corporate delta levy matures to a point where CIT displacement becomes a live policy decision — specifically, what Phase One evidence triggers that decision — cannot be specified in advance. PHASE1 §4.7 assigns this to Phase One cluster 7.

**#16 — Housing price net effect.** The net housing price effect of the WDT depends on the composition shift in demand it induces: if the WDT reduces accumulation of residential property as a wealth-holding vehicle, this competes against any reduction in investment supply. The net direction is ambiguous. ENV §9.2.2 assigns this to Phase One.

## 4.3 Systemic Risk Question

**#30 — Bootstrapping problem: Phase One vulnerability window.** POL §6 identifies a vulnerability window during Phase One in which the WDT is operationally established but the SRR is not yet filled to the level that makes the refund guarantee unambiguously credible. Whether the mitigation measures specified in POL §6 are sufficient to hold the system through that window is not verifiable by design analysis. Phase One is both the test and the only available test.

# 5. Phase One Successor Tasks

These two items are assigned to MACRO, a paper that cannot be written until Phase One data is available. They are not outside the project's scope in principle; they belong to a future project that this project has explicitly named and scoped. ENV Appendix A sets out what MACRO requires and what it is expected to resolve.

**#11 — Consumption multiplier magnitude and net bias direction of RATES estimates.** The directional analysis in ENV §9.2.3 identifies the consumption multiplier channel and argues that the RATES pre-behavioural revenue estimates are biased in an ambiguous direction relative to post-behavioural outcomes. Quantifying the multiplier magnitude and resolving the net bias direction requires a calibrated general equilibrium model. That model cannot be calibrated without Phase One data. MACRO is the assigned paper.

**#12 — Automation and tax-base migration under different automation trajectories.** ENV §9.2.4 identifies automation as a source of tax-base migration that could affect WDT revenue trajectories differently across automation scenarios. The sensitivity of RATES estimates to those trajectories is a modelling question. Like #11, it requires Phase One calibration data and is assigned to MACRO.

# 6. Governing Council Calibration Parameters

These six items are settled in kind — the WDT specifies what each parameter does, what considerations govern its calibration, and what trade-offs bind — but open in value pending Phase One data. They are not scope boundary questions in the same sense as §§2–5: the project has done the design work; Phase One observation closes the value question.

**#10 — SWF governance Phase One parameters.** GOV.B §A.3.4 specifies the DR floor size, constituency dissolution trigger values, and the DR Phase Two scale problem as parameters requiring Phase One data.

**#17 — τ₀ × W_min joint surface.** SWEEPS §7.1 and SWEEPS.A identify this as the primary cross-dataset tension and the only item in this group resolvable without Phase One data, requiring a second-order sweep extension.

**#19 — Liquidity threshold for thinly traded companies.** CORP §4.1 specifies the threshold's function and the trade-offs that govern its calibration; the value is a Phase One parameter.

**#20 — τ₀ exact calibration.** CORP.A §B.1 specifies the collection-security floor logic; the value is expected to require one to two cycles of Phase One observation.

**#21 — τ_h exact calibration.** CORP.A §B.2 specifies the deterrence-floor-to-τ_m range and the joint calibration with CIT/dividend displacement; the value is expected to require three to five cycles.

**#24 — Assessment window premium exact calibration.** VAL §8 and RATES §4 specify the premium's function; its calibration awaits Phase One adoption distribution data (see also #13).

# 7. Jurisdiction-Specific and Institutional Preconditions

These six items are not design gaps. The project has specified the relevant mechanisms in principle; resolution depends on legal analysis, diplomatic process, or institutional negotiation specific to a jurisdiction. JUR provides the reference jurisdiction architecture; it does not close these items.

**#22 — τ_f diplomatic rate-setting.** The foreign-owner rate τ_f is set by bilateral or multilateral agreement. CORP.A §F specifies the rate's function and the negotiating frame; the rate itself is not a WDT design parameter.

**#23 — Route D auction implementation details.** The conduct rules for the Route D enforcement auction at implementation level — mandatory marketing period, no-bid fallback procedure, treatment of internationally-sited assets, administrative fee schedule — are specified in principle in GOV.B §G. Jurisdiction-specific implementation requires legal drafting.

**#25 — Derivatives valuation methodology for illiquid positions.** The WDT's valuation architecture covers the four primary routes (VAL) and the mathematical framework for self-balancing (VAL.A). Derivatives valuation methodology for illiquid positions in complex structured products is a specialist question for future methodology work in the VAL tradition.

**#26 — HMRC data access agreement for microsimulation.** JUR §4.3 identifies the data access agreement required for microsimulation calibration. Whether HMRC grants access, under what conditions, and on what timeline is an institutional negotiation outside the project's control.

**#27 — Post-Brexit information exchange gaps.** JUR §4.3 identifies the loss of DAC and the treatment of EU-domiciled structures as information exchange gaps that post-Brexit arrangements have not closed. Closing them requires diplomatic process.

**#28 — Constitutional and legal analysis of the Route D auction trigger.** The constitutional status of the compelled-sale trigger, the design of exit closure provisions, and the interaction of the bridging facility with domestic deemed-disposal and freedom-of-movement rules all require legal analysis the project cannot supply. The legal implementation in any given jurisdiction requires qualified legal counsel working within that jurisdiction's constitutional framework.

# 8. What This Document Is Not Doing

Deferring these 30 items is not a claim that they are unimportant. Several are consequential: the Domar-Musgrave extension (#1) and the welfare comparison (#2) would materially strengthen the project's theoretical foundations; the constitutional and legal analysis (#28) is a precondition for implementation in any specific jurisdiction; the bootstrapping vulnerability (#30) and the compliance and behavioural questions (#4–6) are the most consequential Phase One deliverables.

The claim is narrower. This project has done what a single-author policy design project can do. The formal modelling tasks in §2 require a technical toolkit and peer-review infrastructure it does not carry. The items in §3 require comparative political economy research or international legal coordination it cannot supply. The items in §4 and §5 require empirical inputs that do not yet exist. The calibration parameters in §6 are open in value, not in kind. The items in §7 require legal, diplomatic, or institutional resolution that no design paper can substitute for.

The project states that plainly rather than leaving 30 forward references that never arrive.
