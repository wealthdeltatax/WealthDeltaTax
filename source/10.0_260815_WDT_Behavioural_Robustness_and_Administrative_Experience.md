# Abstract {.unnumbered .unlisted}

This paper addresses behavioural failure in wealth taxation: the tendency of tax systems to function only under the behavioural assumptions built into their design, and to become fragile or ineffective when taxpayers respond differently. It argues that behavioural robustness — the degree to which a mechanism's effectiveness is independent of any particular assumption about how taxpayers will behave — is a design property that can be derived and argued from first principles, without requiring behavioural predictions the empirical record cannot support.

The paper develops a taxonomy of five friction types that drive the most damaging behavioural responses to taxation regardless of headline rate: information friction (complexity), visibility friction (opacity), legitimacy friction (perceived unfairness), feedback friction (delay), and compliance friction (administrative burden). From this taxonomy, five design principles are derived. The paper then introduces The Membrane — the layer of procedures, communications, defaults, and feedback loops through which taxpayers experience the institution — and analyses the interaction structure between its five health dimensions, showing how failures in some dimensions generate downstream degradation in others. It maps how the WDT's existing mechanism design satisfies each principle, where Membrane-level administrative interventions are required, and where the design is most exposed. Nine behavioural shapes from full cooperative compliance to active resistance characterise the distribution of responses the mechanism must remain functional across. The paper includes a full asset-class route distribution analysis establishing the empirical basis for the enforcement paradigm shift claim, a six-part response to the cross-base fiscal externality, and an analysis of membrane calcification as the institutional drift the WDT's architecture addresses least directly. Phase sequencing analysis establishes that membrane investment is front-loaded and cannot be deferred.

The paper does not model behavioural outcomes, does not predict elasticities, and does not resolve the empirical questions it names. It sets up the design framework within which Phase One can generate evidence on those questions.

# Glossary {.unnumbered .unlisted}

**Behavioural friction:** Any cognitive or administrative variable that makes honest compliance more costly, more uncertain, or less legible than avoidance or exit.

**Behavioural robustness:** The degree to which a tax system's effectiveness is independent of any particular assumption about how taxpayers will behave. A behaviourally robust system functions across the distribution of responses rather than only under the assumptions its designers found convenient to make.

**Behavioural shape:** One of nine identified categories of taxpayer response, ranging from full cooperative compliance (Shape 1) to active resistance and non-compliance (Shape 9). The shapes are not predictions about frequency; they are a complete map of the range against which robustness is evaluated.

**Compliance friction (Type V):** The direct cost of complying honestly with a tax system — professional advice, record-keeping, time spent navigating rules — which functions as a tax on honest engagement and can make avoidance or restructuring economically preferable to remaining within the base.

**Cross-base revenue loss:** The fiscal loss associated with wealth-tax-driven emigration that extends beyond the wealth tax itself to include income tax and VAT revenues attributable to the departing taxpayer, as documented in @AgrawalEtAl2025.

**Exit closure:** The event by which a taxpayer's WDT assessment position ends on jurisdictional departure. The mechanism settles the accrued running account — calculating the final delta, honouring any symmetric refund entitlement, and completing valuation — without treating departure as an act requiring a fiscal penalty. Physical departure is decoupled from settlement completion by the bridging facility. The full position closure framework is in (CLOSE).

**Feedback friction (Type IV):** The experience of delay and unpredictability in the system's response to taxpayer action, which causes the institution to be experienced as unresponsive regardless of whether the substantive outcome is correct.

**Information friction (Type I):** Uncertainty about liability arising from complexity or ambiguity in the rules, which drives route-shopping and professional avoidance activity independently of the headline rate.

**Legitimacy friction (Type III):** Perceived procedural unfairness in the operation of the tax system, which erodes compliance outcomes even among taxpayers who would benefit financially from the system's continuation.

**Membrane calcification:** The tendency of a mature institution to preserve Membrane processes that made sense at implementation even when the taxpayer population and behavioural distribution have changed.

**Membrane health:** The aggregate condition of The Membrane across the five friction dimensions. A membrane is healthy to the degree that each friction type is minimised; it is degraded to the degree that any friction condition fails.

**Membrane-first sequencing:** The implementation principle that the administrative layer governing taxpayer experience must be operational before or simultaneous with the first assessment cycle, not developed in parallel with it.

**Phase One:** The implementation phase in which the mechanism is untested, the Membrane is unproven, and behavioural uncertainty is at its highest. Distinguished from Phase Two (stabilisation) and Phase Three (maturity) by the absence of empirical data on actual behavioural responses.

**The Membrane:** The permeable boundary between a tax system and the taxpayers it governs. It is the layer of procedures, communications, defaults, and feedback loops through which the taxpayer experiences the institution and through which the institution receives information from the taxpayer. Distinct from the mechanism.

**Visibility friction (Type II):** The opacity of the relationship between what a taxpayer pays and what they receive in return, which causes the tax to be experienced as confiscation rather than participation and prevents the institution from generating the public legitimacy that sustains it through hostile political periods.

# 1. Introduction

If you tax wealthy people heavily enough to matter, they will leave. This is not a fringe view or a position requiring any particular political conviction. It is the default assumption of most people who have thought about wealth taxation for more than a few minutes, and it is durable because it is grounded in something real: wealthy people have exit options most people do not, and every wealth tax attempted so far has given them good reason to use them.

The WDT cannot prevent wealthy people from leaving. No tax system can. The question this paper addresses is whether a tax system can be designed to remain functional across the full range of behavioural responses it will encounter — including exit — rather than only under the behavioural assumptions its designers found convenient to make.

The emigration objection is durable for three reasons, each of which deserves to be named precisely.

The first is that it is structurally correct about existing systems. Under a realisation-based capital gains regime, a taxpayer who defers realisation, departs the jurisdiction, and crystallises their gain in a lower-tax territory has done nothing illegal. They have done exactly what a rational actor does when a system offers them no reason to stay and a clear financial advantage to leaving. The problem is not the behaviour. The problem is the design that made the behaviour rational.

The second reason is that exit taxation as currently practised does not close this gap. Most exit tax regimes are either absent, easily structured around, or set at rates that a sufficiently wealthy taxpayer finds net-positive to trigger. Where deemed disposal rules exist, they interact inconsistently with bilateral tax treaties in ways that sophisticated advisers exploit as a matter of routine. Departure from most jurisdictions carries a manageable or negligible tax cost.

The third reason is that international mobility compounds both of the above. A taxpayer with assets and connections across multiple jurisdictions is not choosing between paying and not paying. They are choosing between jurisdictions, all of which are competing for their presence on adversarial terms: each attempting to extract as much as possible while offering as little reciprocal structure as necessary to retain them. That is the game as currently played, and rational actors play it rationally.

The WDT does not claim to win that game. It claims to change the game.

The symmetric loss refund is the mechanism by which it does so. For the first time in the history of wealth taxation, a taxpayer within scope has a financial partner in both directions: a system that shares in their losses at the same rate at which it participates in their gains. That is not an enforcement mechanism. It is a structural reason to remain engaged with the institution that no adversarial system has ever offered.

Behavioural robustness — the degree to which a tax system's effectiveness is independent of any particular assumption about how taxpayers will behave — is a design property, not a prediction. It can be built in or left out. It can be analysed from first principles. The sections that follow establish the friction taxonomy that drives damaging behavioural responses, the design principles that follow from it, the concept of The Membrane and the interaction structure of its health dimensions, the nine behavioural shapes the mechanism must remain functional across, and the empirical basis for the enforcement paradigm shift claim. The paper does not claim to know what people will do. It claims the institution will function across the distribution of things they might do, including leaving.

# 2. Why Behaviour Cannot Be Predicted

## 2.1 What the Evidence Shows

The cross-country literature on wealth taxation is more developed than it was a decade ago, and its primary lesson is not the one most people expect. The headline finding is not that wealthy people leave en masse, nor that they stay put and pay without complaint. Responses vary enormously in ways that resist simple explanation.

Norway is the most carefully studied case. Following an increase in the wealth tax, Kleven and colleagues (2024) found that migration among affected taxpayers did increase — the effect was real and measurable. Revenues from the wealth tax also continued to grow. Both are simultaneously true, which already complicates any clean story about what a wealth tax does to behaviour.

Colombia tells a different story. Londoño-Vélez, J., & Avila-Mahecha, J. (2025) document responses substantially larger than those observed in Norway. The apparent implication is that wealthy populations respond more aggressively to wealth taxes in some countries than others. But their analysis points toward something more specific: the scale of the response tracks closely with the weakness of third-party reporting infrastructure. Where that infrastructure is thin, responses are large. Where it is strong, responses are smaller. The rate of the tax appears in both cases, but it does not explain the difference between them.

@AgrawalEtAl2025 add a further layer. Their analysis suggests that when wealth-tax-driven migration occurs, the fiscal losses are not limited to the wealth tax itself: income tax and VAT revenue associated with departing taxpayers are lost as well, at a scale that can be considerably larger than the direct wealth-tax revenue loss. This finding belongs in any honest account of what the literature contains and is returned to at length in (BEHAV §9.2).

## 2.2 Why the Numbers Don't Travel

Those three findings do not add up to a general rule. They add up to three descriptions of three situations, each shaped by factors specific to its context.

For the Norway finding to transfer reliably to another country, the institutional architecture, compliance culture, political history, available departure destinations, professional advice industry, and public narrative around the tax would all need to be comparable. None of those conditions transfers automatically. The norms that shape whether a wealthy person in a given country regards tax as an obligation or an imposition are not captured in any dataset. The degree to which taxpayers feel the system is operating in rough good faith, or extracting opportunistically, shapes their responses in ways no study has reliably measured.

The Colombia comparison illustrates this from the other direction. The difference between Norway and Colombia in observed behavioural response is large enough that it cannot plausibly be explained by differences in the headline rate alone. The candidates — reporting infrastructure, institutional trust, available alternatives, the character of professional advice — are all real, but quantifying their relative contributions is at least as hard as measuring the rate effect itself.

This is not an argument that the literature is uninformative. The Norway and Colombia evidence supports some directional claims with reasonable confidence: strong third-party reporting infrastructure tends to reduce avoidance responses, and symmetric loss participation is more consistent with the Domar-Musgrave risk-sharing tradition than asymmetric treatment. What the literature cannot do is supply the magnitudes a designer would need to calibrate a mechanism against a specific predicted response in a specific future jurisdiction.

## 2.3 The Design Implication

Designing around specific predicted responses is an unstable foundation. A mechanism that only functions if migration stays below a certain threshold, or avoidance within a certain range, has absorbed a prediction as a design assumption. When the prediction is wrong, the mechanism inherits the error.

The alternative is to ask what properties the institution should have so that it remains functional across the range of things taxpayers might do. That shifts the design objective from prediction to robustness.

# 3. Behavioural Robustness as a Design Objective

## 3.1 The Concept Defined

Behavioural robustness is the degree to which a tax system's effectiveness is independent of any particular assumption about how taxpayers will behave. A perfectly robust system does not require taxpayer cooperation. It does not assume optimising rationality. It does not depend on any single behavioural prediction being correct. It functions across the distribution.

This makes robustness measurable in principle, at least directionally. A system that only functions under cooperative behaviour is less robust than one that also functions under hostility, avoidance, or indifference. A system calibrated to a specific migration rate is less robust than one that remains functional whether migration is higher or lower than anticipated. The question to ask of any design feature is not whether it produces the desired behaviour, but whether the mechanism continues to work if the behaviour turns out differently.

## 3.2 What Robustness Is Not

Behavioural robustness is not the same as behavioural prediction, and the difference matters.

Prediction asks: what will taxpayers do? It requires a model of behaviour specific enough to generate estimates of avoidance rates, migration propensities, and portfolio (responses. §2) established why those estimates cannot be supplied with the precision a calibrated mechanism would need. Robustness asks instead whether the mechanism remains functional across the range of plausible responses, including responses the designer did not anticipate. It is not a weaker form of prediction. It is a different objective.

Robustness is also not behavioural engineering. Engineering asks: how do we produce the response we want? The WDT does shape incentives — the symmetric loss refund, the self-balancing valuation mechanism, and the lifetime contribution envelope all alter the rational calculus facing taxpayers deliberately. But the mechanism is not designed around the assumption that any particular incentive will produce any particular response reliably. It is designed so that the institution remains legitimate and functional across the range of responses those incentives might actually produce. That is a harder standard than engineering a specific predicted outcome, because it cannot be achieved by getting one prediction right.

## 3.3 The Same Logic Applied Twice

(GOV) confronts a structurally identical problem at the institutional level. GOV does not assume that the people appointed to the Governing Council, the Valuation Bodies, or the SWF Custodian will behave well. It designs constraints that function under either assumption. This paper applies the same logic one level down: where GOV asks how the institution responds to imperfect governors, this paper asks how the institution responds to imperfect citizens.

(VAL) addressed informational failure: the impossibility of the state discovering the correct value of assets a taxpayer prefers to undervalue. (GOV) addressed institutional failure: the tendency of well-designed institutions to be captured, drifted, or dismantled. This paper addresses behavioural failure: the tendency of tax systems to function only under the behavioural assumptions built into their design. The design response is robustness: not prediction of behaviour, not engineering of behaviour, but construction of an institution whose effectiveness does not depend on behaviour falling within a predicted range.

# 4. Sources of Behavioural Friction

Robustness requires knowing what to be robust against. The answer is not, primarily, the tax rate. The empirical literature on tax compliance and avoidance is consistent on this point: the magnitude of damaging behavioural responses is driven not by headline rates but by a small number of friction types that operate largely independently of the rate itself. These are cognitive and administrative variables. They shape how taxpayers experience a system, how they understand their obligations under it, and whether they regard honest engagement as the rational path. A mechanism that removes these friction types will perform better across the distribution of taxpayer behaviour than one that does not, regardless of what that distribution turns out to be.

Five friction types can be identified. Naming them precisely does work: it allows the design principles in (BEHAV §5) to be derived from the taxonomy rather than asserted, and allows the interventions in (BEHAV §5) to be explained as responses to specific identified problems.

## 4.1 Type I — Information Friction

When the rules governing a tax are difficult to understand, taxpayers face uncertainty about their liability. That uncertainty is resolved in one of two ways, neither of which serves the system well. Some taxpayers engage professional advice to navigate rules they cannot parse themselves — rational behaviour, but it creates and sustains a professional avoidance industry whose commercial incentive is to find the boundary of the rules rather than stay comfortably within them. Other taxpayers, unwilling or unable to pay for that advice, avoid the taxed activity altogether. Both responses are reactions to uncertainty, not to the underlying rate.

Information friction is the primary driver of route-shopping risk. Where the classification of an asset or the calculation of a liability is ambiguous, the rational taxpayer finds the classification that minimises their obligation. Where it is clear, the incentive to shop disappears.

## 4.2 Type II — Visibility Friction

When the relationship between what a taxpayer pays and what they receive in return is invisible, the tax is experienced as confiscation. A tax whose reciprocal structure is opaque cannot generate the public legitimacy that sustains it through hostile political periods. Taxpayers who cannot see what they are getting for what they contribute have no rational basis for defending the system when it comes under pressure, and every rational basis for resenting it.

Visibility friction is distinct from the size of the reciprocal benefit. A taxpayer who receives a meaningful refund in a loss year but received no advance notification of that entitlement has experienced the same economics as a taxpayer who was clearly informed of their protection. The behaviour those two taxpayers exhibit toward the institution is unlikely to be the same.

## 4.3 Type III — Legitimacy Friction

Perceived procedural fairness materially affects compliance outcomes. This is one of the more robust findings in the cooperative compliance literature (Tyler, 1990; Kirchler, 2007; Gangl et al., 2015), and it applies even among populations that would benefit financially from non-compliance. A tax experienced as structurally rigged — extracting without reciprocating, applying differently to different people through arbitrary distinctions, administered with visible inconsistency — faces legitimacy erosion no enforcement architecture can fully offset.

Legitimacy friction is not the same as visibility friction. A perfectly transparent system can still be experienced as unfair. Transparency addresses one problem, and procedural fairness addresses a different one. Both need to be present.

## 4.4 Type IV — Feedback Friction

When the feedback loop between a taxpayer's action and the system's response is long and uncertain, the system is experienced as unresponsive. Unresponsive systems are worked around rather than engaged with. A refund entitlement that arrives eventually but without notice, after an indeterminate wait, does not produce the same psychological relationship to the institution as one confirmed promptly and arriving when expected. The economics are the same. The experience is not, and the experience is what shapes behaviour.

Feedback friction is specifically relevant to the loss-refund mechanism. The WDT's symmetric refund is designed to make the state a partner in both directions. That partnership is felt, not just calculated. A refund that arrives slowly and unpredictably does not feel like a partnership. It feels like a bureaucratic concession that had to be waited out.

## 4.5 Type V — Compliance Friction

The direct cost of complying honestly — professional advice, record-keeping, time spent navigating rules and processes — functions as a tax on honest engagement. Where that cost is high relative to the underlying liability, the rational response is to reduce both: restructure away from the tax base entirely, eliminating both the liability and the compliance burden in one move. This distortion falls disproportionately on the honest taxpayer, who bears the full compliance cost, relative to the taxpayer who has already paid for professional restructuring and no longer faces the system at all.

Compliance friction also has a professional intermediary dimension. Where honest classification of assets is laborious, professional advisers have both the incentive and the capacity to find classifications that are less laborious but less honest. Where honest classification is easy, the path of least resistance and the path of compliance coincide.

## 4.6 The Design Implication

A wealth tax designed to minimise these five friction types would look substantially different from one designed simply to apply the highest defensible rate to the most comprehensive base. Rate and base matter. But friction is what converts ordinary taxpayer adaptation into the avoidance, exit, and delegitimisation that undermine the institution.

The objective that follows is precise: the honest strategy should be the lowest-friction equilibrium. People follow the path of least resistance in complex systems, and the institution should make sure that path leads somewhere useful. When honest compliance requires more effort, more uncertainty, and more cost than restructuring or exit, the institution has built its own fragility into its design.

# 5. Design Principles for Behavioural Robustness

The friction taxonomy in (BEHAV §4) generates five design principles. Each is stated as a requirement that any behaviourally robust wealth tax must satisfy, derived from the friction type it addresses. They are stated independently of any particular mechanism so that any mechanism can be evaluated against them.

## 5.1 The Clarity Principle

Type I information friction requires that the rules governing liability be unambiguous at the point of application. A taxpayer who understands their obligation without professional assistance has no uncertainty to resolve through route-shopping. A taxpayer who does not understand their obligation will resolve that uncertainty in their own favour, not the institution's.

The Clarity Principle: the determination of liability must be clear enough that honest self-assessment is the path of least effort.

This is a stricter requirement than legislative clarity. A rule can be clearly drafted and still be ambiguous in its application to a specific asset type, a specific ownership structure, or a specific transaction. The test is not whether a lawyer can interpret the rule, but whether a taxpayer can apply it without one.

## 5.2 The Reciprocity Principle

Type II visibility friction requires that the relationship between what a taxpayer contributes and what they receive in return be made explicit and legible. An institution whose reciprocal structure is invisible cannot generate the loyalty that sustains it, and cannot generate the resentment that signals when it is failing its own terms.

The Reciprocity Principle: the benefit structure of the tax must be as visible to the taxpayer as the liability structure.

This requires active communication, not passive availability. The existence of a loss refund entitlement buried in legislation satisfies no version of this principle. The confirmation of that entitlement, delivered to the taxpayer before the loss is realised, does.

## 5.3 The Fairness Principle

Type III legitimacy friction requires that the system be experienced as procedurally fair: applied consistently, without arbitrary distinctions, and with visible accountability for how administrative discretion is exercised. Perceived unfairness erodes compliance among populations that would benefit from the system's continuation, not only among those who would benefit from its removal.

The Fairness Principle: the administrative exercise of the system must be consistent, transparent, and accountable to the same standards it applies to taxpayers.

Fairness in this sense is procedural, not distributive. It is not a claim about whether the tax produces a fair outcome. It is a claim about whether the process by which the tax is administered is experienced as applying the same rules to everyone subject to it.

## 5.4 The Responsiveness Principle

Type IV feedback friction requires that the system's response to taxpayer action be prompt and predictable. An institution that confirms liabilities slowly, processes refunds without notice, and communicates only when something has gone wrong is experienced as adversarial regardless of its formal structure. Taxpayers calibrate their relationship to an institution from experience, not from the legislation.

The Responsiveness Principle: the system must respond to taxpayer action within a timeframe and with a predictability that makes honest engagement feel like engagement with something that is paying attention.

Responsiveness is not a customer service requirement. It is a structural property of cooperative institutions. An institution that responds slowly loses the informational and relational advantage that makes cooperative compliance possible in the first place.

## 5.5 The Accessibility Principle

Type V compliance friction requires that the cost of honest compliance be low enough that it does not drive the rational taxpayer toward avoidance as a cost-reduction strategy. Where compliance is expensive, the taxpayer who has already paid for professional restructuring and exited the tax base has lower ongoing costs than the taxpayer who remains within it and bears the full compliance burden. That asymmetry rewards exit.

The Accessibility Principle: the direct cost of honest compliance must not exceed the cost of the avoidance strategies it competes with.

This is not a requirement that compliance be costless. It is a requirement that the cost of honest engagement not be systematically higher than the cost of dishonest disengagement. Where that condition fails, the institution is selecting against the taxpayers it most needs to retain.

The personal adviser corps operationalises the Accessibility principle at the individual level in a way systemic interventions cannot reach. Systemic improvements reduce average friction; they do not eliminate the comprehension advantage accruing to taxpayers with extensive professional support networks. The personal adviser addresses this residual directly: a TP member navigating their first assessment with a dedicated adviser has a comprehension experience that does not depend on private resources. This is a membrane investment whose return is cooperative compliance from a population whose participation is load-bearing for Phase One credibility.

## 5.6 Administrative-Layer Intervention

An administrative-layer intervention addresses both the individual membrane and the displaced professional services industry simultaneously: a state-funded personal adviser assigned to each Phase One TP member.

The five interventions above are systemic — they improve the average experience across the taxpayer population. The personal adviser is the individual-level complement. At thirty-two thousand initial TP members the population is small enough to support high-touch professional delivery. Each adviser works with a specific taxpayer through their first assessment cycle, covering route classification, delta calculation, window election, and privacy election. The adviser's function is navigation and comprehension, not optimisation against the mechanism. The adviser works for the state, explains the mechanism honestly, and has no mandate to minimise the taxpayer's liability. Taxpayers wanting optimisation continue to engage private advisers; the personal state adviser is the baseline guarantee that the mechanism is comprehensible regardless of private resources.

This has a distributional property consistent with the Accessibility principle. A near-threshold TP member without a family office receives the same quality of mechanism explanation as an ultra-high-net-worth member with extensive professional infrastructure. The comprehension gap that currently makes tax systems more navigable for the very wealthy is partially closed by design.

Advisers should be drawn from the professional intermediary industry the WDT displaces. That workforce carries relevant skills — asset classification, valuation methodology, complex ownership structure analysis — currently oriented toward avoidance. The adviser role reorients those skills toward comprehension and compliance, converts a source of organised Phase One opposition into a constituency with positive interest in the mechanism's success, and builds institutional knowledge of how the mechanism operates across a wide range of asset compositions and ownership structures. Over-employment relative to strict efficiency in Phase One is justified on two grounds: it absorbs more of the displaced professional capacity, and it generates richer institutional learning across a larger number of edge cases than a minimally staffed corps would encounter.

In Phase Two, as the TP population expands, Phase One advisers become the training pool. The institutional knowledge accumulated across thirty-two thousand complex early cases cannot be replicated by any other means.

# 6. The Membrane

## 6.1 Definition

The Membrane is the permeable boundary between a tax system and the taxpayers it governs. It is not the mechanism. It is not the legislation. It is the layer of procedures, communications, defaults, and feedback loops through which the taxpayer experiences the institution and through which the institution receives information from the taxpayer. A mechanism can be sound while The Membrane is degraded. A mechanism can have serious design flaws that a well-functioning Membrane partially compensates for. The two are distinct, and conflating them is one of the more common errors in tax system evaluation.

The Membrane is a general concept describing the boundary between any institution and the people it interacts with, not a property specific to tax design. The five principles in (BEHAV §5) are properties of The Membrane: a tax system satisfies the Clarity Principle, the Reciprocity Principle, the Fairness Principle, the Responsiveness Principle, and the Accessibility Principle to the degree that its Membrane transmits the right signals clearly in both directions. Where a principle is violated, it is almost always The Membrane that has failed, not the underlying mechanism.

Two worked examples — a healthy membrane and two distinct failure modes — are set out in Appendix A.

## 6.2 Membrane Health

Membrane health is the aggregate condition of The Membrane across the five friction dimensions. A membrane is healthy to the degree that each of the five friction types is minimised: information is clear, reciprocal benefit is visible, administration is experienced as fair, feedback is prompt, and compliance is accessible. A membrane is degraded to the degree that any of those conditions fails.

Membrane health cannot be quantified. Experience is subjective, and the same communication that reads as clear and responsive to one taxpayer reads as opaque and slow to another. What can be assessed is whether The Membrane has been designed with each friction type in mind, and whether the observable outputs — communication clarity, refund processing times, dispute resolution processes, self-assessment completion rates — are consistent with a membrane that is functioning. These are measurable proxies, not measurements of health itself.

A degraded membrane is self-reinforcing. When taxpayers experience friction in honest engagement, they disengage. When they disengage, the institution loses the informational signal that would allow it to identify where The Membrane is failing. The system continues operating, but it is operating on a narrowing base of engaged taxpayers while the disengaged population grows and becomes harder to re-integrate.

## 6.3 The Interaction Structure of Membrane Health Dimensions

The five membrane health dimensions are not independent. Failures in some generate downstream consequences in others regardless of whether those others were independently healthy, and some degradation paths are self-reinforcing in ways that resist correction even when the underlying cause is removed. Understanding this matters for two reasons: it determines where membrane investment is most leveraged, and it determines which failure sequences are recoverable. The interaction matrix is summarised at the end of this section; each pairwise relationship and its observable signature follow.

**Clarity and Reciprocity — complementary.** A taxpayer who understands their liability also understands their entitlements. The same rule clarity that removes uncertainty about what is owed removes uncertainty about what is owed back. In the WDT context, clear route classification removes route-shopping incentives on the liability side and simultaneously removes uncertainty about refund entitlement on the loss side. Investment in Clarity therefore yields returns on both dimensions simultaneously. Observable signature: self-assessment completion rates and unprompted refund claim rates should move together; if they diverge — high completion, low claim rates — the Reciprocity dimension is failing independently of Clarity.

**Clarity and Fairness — complementary.** Ambiguous rules are experienced as selectively applied even when they are not. Where classification boundaries are clear, inconsistent administrative outcomes are legible as inconsistency rather than attributable to deliberate arbitrariness. Clarity is therefore a precondition for Fairness being observable at all: a taxpayer cannot evaluate whether the rules were applied consistently to them if the rules themselves are unclear. Observable signature: dispute rates that remain elevated after rules are clarified signal a Fairness problem; dispute rates that fall with clarification confirm that Clarity was the binding constraint.

**Clarity and Accessibility — complementary.** These two dimensions share the strongest complementarity in the framework. A simpler rule reduces both information friction and compliance cost simultaneously — the taxpayer who can self-classify without professional assistance pays neither the uncertainty cost nor the advisory cost. This is where the WDT's four-route architecture does its most important membrane work: collapsing the valuation question from an open problem to a bounded classification problem reduces Compliance friction and Information friction in the same move. Observable signature: professional adviser engagement rates for routine cases; if these fall as route guidance improves, both dimensions are responding.

**Clarity and Responsiveness — tension.** More precise rules generate more edge-case queries. A rule clear enough that a taxpayer can self-assess in routine cases may still require back-and-forth where it meets novel asset structures, lifecycle events, or ownership patterns the rule did not anticipate. Efforts to improve Clarity through elaboration — more detailed guidance, more worked examples, more published interpretations — increase the volume of cases that fall into gaps between the new categories. Responsiveness therefore requires its own resourcing rather than being assumed as a byproduct of clearer rules. Observable signature: query volumes per taxpayer after rule clarification exercises; if these rise rather than fall, Clarity investment has been elaborative rather than simplifying.

**Reciprocity and Fairness — asymmetric.** This is the most important relationship in the framework and the most easily misread. In design, Reciprocity and Fairness move together: a system that participates in the taxpayer's downside at the same rate as their upside ought to feel procedurally fair. But in practice the two dimensions can diverge sharply. A system that delivers symmetric economics but is perceived as administered inconsistently — different processing times for different taxpayers, opaque bases for valuation decisions, discretion exercised without explanation — satisfies Reciprocity and fails Fairness simultaneously. The economics are correct; the experience is not. This is the WDT's most acute Phase One vulnerability: the refund mechanism is symmetric by construction from the first assessment cycle, but the administrative exercise of that mechanism may not feel consistent for years. Reciprocity and Fairness therefore require independent maintenance, not treatment as a single dimension. Observable signature: taxpayer satisfaction surveys and formal dispute rates disaggregated by wealth band and asset type; systematic variation across groups confirms a Fairness failure independent of Reciprocity delivery.

**Reciprocity and Responsiveness — complementary.** The refund mechanism's cooperative character is felt through timing, not just through entitlement. A refund confirmation that arrives promptly before a loss year converts the mechanism's symmetric commitment into a present-tense experience of partnership. A refund that arrives eventually but unpredictably is experienced as a bureaucratic concession, not a structural guarantee. Responsiveness is therefore the channel through which Reciprocity becomes behaviourally real rather than formally correct. Observable signature: refund processing time against subsequent declaration accuracy; if taxpayers who received prompt refund confirmation in loss years show higher subsequent declaration accuracy, the Responsiveness–Reciprocity channel is functioning.

**Reciprocity and Accessibility — neutral.** These two dimensions operate through largely independent mechanisms. Reducing compliance friction does not directly affect the visibility of entitlements, and improving entitlement visibility does not directly affect the cost of honest compliance. They share the same taxpayer population and the same administrative infrastructure, but investment in one does not propagate to the other. This makes Accessibility the one dimension that must be resourced on its own terms without expecting returns elsewhere in the membrane.

**Fairness and Responsiveness — downstream.** Slow or unpredictable system responses are eventually experienced as arbitrary, regardless of whether the underlying process is principled. A taxpayer waiting without status updates for a refund decision that takes four months does not experience that wait as a neutral delay; they experience it as evidence that the system does not treat their case with the same seriousness as others. Responsiveness failures therefore generate Fairness degradation over time even when no actual inconsistency exists. Improving Responsiveness reduces one source of Fairness degradation, but Fairness can also degrade independently through arbitrary administrative discretion that Responsiveness improvements cannot address.

**Fairness and Accessibility — downstream.** A system expensive to engage honestly is experienced as rigged toward those who can afford to disengage. Where compliance friction is high, the taxpayer who has restructured and exited the base has lower ongoing costs than the one who remains and bears the full compliance burden. That asymmetry is not merely an economic distortion; it is perceived as evidence that the system is operating in the interests of those who can afford to leave it. Accessibility failures therefore generate Fairness degradation through the perceived asymmetry they create, independently of whether the rules are in fact applied consistently.

**Responsiveness and Accessibility — resource tension.** These two dimensions compete directly for administrative resource. The investment required to respond to every taxpayer action within defined windows is exactly the kind of overhead that makes honest compliance expensive when funded by compliance fees or administrative levies. A membrane attempting to maximise both simultaneously faces a cost constraint that must be acknowledged rather than assumed away. The appropriate sequencing follows from Phase One data: which dimension's degradation generates faster legitimacy erosion determines which receives first claim on administrative investment.

**The special status of Fairness.** Fairness is downstream of all four other dimensions and degrades independently through arbitrary administrative discretion that none of them can address. A membrane whose Fairness dimension has degraded cannot be restored simply by improving Clarity or Responsiveness; the legitimacy damage requires direct administrative accountability intervention. For practical monitoring purposes, Fairness degradation should be treated as a leading indicator of membrane failure rather than a lagging consequence: by the time Fairness has visibly degraded, the compounding self-reinforcing dynamic is likely already in progress.

## 6.4 The Membrane and the WDT

The WDT mechanism is described in (WP) and its companion papers. This paper's contribution is to analyse The Membrane through which that mechanism will be experienced, to establish the design requirements that follow from the five principles, and to identify the interaction structure through which membrane investment propagates or fails to propagate across dimensions. The sections that follow address each principle in turn, showing where the WDT's existing design satisfies it and where specific administrative interventions are required to do so.

## 6.5 Privacy Compatibility as a Membrane Property

The privacy objection is the most visceral and politically potent attack the WDT will face among populations broadly sympathetic to wealth taxation. The architecture provides a precise answer.

Full privacy for any individual taxpayer is compatible with the WDT functioning identically in every dimension that matters for revenue, compliance, and cooperative architecture. A fully private taxpayer declares their net worth, commits to it, receives confirmation that their tax bill is correctly calculated, pays or receives accordingly, and holds their longitudinal participation record entirely themselves. The mechanism never required the Administrator to hold or publish individual asset data in order to work.

Full privacy does not affect revenue. Full privacy does not affect refund symmetry. Full privacy does not affect the Route D auction deterrent — it operates on the committed declared value, not on what is publicly visible. Full privacy does not affect the lifetime contribution envelope. The democratic-visibility goal at individual level and the price discovery function for Route D assets are both served by aggregate statistical outputs rather than individual disclosure. The Administrator can publish distributional snapshots, delta flow data, and asset-class aggregates derived from the full population including fully private declarations, because generating those aggregates requires only the committed values and the tax calculations, not the underlying asset composition.

Full privacy is not a concession to wealthy taxpayer resistance. It is a consequence of building the Administrator-as-consistency-confirmer architecture correctly. The WDT never needed individual-level disclosure to function. A privacy election is therefore a choice between two equivalent ways of participating in a mechanism that works identically either way — with a fee that compensates the democratic-visibility goal for the aggregate information it receives regardless of individual elections. This reframing addresses legitimacy friction (Type III) among the specific taxpayer population where that friction is highest: the privacy objection dissolves when the architecture demonstrably delivers it.

The personal adviser corps operationalises the Accessibility principle at the individual level in a way systemic interventions cannot reach. Systemic improvements reduce average friction; they do not eliminate the comprehension advantage accruing to taxpayers with extensive professional support networks. The personal adviser addresses this residual directly: a TP member navigating their first assessment with a dedicated adviser has a comprehension experience that does not depend on private resources. This is a membrane investment whose return is cooperative compliance from a population whose participation is load-bearing for Phase One credibility.

# 7. How the WDT Satisfies the Five Principles

## 7.1 The Clarity Principle

The Clarity Principle requires that the determination of liability be clear enough that honest self-assessment is the path of least effort. The WDT's mechanism addresses this more directly than most wealth tax designs, primarily through the four-route valuation architecture set out in (VAL) and (VAL §4).

The four-route architecture matters for Clarity because it eliminates the most common source of information friction in wealth taxation: the open question of how an asset is to be valued. Under a conventional wealth tax, a taxpayer holding illiquid assets faces uncertainty about their liability, because the method of valuation is either unspecified or contested. That uncertainty is the condition under which professional route-shopping flourishes. The four-route architecture resolves it by assigning every asset class to a specific valuation method with known inputs. The taxpayer who understands which route applies to their assets understands their liability. This does not eliminate complexity entirely — the correct route assignment for novel asset structures will occasionally be ambiguous — but it relocates the complexity from an open question to a bounded classification problem, which is a materially different experience for the taxpayer.

The self-balancing valuation mechanism (VAL §6) provides a further contribution to Clarity. Where a taxpayer disagrees with a state-assigned valuation, the mechanism provides a defined process for challenge, with known consequences. Honest self-assessment and dispute are not in tension.

The Membrane must translate the four-route architecture into communications that allow a taxpayer to identify their applicable route without professional assistance in routine cases. That administrative-layer requirement is not yet fully specified. See (BEHAV §9).

## 7.2 The Reciprocity Principle

The Reciprocity Principle requires that the benefit structure of the tax be as visible to the taxpayer as the liability structure, and that visibility be active rather than passive. This is the principle the WDT's mechanism satisfies most directly and most distinctively.

The symmetric loss refund is the central feature. Under a conventional wealth tax, the relationship between taxpayer and state is asymmetric: the state taxes gains and does not share losses. The taxpayer who experiences a loss year has paid tax on wealth they no longer have and receives nothing back. The WDT's symmetric structure changes that relationship materially: the state is a partner in both directions, taxing gains and refunding losses at the same rate.

The condition is visibility. A symmetric loss refund that the taxpayer does not know about in advance does not produce the relational effect the mechanism is designed to achieve. The Reciprocity Principle therefore requires, at the Membrane level, that every taxpayer within scope receive confirmation of their refund entitlement before they need to exercise it. Not after a loss is declared. Before. A taxpayer who enters a difficult year knowing that the institution will share in their loss is in a different relationship with that institution than one who discovers the refund entitlement after the fact.

The Sovereign Wealth Fund (WP §8) contributes to Reciprocity at a second level. The fund's public ownership and visible deployment of accumulated wealth provides the population-level equivalent of the individual refund: a demonstration that the institution is generating returns for the broader public, not simply accumulating state assets.

Both the individual notification architecture and the SWF communication strategy require operational detail not yet provided. See (BEHAV §9)

## 7.3 The Fairness Principle

The Fairness Principle requires that the administrative exercise of the system be consistent, transparent, and accountable to the same standards it applies to taxpayers. This is the principle most dependent on governance architecture rather than mechanism design, and it is the one most thoroughly addressed by (GOV).

GOV's three-chamber Governing Council, the dual-threshold voting rule, and the ten enumerated structural clauses collectively address the institutional conditions for procedural fairness. The enumerated structural clauses define the properties the system must preserve across political periods and include constraints on administrative discretion that directly serve the Fairness Principle.

GOV's architecture constrains but does not eliminate Membrane-level fairness failures: inconsistent administrative interactions, opaque dispute resolution, decisions communicated without explanation. The Membrane must specify that decisions affecting individual taxpayers are accompanied by a statement of basis and a clear route to challenge. See (BEHAV §9).

## 7.4 The Responsiveness Principle

The Responsiveness Principle requires that the system respond to taxpayer action within a timeframe and with a predictability that makes honest engagement feel like engagement with something that is paying attention. This is the principle least addressed by the WDT's existing mechanism design, and the one that most directly requires Membrane-level specification.

The mechanism sets no response-time standards, because response times are an operational question rather than a structural one. The Membrane must supply them. Any taxpayer action generating a system obligation should produce a confirmed acknowledgement within a defined period, followed by a substantive response within a further defined period. The loss refund is the most important case: a taxpayer who has declared a loss and is waiting for a refund, without any notification of processing status, experiences exactly the kind of institutional unresponsiveness the Responsiveness Principle is designed to prevent. See (BEHAV §9).

## 7.5 The Accessibility Principle

The Accessibility Principle requires that the direct cost of honest compliance not exceed the cost of the avoidance strategies it competes with. This is the principle where the WDT faces its most structurally embedded obstacle, and where naming that obstacle plainly is a precondition for designing around it.

The professional compliance industry — tax advisers, wealth managers, and the specialist legal practices that serve the population the WDT will primarily affect — currently profits from complexity. A system simple enough for honest self-assessment in routine cases removes a significant revenue source from that industry. The industry's commercial incentive is therefore structurally opposed to membrane health on the Accessibility dimension. This is not a statement about the motives of individual advisers. It is a statement about the structural position of a professional class whose services are most valuable when the system is most complex. Naming this clearly is not a reason to abandon the Accessibility Principle. It is a reason to design the administrative architecture with that opposition in mind rather than assuming it away.

The WDT's four-route valuation architecture reduces compliance costs in routine cases by eliminating the open valuation question. The self-balancing mechanism reduces the cost of dispute by providing a defined process with known parameters. Together these reduce the gap between honest self-assessment and professionally assisted avoidance in the cases that cover most of the taxpayer population within scope.

One property of the WDT's mechanism directly reduces the Administrator's information burden — and by extension the compliance burden placed on taxpayers. The Administrator's verification task is not truth-discovery. It is arithmetic confirmation: checking that the delta between two declared values is correctly calculated according to the rules, not that any single declared value was accurate. The delta self-correction means that an incorrect basis today generates an incorrect delta next year, and the compounding penalty falls on the taxpayer rather than on the revenue. The Valuation Bodies catch egregious Route D outliers. Truth-discovery is outsourced to market processes and competitive valuation.

This changes what the Accessibility Principle requires of the administrative layer. In a conventional wealth tax, an authority holding more data improves compliance because the authority is engaged in discovering whether declared values are correct. In the WDT, the Administrator holding extra data does not improve compliance, because the mechanism's own structure catches most errors regardless. The minimum-necessary-information principle therefore follows from the mechanism's design, not only from privacy values. The Administrator-as-consistency-confirmer is not a concession to taxpayer preferences. It is what the mechanism requires.

# 8. The Nine Behavioural Shapes

The friction taxonomy in (BEHAV §4) and the principles in (BEHAV §5) establish what a behaviourally robust mechanism must achieve. This section maps the distribution of responses that mechanism will actually encounter. The nine shapes below are not predictions about which responses will be most common. They are a complete account of the range, from full cooperation to active resistance, against which the mechanism and The Membrane must both be evaluated. A system that is robust against all nine is robust in the relevant sense. A system that functions only against the first two or three has absorbed a behavioural assumption it has not acknowledged.

## 8.1 Shape 1 — Full Cooperative Compliance

The taxpayer understands their liability, values the reciprocal structure of the system, files accurate declarations, and pays without seeking to minimise. This is the shape most tax systems are implicitly designed around, and the one that requires the least from the mechanism.

The WDT mechanism handles this shape without difficulty. The delta base is straightforward to apply where asset values are clear and the taxpayer is not seeking ambiguity. The loss refund is claimed and received. The system functions as designed.

The Membrane requirement for Shape 1 is low but not zero. A cooperatively compliant taxpayer who receives poor communication, slow acknowledgement, or inconsistent treatment will not necessarily remain cooperative. The membrane must sustain the cooperative relationship rather than erode it through administrative friction that the taxpayer experiences as indifference.

## 8.2 Shape 2 — Passive Non-Optimisation

The taxpayer is compliant but disengaged. They pay what is asked, do not seek professional advice, do not optimise their position, and do not exercise entitlements they are not prompted to claim. This shape is common among taxpayers within scope at the lower end of the threshold and among those who regard the compliance cost as not worth bearing.

The WDT mechanism handles this shape adequately on the liability side. The problem is on the entitlement side: a passively non-optimising taxpayer will not claim a loss refund they have not been told about. The mechanism is symmetric; the taxpayer's engagement is not.

The Membrane requirement for Shape 2 is significant. The principle of active rather than passive visibility (BEHAV §5.2) is specifically designed for this shape. The Membrane must proactively surface entitlements: notifying the taxpayer of their refund entitlement before it is needed, flagging when a declared value appears inconsistent with comparable transactions, and prompting a challenge where one appears warranted.

## 8.3 Shape 3 — Active Optimisation Within the Rules

The taxpayer engages professional advice to minimise their liability within the rules as written. They choose the most favourable valuation route available to them, time realisations to reduce their delta, and structure their affairs to keep within the rules while paying as little as the rules permit. This is rational behaviour and not a failure of the system. Rule-compliant optimisation is the expected response of a rational actor to a well-designed tax.

The WDT mechanism handles this shape through the self-balancing valuation mechanism (VAL §6). Where a taxpayer selects a valuation route that understates their asset value, the purchase option creates a credible cost to that selection: an undervalued asset can be acquired by the state at the declared value. That constraint does not eliminate optimisation; it bounds it. The taxpayer can optimise within a range defined by what they are willing to sell at.

The Membrane requirement for Shape 3 is primarily one of Clarity. The taxpayer optimising within the rules must be able to identify, without excessive professional assistance, which optimisations are within the rules and which are not.

## 8.4 Shape 4 — Avoidance Through Restructuring

The taxpayer restructures their asset holdings to reduce their exposure to the tax base. This may involve transferring assets into structures that sit outside the delta base, disaggregating holdings across family members to stay below thresholds, or converting taxable asset forms into less taxable ones. The restructuring is legal; the intent is to exit the base rather than pay within it.

The WDT mechanism addresses this shape through the attribution test in (CORP), the lifetime contribution envelope (WP §3.5), and the threshold design that limits the disaggregation benefit. No mechanism eliminates restructuring entirely. The WDT's design narrows the restructuring options relative to a conventional wealth tax.

The Membrane requirement for Shape 4 is primarily one of Fairness and Clarity. Where restructuring is legal, the system must not treat it as evasion. Where it crosses into avoidance the rules are designed to prevent, the system must communicate that clearly enough that the taxpayer and their adviser understand the boundary before, not after, the structure is implemented.

## 8.5 Shape 5 — Deferral and Timing Manipulation

The taxpayer manages the timing of realisations and declarations to shift liability between periods, smooth their delta across years, or defer crystallisation of gains into periods where rates may be lower. The WDT mechanism is more resistant to timing manipulation than a conventional realisation-based tax, because the delta base accrues annually rather than being triggered by realisation events. A taxpayer cannot defer the accrual of wealth by deferring a sale. They can, however, manage the composition of their portfolio to reduce the delta in any given year.

The Membrane requirement for Shape 5 is modest. The primary protection is in the mechanism itself. The Membrane must ensure that multi-year patterns in declarations are visible to the administering authority in a way that allows anomalous timing patterns to be identified without the taxpayer being presumed dishonest.

## 8.6 Shape 6 — Migration of Assets Without Personal Exit

The taxpayer retains personal residence but transfers assets to offshore structures, foreign trusts, or jurisdictions outside the reach of the tax. This does not involve the taxpayer leaving; it involves their assets leaving on their behalf. It is the most technically complex avoidance shape and the one most dependent on the quality of international information-sharing architecture.

The WDT mechanism addresses this shape primarily through the individual-as-subject principle (MF §3), which anchors liability to the individual regardless of the jurisdiction of the asset, and through the reporting requirements that flow from that principle.

The Membrane requirement for Shape 6 is significant and partially unresolved. The Membrane must supply guidance in a form accessible without specialist international tax advice for the most common structures, while acknowledging that the most complex offshore arrangements will remain outside practical self-assessment reach. See (BEHAV §9).

## 8.7 Shape 7 — Personal Exit

The taxpayer emigrates. They leave the jurisdiction and establish residence elsewhere, reducing or eliminating their liability under a jurisdiction-based wealth tax. This is the shape the emigration objection is almost entirely focused on, and it is addressed directly here and at length in (BEHAV §9.1) and (BEHAV §9.2).

The WDT mechanism addresses jurisdictional exit through position closure: the assessment position ends, the final delta is calculated, and any symmetric refund entitlement on a negative final delta is honoured and settled. The mechanism does not impose punitive exit taxation; departure is treated as a legitimate termination event. The full exit closure framework, including the exit valuation service, the bridging facility that decouples physical departure from settlement completion, and the no-punitive-exit-taxation position, is developed in (CLOSE §3–§5).

Beyond exit closure mechanics, the mechanism's response to jurisdictional exit is structural rather than punitive. A tax system that loses taxpayers through departure at a rate that materially affects its revenue base has a problem that enforcement cannot solve. The correct response is to reduce the friction that makes departure an attractive option relative to staying and paying. That returns directly to the five principles. (CLOSE §6) establishes a re-entry rule under which a departing taxpayer's lifetime contribution envelope travels with them: a taxpayer who has built up a positive envelope balance through prior contributions finds that re-entry restores their accumulated refund entitlement.

The Membrane requirement for Shape 7 is to ensure that the departure process itself is clear and complete. A taxpayer who is leaving should be able to understand their final liability, exercise any outstanding refund entitlements, and close their account without that process requiring professional assistance in routine cases.

## 8.8 Shape 8 — Partial Exit

The taxpayer maintains a formal presence in the jurisdiction while substantially offshoring their assets and economic life. This shape is more common than full emigration among the population the WDT primarily affects, and it is harder to address because the taxpayer's formal status remains within the system while their substantive engagement has largely left it.

The WDT mechanism addresses this shape through the individual-as-subject principle combined with the residency determination rules developed in (JUR). The boundary between continued residence and maintained nominal presence is a factual determination that requires administrative capacity to investigate, not a mechanism design question.

The Membrane requirement for Shape 8 is primarily one of Fairness. The system must be able to distinguish continued residence from nominal presence without treating every taxpayer with offshore assets as a nominal resident. See (BEHAV §9).

## 8.9 Shape 9 — Active Resistance and Non-Compliance

The taxpayer does not comply. They do not file, file inaccurately, or file in ways designed to misrepresent their position. Where this is coordinated — organised non-compliance by a population of taxpayers acting collectively, or by professional advisers operating in concert — it presents a qualitatively different challenge from individual non-compliance.

The WDT mechanism addresses individual non-compliance through the standard enforcement architecture available to any tax authority: third-party reporting requirements, information powers, and penalty structures. The mechanism's self-balancing valuation feature reduces one dimension of non-compliance risk: the incentive to declare undervalued assets is bounded by the purchase option. It does not address non-filing or deliberate misrepresentation.

The Membrane requirement for Shape 9 is limited but important. Where non-compliance is individual and results from confusion or disengagement rather than intent, the membrane interventions described for Shapes 1 through 6 reduce the population in this shape by making honest compliance easier. Where non-compliance is intentional, The Membrane cannot substitute for enforcement. What it can do is ensure that the experience of honest taxpayers is not degraded by the enforcement responses directed at non-compliant ones.

## 8.10 The Distribution as a Whole

The nine shapes are not equally populated and are not equally important for revenue. Shapes 1 and 2 will account for most taxpayers within scope. Shapes 3 through 6 will account for most of the value at risk from avoidance. Shapes 7 and 8 will account for most of the political visibility of behavioural response. Shape 9 will account for the most direct enforcement cost.

A behaviourally robust mechanism does not need to eliminate any of these shapes. It needs to remain functional across all of them simultaneously: collecting revenue from Shapes 1 and 2, bounding the loss from Shapes 3 through 6, ensuring that Shapes 7 and 8 do not undermine revenue at a scale that makes the system unviable, and maintaining the legitimacy that makes Shape 9 a minority position rather than a growing one.

## 8.11 The Enforcement Paradigm Shift

The analysis of Shapes 7 and 9 shares a premise with most criticism of wealth taxation: that enforcement is fundamentally a detection contest, in which the state tries to find hidden assets and sophisticated actors with large resources routinely win. That premise does not hold for the WDT's specific architecture.

The WDT's enforcement paradigm is passive collection rather than active detection. Consider each hiding strategy in turn. Assets hidden legally through unattributed corporate structures: the attribution test means unattributed ownership faces $\tau_h$ by default. The state does not need to pierce the structure — it taxes it at the highest rate and places the burden of attribution on whoever wants to claim a lower one. Establishing standing to challenge $\tau_h$ requires demonstrating beneficial ownership, which is itself an attribution event reclassifying the position to the correct tranche (CORP.A §B.2). Assets hidden illegally: every year an appreciating asset remains hidden off the books generates a compounding concealment cost, because surfacing it in the formal economy at any future point crystallises a larger liability than honest declaration would have. Realisation to cash: any realisation through the formal financial system creates a recorded delta; the lifetime contribution envelope means prior hiding does not reset the clock. Hard cash off the books is criminal activity, punishable under existing law.

The genuine residual is the Route D entry basis. An asset that enters Route D at a dramatically understated basis is formally in the system — visible, identified, accumulating delta history — but with every subsequent delta calculation wrong relative to true value. The Route D auction mechanism is the deterrent response. The WDT substantially closes the hiding problem while creating a narrower basis-manipulation problem on Route D, one that is more visible, more tractable, and more self-correcting over time than conventional hiding.

The severity of this residual depends on a prior factual question: what proportion of WDT-taxable wealth actually lands in Route D? A mechanism whose most vulnerable point covers a small fraction of the total tax base has a contained residual. One whose most vulnerable point covers a large fraction has a structural problem. That question is addressed directly in (BEHAV §8.12).

## 8.12 The Route Distribution of WDT-Taxable Wealth

The enforcement residual identified in (BEHAV §8.11) scales with the share of WDT-taxable wealth that classifies as Route D. This section maps the classification logic, identifies the primary asset classes and their natural routes, names the genuine edge cases, and argues directionally — without Phase One data — that Route D is likely a minority of WDT-taxable wealth by volume.

**Classification logic.** The four-route architecture sorts on two independent dimensions. The first is fungibility: whether the asset has a market price discoverable through third-party transaction data without requiring individual appraisal. The second is who performs the valuation: professional valuator or taxpayer self-declaration. The classification question for any given asset is sequential. First: does a reliable third-party market price exist for this asset or close comparables? If yes, the asset is fungible — Routes A or C. If no, it is non-fungible — Routes B or D. Second: is the taxpayer willing to accept professional valuation, with the cost and accountability that entails? If yes, Routes A or B. If no, self-declaration — Routes C or D.

In practice, the second question resolves differently for fungible and non-fungible assets. For fungible assets, professional valuation is cheap and the must-transfer mechanism in Route C creates strong self-correction incentives anyway; the taxpayer's choice between A and C matters less than it might appear. For non-fungible assets, the choice between B and D is consequential: Route B requires periodic professional appraisal with accountability consequences for the valuator, while Route D defers all settlement to realisation.

**Route A — Professional valuation, fungible.** Listed equities held directly. Exchange-traded funds. Government and corporate bonds with active secondary markets. Money market instruments. Listed real estate investment trusts. Foreign-listed securities with transparent pricing. Cash and bank deposits. Pension assets in defined-contribution schemes where the fund NAV is published daily.

These assets have one defining property: their price on any given assessment date is independently verifiable by a third party without appraisal. The "professional valuation" for Route A assets is therefore largely mechanical — it confirms the market price rather than generating one. Valuation risk for the professional valuator is minimal; the assets price themselves.

By wealth share: UK Wealth and Assets Survey data, despite its acknowledged undersampling above £3m, consistently shows financial assets — primarily listed equities and pension wealth — accounting for the dominant share of wealth at the top of the distribution. At the population the WDT primarily addresses (above £10m), pension and financial asset concentration is particularly high. A rough directional estimate, acknowledging the data limitations (JUR §2.4) and (RATES §5.1) identifies, places Route A assets at 40–55% of total WDT-taxable wealth. This is the most uncertain magnitude estimate in this section; Phase One data will correct it.

**Route C — Self-declaration, fungible.** Listed equities held through nominee accounts or platforms where the taxpayer prefers not to engage a professional valuator. Shares in listed companies held by founding shareholders below the threshold for Route D reclassification. Liquid alternative investments with published NAVs. Cryptocurrency holdings traded on regulated exchanges.

The must-transfer settlement mechanism in Route C creates the key discipline: a taxpayer who self-declares a listed equity at below market price is offering to transfer shares at that price to the state — a costly strategy for any asset with an observable market value. In practice, most assets that could sit in Route C will also qualify for Route A, and the taxpayer's incentive to choose Route C over Route A for fungible assets is weak. Route C's primary population is likely assets where the professional valuator's added value is low and administrative overhead is the dominant cost. Estimated share: 5–10% of WDT-taxable wealth.

**Route B — Professional valuation, non-fungible.** Directly held residential and commercial property. Agricultural land. Forestry. Infrastructure assets held directly. Unlisted bonds with thin secondary markets. Structured products without transparent pricing. Significant minority stakes in private companies where a professional appraisal is both practicable and contested.

Property is the most important Route B asset class by volume. ONS data on property wealth distribution, combined with the WAS figures JUR relies on, suggests property accounts for approximately 20–30% of top-decile wealth, though the WAS undersampling caveat applies here too. Route B is the route where the existing professional infrastructure most closely matches what the WDT requires — the VOA operates at scale for rating and council tax purposes, and the private valuation market for high-value property is mature.

The Route B settlement mechanic — deferred cash settlement available as a lien, forced settlement at every change of ownership — means periodic cash flow pressure is reduced relative to annual assessment but the liability accumulates and crystallises at transfer. This is the correct design for property: forced annual cash settlement against illiquid property holdings would be the liquidity problem in concrete form. Estimated share: 20–30% of WDT-taxable wealth.

**Route D — Self-declaration, non-fungible.** Significant controlling or majority stakes in private companies. Family business equity where no reliable external comparables exist. Intellectual property held directly. Bespoke financial instruments without secondary markets. Art, jewellery, and other collectibles above the threshold. Interests in private equity and venture capital funds where the underlying portfolio is illiquid. Carried interest and performance fee structures yet to crystallise. Significant interests in partnerships and LLPs without observable market prices.

Route D is the route for assets where periodic cash settlement is not workable and in-kind settlement is not possible. The entry basis is the vulnerable point. Among UK individuals above £10m, private company equity is the largest Route D asset class. However, several structural features constrain Route D's share of total WDT-taxable wealth.

Three structural features constrain Route D's share. First, the UK corporate landscape is dominated by listed companies and subsidiaries of listed groups; genuinely independent private companies at high valuations are fewer than they might appear. Second, the corporate delta levy covers listed companies separately: a founder who has taken a company public holds Route A equity, not Route D. Third, Route B is available for private company equity where the taxpayer accepts professional valuation, and the compounding cost of understatement established in VAL pushes Route D-eligible assets toward Route B where a credible appraisal is possible. Route D is most populated by assets where professional valuation is not feasible — no comparable transactions, no defensible methodology.

Estimated share: 10–20% of WDT-taxable wealth, concentrated in private company equity at the very top of the distribution and in illiquid alternatives and collectibles further down.

**Edge cases and classification ambiguities.** Several asset classes resist clean classification.

*Cryptocurrency held on unregulated or decentralised exchanges.* Fungibility is technically answerable (a price exists) but verifiability is not — prices on thin or unregulated venues may be manipulable. The classification default should be Route B where the position is large enough to warrant it and Route A where a regulated exchange price is available and the position is not large enough to move the market.

*Significant shareholdings in thinly traded listed companies.* (CORP §4.1) addresses this through the thin-trading threshold: below a defined liquidity floor, a nominally listed company is reclassified to Route B rather than handled through the corporate delta mechanism. The same reclassification logic applies at the individual level for direct shareholdings where market depth is insufficient to give the assessment-date price informational content. The threshold is a Governing Council parameter.

*Interests in private equity and venture capital funds.* The classification depends on whether the fund NAV is independently audited and published. Where it is (institutional PE funds with annual audited accounts), the interest is closer to Route B. Where it is not (early-stage venture funds with carrying values determined by the GP), the interest is closer to Route D.

*Carried interest not yet crystallised.* A contingent claim on future performance with no current market price and no reliable comparable. This is the clearest Route D case. The entry basis is necessarily speculative. VAL does not address this case explicitly; it is assigned to Phase One classification guidance.

*Agricultural land with development potential.* The land itself has a professional valuation market (Route B). The development option embedded in the land does not. The classification is Route B, but the professional valuation methodology for development potential is a Valuation Code Board question, not a route classification question.

*Residential property held through a company structure.* Where the company is unlisted, the company is a Route D asset, but its primary asset is property that would ordinarily be Route B. The WDT's look-through principle argues for treating the beneficial interest as Route B by analogy, with the company structure irrelevant to classification. This requires explicit statement in classification guidance.

**The magnitude argument and its limits.** Assembling the directional estimates:

| Route | Asset classes | Estimated share of WDT-taxable wealth |
|---|---|---|
| A | Listed equities, bonds, pensions, cash | 40–55% |
| B | Property, agricultural land, illiquid alternatives with professional valuation | 20–30% |
| C | Self-declared fungibles | 5–10% |
| D | Private company equity, illiquid non-fungibles, collectibles | 10–20% |

The key point is the direction: Route D at 10–20% — with the upper bound applying only at the very top of the distribution where private company equity concentration is highest — means that the enforcement residual identified in (BEHAV §8.11) applies to a minority of WDT-taxable wealth, not the majority.

Three caveats. First, Route D concentration is heavily skewed toward the very top of the WDT population. The individuals for whom Route D assets constitute the majority of their WDT-taxable wealth — founder-shareholders of significant private companies — are also the individuals whose WDT liability is largest. A route that covers 10–20% of aggregate WDT-taxable wealth might cover 40–50% of the liability attributable to the top 500 taxpayers. Volume share and revenue share are not the same number.

Second, the estimates rely on wealth survey data that systematically undersample the top of the distribution. (JUR §2.4) flags that the WAS lost Official Statistics accreditation in June 2025 and undersamples above £3m. The assets most likely to be undersampled are also the assets most likely to be Route D. The true Route D share could be higher than these estimates suggest.

Third, the boundary between Route B and Route D is partly a taxpayer election. A taxpayer who prefers not to engage professional valuation can elect Route D for an asset that would qualify for Route B. The equilibrium distribution across routes will depend on Phase One adoption behaviour — whether the compounding cost of understatement and the audit deterrent are sufficient to push Route D-eligible assets toward Route B in practice.

The conclusion that follows is calibrated rather than strong: the enforcement paradigm shift claim in (BEHAV §8.11) is defensible as a statement about the mechanism's architecture, but its practical significance scales with Route D's actual share of WDT-taxable wealth, which is a Phase One observable.

## 8.13 Why the Bias Toward Routes A and B Is Structurally Positive

The route distribution analysis in (BEHAV §8.12) establishes that WDT-taxable wealth is biased toward Routes A and B, with Route D a minority even at the top of the distribution. Three reasons explain why this matters beyond the obvious "less Route D exposure."

**The self-correction mechanisms are strongest in A and B.** Route A assets price themselves — the assessment-date market price is independently verifiable without action by either party. Route B brings a professional valuator with accountability consequences for overturned valuations. Both routes remove the entry basis problem entirely: the basis is set by an external reference, not by the taxpayer's declaration. The enforcement residual that exists in Route D simply does not exist in A or B.

**The delta self-correction works most powerfully in A and B.** An understated Route A asset corrects automatically at the next assessment date. An understated Route B asset corrects at the next professional valuation or at realisation. A population biased toward A and B is one where the mechanism's self-correction is most active.

**Revenue predictability improves with A and B concentration.** Route A and B assets produce deltas tracking observable market conditions. Route D revenue is less predictable because it depends on realisation timing, inheritance events, and auction triggers. A WDT whose tax base is predominantly A and B is more forecastable, which matters for SRR capitalisation and Governing Council planning.

The Route D entry basis residual is bounded in three ways. The conditions for maintaining a genuine lifetime basis gap are mutually constraining: an asset generating meaningful WDT liability at realisation has typically produced a credible valuation signal at some point, through financing, equity programmes, or regulatory contact. The gap is first-generation and self-diminishing, since subsequent holders inherit an auction-set basis. The extreme residual sits at the outer edge of what the mechanism is designed to reach. (MF §9.4.8) establishes that on the project's own foundational terms, this ordering is correct: the mechanism targets capital exercising institutional and political power, and a founder who never converts their position into that kind of power is not the primary subject of the concern that motivates the design. The residual is characterised, principled, and accepted — not a structural failure.

For the large majority of WDT taxpayers by count, and a significant majority by wealth volume, the enforcement residual is bounded and self-correction is active. For the small population at the very top where Route D exposure is highest, the residual is a named boundary rather than an open problem. This is where the audit deterrent, the Route B election incentive, and the VAL compounding cost argument carry the most weight, and where Phase One route adoption data is most consequential.

# 9. Principal Objections

## 9.1 The Wealthy Will Leave

The emigration objection holds that a wealth tax of sufficient magnitude will trigger departure at a scale that renders the system unviable: the population most able to pay is also the population most able to exit, and that structural asymmetry means no wealth tax can generate sustained revenue from those it most intends to reach.

The objection is correct that wealthy people have exit options ordinary people do not, and that some will use them. The question is whether that observation constitutes an argument against the WDT specifically.

The emigration objection is not an argument against the WDT; it is an argument against any wealth tax that is not robust against exit. The WDT's exit closure framework (CLOSE §3–§5) settles the accrued running account at departure without treating exit as an act requiring a punitive fiscal response. The revenue lost on departure is the revenue that would have accrued in future years, the same revenue lost by any tax when a taxpayer dies, moves sector, or otherwise exits the base.

The objection also implicitly assumes that exit is the dominant response. Jurisdictional exit is Shape 7 in a nine-shape distribution. The population that departs is a subset of a population that predominantly stays, optimises within the rules, restructures, or defers. Designing the system around the assumption that Shape 7 is the modal response is a category error.

Membrane quality matters here too. A taxpayer who experiences the system as fair, reciprocal, and accessible has a weaker incentive to leave than one who experiences it as arbitrary and burdensome. The objection treats membrane quality as a fixed property of wealth taxation; this paper has established that it is a design variable.

The WDT's re-entry rule (CLOSE §6) provides a structural reason to return that no adversarial system can offer. A taxpayer's lifetime contribution envelope travels with them on exit and is restored on re-entry: a long-term participant who has built up a positive envelope balance recovers their accumulated refund entitlement when they return. The mechanism incentivises re-entry for exactly the population whose continued participation generates the most revenue.

The claim is not that departure will not occur. It is that a system designed for behavioural robustness with a healthy membrane loses less revenue to exit than a poorly designed one, and loses it in ways that do not compound into institutional failure.

## 9.2 The Cross-Base Fiscal Externality

@AgrawalEtAl2025 find that when wealth-tax-driven migration occurs, the associated income tax and VAT losses can be approximately six times the direct wealth tax revenue loss. This is the most serious empirical challenge the paper faces and is stated without softening. A wealth tax that triggers sufficient departure to generate cross-base losses at that ratio could be net-fiscally negative even while continuing to raise direct revenue. The finding deserves a response that engages with it seriously, and the WDT has more to say in response than any prior wealth tax design has been able to offer. Six responses follow, ordered from most conservative to most ambitious.

**The jurisdictional transfer caveat.** The six-to-one ratio is derived from Spanish regional data — specifically the comparison between regions that applied the wealth tax and those that did not following the 2011 reintroduction. It is a point estimate from a specific context, not a structural constant. Its magnitude in any given jurisdiction will depend on how concentrated the departing population's income and consumption is relative to the wealth tax base, how robust the exit infrastructure is, and how much of the apparent emigration response reflects genuine relocation versus administrative restructuring of residence.

In the UK reference jurisdiction, the WDT threshold is high and the taxpayer population small. The income and consumption concentration of that population is likely higher than in the Spanish regional study, which would push the ratio up. The UK's stronger administrative capacity and HMRC's third-party reporting infrastructure would constrain pure administrative restructuring, which would push the departure rate down. The honest position is that the Agrawal ratio is a directional warning of real force, not a transferable number. The cross-base risk is genuine; its magnitude in a UK WDT implementation is unknown and cannot be established from the existing literature.

**The membrane investment multiplier.** If the six-to-one ratio is directionally correct, it transforms the economics of membrane investment. Each departure event prevented by better membrane quality — by higher Reciprocity, clearer Clarity, more responsive administration — saves not only the direct WDT revenue attributable to that taxpayer but approximately six times that amount in preserved income tax and VAT base. The return on membrane investment is therefore an order of magnitude larger than the direct revenue it protects.

This reframing makes membrane quality not merely institutionally desirable but fiscally urgent in a way that has a quantifiable basis. An administration that treats the membrane as a secondary implementation concern — something to develop after the mechanism is operational — is implicitly accepting avoidable cross-base losses at a six-to-one ratio. The correct sequencing is membrane-first precisely because the cost of Phase One membrane failure is not limited to the WDT revenue it forfeits. The Agrawal finding is therefore not only a warning about emigration. It is the strongest available argument for front-loading membrane investment, and it produces that argument from fiscal arithmetic rather than institutional preference.

**The rate calibration lever.** The Governing Council can reduce rates in response to observed departure. This statement sounds simple; its implications are not.

A conventional wealth tax facing the Agrawal externality has no equivalent response. Its revenue is directly proportional to its rate. A rate reduction sacrifices direct revenue without any mechanism for recovering cross-base losses already incurred. The tax either holds its rate and accepts the departure, or reduces its rate and accepts the revenue loss. There is no third option.

The WDT's position is different. If Phase One data shows departure rates generating cross-base losses at or near the Agrawal magnitude, the Governing Council can reduce rates to reduce the friction that makes departure attractive. A rate reduction that prevents enough departures to preserve more cross-base revenue than it sacrifices in direct WDT revenue is a net-positive fiscal move. The Governing Council has both the authority to make this adjustment (GOV §5.3) and — through Phase One measurement of departure rates and cross-base loss per departure event — the empirical basis to calibrate it (SWEEPS §3). This is active fiscal management of the Agrawal risk rather than passive exposure to it. No prior wealth tax design has had this capacity because no prior wealth tax design has had a Governing Council with explicit rate calibration authority tied to observable Phase One signals.

**The timeline extension buffer.** The SRR and LRR fill timelines are Governing Council parameters, not fixed commitments. A Governing Council facing higher-than-expected Phase One departure rates can extend the capitalisation window — accepting a longer path to Phase Two — in exchange for operating at lower rates that reduce the departure incentive. This trades time for retention.

No other tax system has this lever. The WDT's structured capitalisation phase, with explicit milestone gates rather than a fixed calendar, means adverse Phase One conditions are absorbable without systemic failure. A conventional wealth tax that faces high emigration in its early years has no buffer: the revenue shortfall either forces rate increases that worsen departure, or forces abandonment. The WDT can lengthen its timeline to give the membrane time to establish credibility and the mechanism time to demonstrate its properties.

This option has a cost that must be stated plainly. Extending the LRR fill window delays the labour tax relief dividend. POL identifies the vulnerability window as the period of maximum political exposure; extending it makes the bootstrapping problem harder. The timeline extension buffer is a real fiscal option with a real political price.

**The re-entry rule and the externality's temporal shape.** The lifetime contribution envelope travels with a departing taxpayer. On re-entry, their accumulated balance is restored: prior taxes paid, prior refunds received, prior envelope history. A long-term WDT participant who has built up a positive envelope balance — who has experienced the symmetric refund in practice, who has governance standing through the Taxpayer Chamber, who has a published longitudinal participation record — carries a financial reason to return that no adversarial exit tax regime can offer or replicate.

This creates a temporal structure to the Agrawal externality that prior wealth taxes have not exhibited. Under a conventional wealth tax, departure is permanent: the departing taxpayer has no mechanism-based incentive to return. Under the WDT, the incentive to return strengthens as the envelope deepens. A taxpayer who departed in year two of Phase One, before the envelope had meaningful value, faces a different re-entry calculation in year ten than in year two.

The Agrawal externality for the WDT is therefore front-loaded in Phase One — when the envelope is thin, refund experience is limited, and the mechanism's properties are unproven — and structurally diminishing as the system matures. This does not resolve the Phase One exposure. It changes the honest characterisation of what the exposure is: a transitional risk concentrated in the period before the mechanism has demonstrated itself, not a permanent structural feature of wealth taxation.

**The immigration possibility.** The five responses above accept Agrawal's foundational assumption: that a wealth tax produces net emigration. That assumption may not hold for the WDT. This is framed here as a formal possibility, not a prediction — its realisation depends on conditions that only Phase One can confirm.

The Agrawal finding is derived entirely from conventional wealth taxes. Every wealth tax in the empirical record shares a common architecture: asymmetric extraction, no loss participation, adversarial enforcement posture, no governance participation by the taxed population. The emigration response Agrawal documents is a rational response to that specific design. Taxpayers facing an asymmetric, adversarial system with no reciprocal commitment depart when the cost of departure is lower than the cost of remaining. That is a statement about the rational response to a specific institutional structure, not about wealthy people in general.

The WDT is not that structure. Three features distinguish it from every wealth tax in the empirical record in ways that directly affect the rational location calculus of a mobile wealth-holder.

The symmetric refund changes the risk profile of holding wealth in a WDT jurisdiction. A taxpayer with significant exposure to asset volatility — a founder with concentrated private company equity, a large listed-equity holder through a market cycle — faces substantial downside risk. Under every existing system, that downside is entirely theirs. The WDT is the only system in which the state participates in losses at the same marginal rate at which it participates in gains — for a rational wealth-holder evaluating jurisdictions, this transforms the expected value of holding wealth there.

The delta base means the tax is always proportional to the gain. A taxpayer whose wealth grows at 10% annually and faces a 40% marginal rate on the delta pays 4% of their wealth that year; their wealth still grows at 6% after tax. In a loss year they receive a proportional refund. For a rational wealth-holder comparing jurisdictions across a multi-decade horizon, this is a materially better risk profile than any stock wealth tax, any conventional capital gains regime, or any combination of the two.

Governance participation has no precedent in the history of wealth taxation. The Taxpayer Chamber gives WDT taxpayers formal institutional standing in the body that governs their own taxation, with enumerated structural protections that require supermajority action to modify and generate observable rebalancing costs when challenged. A wealth-holder who has relocated to a low-tax jurisdiction has no institutional voice in the system they fled and no institutional voice in the system they relocated to. A WDT taxpayer has formal governance rights, published decision records, and structural protection against the incremental erosion that ended prior wealth taxes.

If these three features change the rational calculus sufficiently, it is formally possible that a WDT jurisdiction could attract net inward migration of wealthy individuals — not despite the tax, but because of what the tax offers that no alternative jurisdiction provides. A rational actor evaluating where to hold significant wealth across a multi-decade horizon, under genuine asset volatility, with concern for institutional stability, may find that a WDT jurisdiction dominates the available alternatives on purely self-interested grounds.

If net inward migration occurred rather than net emigration, the Agrawal argument would invert entirely. The six-to-one cross-base ratio cuts in both directions: inward migration of high-income, high-consumption individuals would generate income tax and VAT gains alongside direct WDT revenue, potentially dwarfing the direct WDT revenue from those individuals.

Three constraints on this possibility are stated plainly. It is conditional on credible delivery: the rational case for WDT-jurisdiction residence depends entirely on the symmetric refund, the governance protections, and the rate structure being demonstrably maintained. Phase One is the period during which that credibility is established or not, and the immigration argument is only as strong as the Phase One demonstration. It applies most directly to mobile internationally wealthy individuals making active location decisions, not to the domestic taxpayer population. And it is a Phase One empirical question: whether the rational case translates into actual inward registration is measurable, and Phase One data on inward migration by individuals previously resident in other jurisdictions is the first observable test. The paper does not predict that net immigration will occur.

## 9.3 The Complexity Objection

The objection holds that any system sophisticated enough to be robust across the nine behavioural shapes is too complex to be accessible, and therefore fails the Clarity and Accessibility Principles it claims to satisfy.

The complexity required for behavioural robustness lives in the mechanism and the administrative architecture: the four-route valuation system, the self-balancing mechanism, the attribution test, the position closure framework. It does not need to live in the taxpayer's experience of the system. A taxpayer does not need to understand the full four-route valuation architecture to file an accurate declaration; they need to understand which route applies to their assets and what value that route requires them to declare. A well-designed membrane makes that task achievable without mastery of the underlying architecture. The claim is not that the WDT's mechanism must be simple. The claim is that The Membrane must be clear.

## 9.4 The Membrane Is Undeliverable

The objection holds that the administrative layer described in this paper requires a quality of public administration that no tax authority has demonstrated it can sustain at scale.

This is an honest objection and it does not have a complete answer. The objection applies to implementation, not to design. A design that requires good administration is not invalidated by the observation that good administration is difficult. This paper's claim is that if The Membrane functions as specified, the mechanism will be more behaviourally robust than the alternatives. Whether the required administration can be built is deferred to implementation planning. The relevant comparison is not absolute: the WDT's administrative requirements should be measured against those of the alternatives, including the alternative of doing nothing, which carries its own administrative and fiscal costs.

## 9.5 Robustness Is Rebranding Failure

The objection holds that describing a tax system as "robust across the distribution of behavioural responses" is a sophisticated way of saying it will not work as well as intended, and that the concept of behavioural robustness is a framework for managing expectations downward rather than a design principle.

This objection is a rhetorical position, not an analytical one. It does not engage with the friction taxonomy, the five principles, the membrane concept, or the nine behavioural shapes. Every institution operates across a distribution of responses from the people it governs. The question is whether the institution is designed with that distribution in mind or assumes it away. Assuming it away does not produce a more effective institution. It produces a less honest account of what is being built.

# 10. Membrane Calcification and the Monitoring Architecture

The governance decay analysis in GOV identifies four sub-forms of institutional drift and concludes that three are irreducible. The honest response to irreducible drift is residue — durable assets that survive a captured period and give a future legitimate effort something to rebuild from. This section applies the same intellectual honesty to the membrane concept. Membrane calcification is the one form of institutional drift the WDT's architecture addresses least directly, and the response described below is the best available answer within the existing institutional design rather than a complete solution.

## 10.1 The Nature of the Problem

Membrane calcification differs from governance decay in a way that determines what kind of fix is available. Governance decay in GOV is primarily a capture and drift problem — actors with interests in subverting the mechanism gradually reshape it, or institutions drift from their founding mandate through ordinary entropy. GOV's structural responses — enumerated clauses, rebalancing costs, seat-burns, constituency dissolution triggers — work because they attach observable costs to deliberate acts of deviation. Someone has to do something to trigger them.

Membrane calcification operates through omission rather than commission. No actor decides to calcify the membrane. The notification architecture designed for a Phase One population of sophisticated founder-shareholders gradually becomes the wrong membrane for a Phase Two population that includes a much broader wealth distribution. The communications that worked for 32,000 individuals above £10m may not work for the larger Phase Two population. The route guidance adequate when the four asset classes were well-understood becomes inadequate when novel asset structures accumulate. The response-time standards achievable in Phase One become harder to maintain as the taxpayer population grows. None of this requires bad actors. It requires only that updating the membrane demands active effort the institution consistently has other priorities for.

This distinction determines why GOV's mechanisms do not transfer directly. An enumerated clause protecting membrane health is unenforceable without an agreed measurement of it. A rebalancing cost that fires on membrane degradation requires a trigger condition observable without subjective judgment. Without observable proxies and specified thresholds, governance-style structural responses to calcification cannot be designed.

## 10.2 The Monitoring Instrument

The partial structural response begins with measurement. The five membrane health dimensions each have observable proxies established in (BEHAV §5): self-assessment completion rates for Clarity, unprompted refund claim rates for Reciprocity, dispute rates disaggregated by wealth band for Fairness, refund processing times for Responsiveness, and professional adviser engagement rates for routine cases for Accessibility. These observables are byproducts of the Administrator's existing data collection — they do not require new data gathering, only new publication requirements.

The architectural commitment is this: membrane health observables should be mandatory Administrator outputs, published on the same cycle as other mandatory publications specified in (GOV.B §B.4.4), in a consistent format that allows longitudinal comparison. Phase One data establishes the baseline against which subsequent drift is measured — without it, any later measurement identifies the current state but cannot identify drift. Phase One measurement is therefore load-bearing for the entire monitoring architecture, not merely useful for Phase One evaluation. The actor with standing to act on observed drift is the second element of the response.

## 10.3 The Taxpayer Chamber as Monitor

The Taxpayer Chamber is the natural institutional home for membrane monitoring, not by design but by structural alignment of incentive, information, and standing.

TP members are the primary interactors with the membrane. They experience membrane degradation directly and personally — slower response times, less clear route guidance, higher compliance friction, less visible reciprocal benefit. No other body in the institutional architecture has this experiential information base. The Administrator observes aggregate metrics; TP members observe individual experience. The Governing Council observes published outputs; TP members observe what the published outputs fail to capture. The information asymmetry runs in TP's favour on membrane quality in a way it runs in no other direction.

TP members also have formal standing to act on what they observe. Proposal initiation rights, access to the DR forum, and the ability to engage the Allocator through both formal and informal channels are all available to TP without requiring any architectural extension. The membrane monitoring function does not need a new institution. It needs existing TP standing to be exercised in a specific direction.

## 10.4 The Internal TP Voting Structure Hypothesis

Within TP, one-member-one-vote means the numerically larger entry-level population holds greater aggregate voting power than the ultra-wealthy minority. This formal equality interacts with a structural asymmetry in the marginal interest each population has in membrane health, producing a hypothesis about TP's collective behaviour worth stating precisely.

Compliance friction falls disproportionately on the less wealthy within TP. A taxpayer at the entry threshold with moderate asset complexity bears more of the compliance burden relative to their wealth than a taxpayer at fifty times the threshold who has already invested in administrative infrastructure to manage complexity regardless of the WDT's specific requirements. The entry-level TP member gains more from Accessibility improvement than the ultra-wealthy member does, because the ultra-wealthy member has already partially solved the compliance cost problem through institutional capacity the mechanism did not create and cannot take away.

Reciprocity visibility matters more at the lower end of TP for a parallel reason. A taxpayer at the entry threshold who experiences a significant loss year and receives a symmetric refund has had a materially significant experience of the mechanism's cooperative character. A taxpayer fifty times the threshold has more cushion; the refund is financially meaningful but less existentially significant. The lower TP members therefore have stronger direct interest in the Reciprocity dimension functioning visibly and promptly, and stronger motivation to escalate when it does not.

Within TP, the majority coalition — entry-level and middle-range members — therefore has stronger marginal interest in membrane health than the minority coalition of ultra-wealthy members. This is a structural feature of the one-member-one-vote rule interacting with the wealth distribution within TP, not a claim about the character of wealthy people. The formal voting arithmetic gives this majority coalition the power to initiate proposals and drive agenda items that the ultra-wealthy minority cannot block unilaterally.

One complication is stated plainly. Ultra-wealthy TP members have stronger incentive and greater capacity to organise within TP. One-member-one-vote is the formal rule; agenda-setting and coalition-building within TP are not equally distributed. The membrane monitoring function depends on the majority coalition exercising its formal power, which is a reasonable expectation given the structural incentive alignment but is an expectation rather than a guarantee.

## 10.5 The Allocator as Fairness Signal

The Fairness dimension presents a specific monitoring challenge. The observable proxies for Clarity, Responsiveness, and Accessibility are tractable — completion rates, processing times, and adviser engagement rates are measurable from Administrator data. The observable proxy for Fairness — the taxpayer's experience of consistent and procedurally just administration — resists clean quantification. Survey data is the obvious instrument but is expensive, gameable, and subject to systematic non-response bias among the population the WDT primarily addresses.

The Allocator provides an imperfect but structurally grounded alternative signal for this dimension specifically. The Allocator's mandate is explicitly broad-advisory, covering the cultural and political context bearing on what form of relief serves the terminal goal at any given moment. Sustained TP lobbying on membrane quality — grievances raised consistently through the DR forum, proposals initiated around membrane funding, informal pressure communicated through the relational channels the Allocator's design explicitly accommodates — is exactly the kind of signal the Allocator is positioned to synthesise and surface. The Allocator does not need a formal Fairness metric to observe that membrane complaints are crowding out other TP agenda items. The lobbying intensity is itself the signal.

This instrument is stronger for Fairness specifically than survey data would be, for a reason that follows from the nature of Fairness grievances. Perceived procedural unfairness generates sustained political pressure rather than quiet disengagement. A taxpayer who experiences the system as arbitrarily applied does not simply reduce their declaration accuracy — they complain, they organise, they persist. TP's proposal initiation rights and the DR forum give that pressure a formal channel. Sustained, broad-based TP pressure on membrane quality that saturates the Allocator's published recommendations is a more reliable signal of Fairness degradation than any survey instrument, because it is revealed preference rather than stated preference and because it is costly — it displaces other TP agenda items to maintain.

The one limit is that the Allocator's recommendations are published and DR-evaluated but not self-executing. FS can resist funding reallocation toward membrane improvement on fiscal grounds.

## 10.6 The TP/DR Coalition and the FS Constraint

A TP/DR coalition on membrane funding clears both dual-threshold conditions without FS. TP and DR together hold 75% of total vote share. The nay condition requires nays to stay below DR's 50% share; the yay condition requires a simple majority of votes cast. A TP/DR coalition whose DR component is motivated satisfies both conditions regardless of FS opposition.

DR members are lottery-selected from the general population and have no personal experience of the WDT membrane. Their connection to membrane health is indirect but real and operates through two channels.

The first is dividend protection. A membrane that generates departures — through Fairness degradation, through compliance friction that drives restructuring, through Reciprocity failures that erode cooperative engagement — reduces the WDT tax base and ultimately reduces the dividend DR members exist to receive. The Allocator's role includes making this connection legible in published recommendations. DR members who understand the transmission channel from membrane degradation to dividend reduction have an indirect but real interest in supporting TP membrane proposals.

The second is the credibility of TP's signal. DR members observe TP's lobbying intensity through the publicly visible DR forum. A DR chamber watching sustained, broad-based TP complaints about membrane quality — particularly complaints originating from entry-level TP members rather than the ultra-wealthy minority — is receiving a signal that is structurally harder to discount than complaints from a small wealthy elite. The one-member-one-vote structure within TP makes the signal's origin visible: if the majority coalition is driving the complaint rather than a wealthy faction, DR members have less rational basis for treating it as special pleading.

FS resistance on fiscal grounds is real and should not be understated. Membrane investment competes with other claims on the Governing Council's attention and the Administrator's resource envelope. But FS cannot indefinitely resist a motivated TP/DR coalition without incurring rebalancing costs. The structural arithmetic provides the answer to the FS constraint in the limit, though the political path to exercising it requires TP organisation and DR engagement that are not guaranteed in any given cycle.

## 10.7 The Honest Limit

The monitoring architecture described in this section is the best available response to membrane calcification within the existing institutional design. It converts periodic review from an aspiration into a named architecture with specified instruments, specified actors, and specified channels for escalation.

It does not provide the self-executing consequence that GOV's strongest mechanisms have. A rebalancing cost fires automatically when a structural proposal concludes. A constituency dissolution trigger fires automatically when the volunteer rate falls below threshold. The membrane monitoring architecture described here requires TP to organise, the Allocator to surface the signal, and DR to prioritise membrane health over competing claims — reasonable expectations given the structural incentive alignment, but expectations rather than guarantees.

Membrane calcification therefore remains the one form of institutional drift the WDT addresses least directly. The governance decay analysis in GOV reaches the same honest conclusion about three of its four sub-forms and responds with residue — durable assets that give a future legitimate effort something to rebuild from. The membrane equivalent of residue is the mandatory publication cycle itself: longitudinal membrane health data, consistently formatted, archived on the Administrator's standard cycle, constitutes a record against which a future administration can identify when drift occurred, how far it progressed, and what a restored membrane baseline looked like — the honest acknowledgement that prevention may fail, and that the record of what was built should survive the failure.

# 11. Phase Sequencing and Behavioural Evolution

The preceding sections treat the institution largely as a static object — a mechanism and a membrane evaluated against a distribution of behavioural responses. That framing is useful for establishing design principles, but the institution will change as it operates, and so will the distribution of responses it encounters.

## 11.1 Phase One — Implementation

The implementation phase is the period of highest behavioural uncertainty and highest institutional vulnerability. The mechanism is untested. The Membrane has not yet demonstrated whether it functions at design quality. Taxpayers have not yet formed settled views about the system's fairness, responsiveness, or reciprocal character. The institution has no empirical basis for distinguishing responses that reflect a design flaw from responses that reflect the normal friction of any new system finding its footing.

The Agrawal risk is most acute here. If emigration at scale is the response to implementation, the cross-base revenue losses occur before the institution has established the legitimacy and membrane quality that would have reduced that response. Phase One emigration is the worst-case scenario precisely because it happens before the institution has had the opportunity to demonstrate what it is.

The membrane investment required in Phase One is front-loaded and cannot be deferred. A membrane not functioning at design quality from the point of first taxpayer contact will generate legitimacy friction that compounds into subsequent phases. A taxpayer who experiences the system as opaque, unresponsive, or unfair in their first year does not approach their second year neutrally. The cost of Phase One membrane failure is not confined to Phase One.

The administrative architecture described in (BEHAV §7) — the notification systems, the response-time standards, the accessible self-assessment guidance, the dispute resolution processes — must be operational before the first assessment cycle, not developed in parallel with it. Understating that requirement leads to exactly the sequencing error that makes Phase One failure more likely.

## 11.2 Phase Two — Stabilisation

The stabilisation phase begins when the first full assessment cycle has completed and the first observable behavioural data is available. The institution can now see the actual distribution of responses rather than the anticipated one. Avoidance structures that did not materialise can be deprioritised; those that emerged can be addressed. Membrane failures that generated friction in Phase One can be identified and corrected with evidence rather than inference.

If Phase One membrane investment was effective, the stabilisation phase will show a distribution weighted toward Shapes 1 through 3, with Shapes 4 through 9 present but not dominant. If membrane investment was insufficient, the distribution will be shifted toward avoidance and exit. Recovery is possible but costly: a taxpayer who restructured in Phase One in response to membrane failure has already incurred that cost and has no automatic incentive to reverse it when the membrane improves.

## 11.3 Phase Three — Maturity

The mature institution has an empirical record. It knows which membrane interventions have been effective, where the professional compliance industry has found the boundaries of the rules, and what the actual distribution of behavioural shapes looks like across the population. Its membrane has been calibrated against evidence rather than inference.

Phase Three is also when the membrane calcification risk identified in (BEHAV §10) becomes most acute. The notification architecture designed for a Phase One population of sophisticated founder-shareholders has by now been operating for years, and the taxpayer population has changed. The monitoring architecture established in (BEHAV §10) — mandatory publication of membrane health observables, TP as institutional monitor, Allocator as Fairness signal — exists precisely to allow Phase Three to identify and correct membrane drift before it compounds. Whether that architecture succeeds depends on whether the Phase One baseline data was collected and preserved, and whether TP organises around the monitoring function as (BEHAV §10) anticipates. Both conditions are structural features of the design; neither is guaranteed.

## 11.4 Sequencing as a Design Variable

Behavioural robustness is not a property the institution either has or does not have at implementation. It must be built and maintained across time. The single most important sequencing decision is membrane-first: a mechanism that is operational before its membrane is ready will generate Phase One friction that compounds. A membrane ready before its mechanism is fully tested can absorb early behavioural responses while the mechanism is refined. That asymmetry argues consistently for prioritising membrane readiness in implementation planning.

# 12. Limitations and Further Work

## 12.1 Formal modelling gaps

The existing compliance literature does not address whether specific institutional communication choices alter adviser compliance norms in predictable ways. The OECD Cooperative Compliance programme found that certainty, predictability, and early disclosure arrangements improve compliance among sophisticated actors, but it did not isolate the effect of any particular administrative feature within that broader relationship architecture. This is a gap in the literature's agenda, not merely a data availability problem.

## 12.2 Phase One empirical unknowns

The most acute unresolved exposure is the Agrawal net-effect question. The cross-base fiscal loss ratio cannot be quantified against membrane investment at the margin before Phase One data exists. The risk is highest in Phase One, where the membrane is untested and the cross-base taxes still operate in parallel. Six responses to this risk are set out in (BEHAV §9.2); none resolves the underlying empirical uncertainty.

The route distribution estimates in (BEHAV §8.12) are the section most exposed to revision by Phase One data. The 40–55% estimate for Route A is directionally defensible from UK wealth composition data but carries the WAS undersampling caveat (JUR §2.4) identifies. The Route D estimate of 10–20% is the most uncertain and the most consequential for the enforcement paradigm shift claim: if Phase One route adoption shows Route D concentration significantly above the upper bound, the residual is larger than (BEHAV §8.12) suggests.

Several administrative-layer design questions are implementation-dependent. The four-route valuation architecture is logically clear but has not been translated into communications that allow a taxpayer to identify their applicable route without professional assistance in routine cases. The individual notification architecture for the loss refund is unspecified at the operational level. Response-time standards for taxpayer actions are calibration questions Phase One data will begin to answer.

## 12.3 Jurisdiction-specific legal and implementation work

Whether the administrative test for distinguishing continued residence from nominal presence is legally robust in the UK reference jurisdiction requires analysis outside this paper's scope. The basis-of-decision requirement's legal character — what obligations it places on the Administrator and what remedies are available on challenge — similarly requires jurisdiction-specific legal design.

## 12.4 Structural and irreducible limits of the design

BEHAV does not model behavioural outcomes, does not predict elasticities, and does not resolve the principal empirical unknowns. It sets out the design framework within which Phase One can generate evidence on those questions. What the paper establishes is that the mechanism's behavioural robustness derives from its design properties — not from optimistic assumptions about taxpayer psychology — and that this robustness can be argued from first principles without requiring behavioural predictions the empirical record cannot currently support.

Membrane calcification (BEHAV §10) is the one form of institutional drift the design addresses least directly. The monitoring architecture described in (BEHAV §10) is the best available response within the existing institutional design; it is a named architecture with specified instruments and actors, not a prevention mechanism. The membrane equivalent of GOV's residue concept — the mandatory publication cycle providing longitudinal membrane health data — is the honest acknowledgement that the prevention may fail and the record of what was built should survive the failure.

# 13. Conclusion

The folk knowledge is correct. Wealthy people do leave in response to taxation. They do restructure, defer, offshore, and depart. They do find a way. The frustration that generates the emigration objection is not a misreading of reality. It is an accurate description of how every wealth tax designed so far has performed against a mobile, well-advised population with access to a global market in lower-tax jurisdictions.

The question this paper has been answering is why that is true, and whether it has to be.

The why is not mysterious. A realisation-based capital gains regime defers liability until the taxpayer chooses to trigger it, which means departure before realisation exports the gain tax-free. Exit tax regimes that exist to prevent this are routinely structured around, set at rates that make departure net-positive, or undermined by bilateral treaty conflicts that sophisticated advisers exploit as a matter of course. And above all of this sits a global landscape in which every jurisdiction is playing the same adversarial game, extracting as much as possible while offering as little as necessary. A wealthy taxpayer with international mobility is not choosing between paying and not paying. They are choosing between jurisdictions. The rational choice, in a world built entirely of adversarial systems, is to find the least adversarial one.

The folk knowledge is therefore not a statement about the character of wealthy people. It is a statement about the character of the systems that have been built so far. Every wealth tax that has been attempted has been built on an adversarial model: the state extracts, the taxpayer minimises, and the outcome depends on whose advisers are better. On that model, mobile capital will always find a way out, because the system offers nothing worth staying for that cannot be replicated elsewhere at lower cost.

The WDT does not improve that model. It replaces it.

The symmetric loss refund is the mechanism by which that replacement occurs. It is not a compliance incentive or a behavioural nudge. It is a structural commitment: the state participates in the taxpayer's losses at the same rate at which it participates in their gains. No existing tax system makes that commitment. No existing wealth tax offers a rational actor a financial reason to remain engaged with the institution that goes beyond the cost of exit. The WDT does.

The Membrane is the concept this paper introduces to make that change operational. The mechanism can be well-designed and still be experienced as adversarial if the administrative layer that sits between the taxpayer and the system is opaque, unresponsive, or inaccessible. The Membrane is not a peripheral feature of the WDT. It is the layer through which the institution's reciprocal character is either demonstrated or undermined at every point of contact. A symmetric mechanism behind a degraded membrane is still experienced as extraction. Clarity, Reciprocity, Fairness, Responsiveness, and Accessibility are the properties The Membrane must have for the mechanism's design to be felt rather than merely calculated.

The interaction structure of the membrane's health dimensions — established in (BEHAV §6.3) — shows that these five properties are not independent. Investment in Clarity propagates to Reciprocity and Accessibility. Responsiveness failures generate Fairness degradation even where no actual inconsistency exists. Fairness is downstream of all four other dimensions and independently degradable; it is the leading indicator of membrane failure, not a lagging consequence. This interaction structure matters for implementation sequencing: the highest-leverage membrane investments are those that are multiply complementary, and the most dangerous failures are those that generate cascading downstream consequences in dimensions not independently invested in.

The route distribution analysis in (BEHAV §8.12) establishes the empirical basis for the enforcement paradigm shift claim. Route D — the mechanism's most vulnerable point — likely covers a minority of WDT-taxable wealth by volume. The mechanisms whose self-correction is most active (Routes A and B) likely cover the majority. This is not a guarantee that the residual is manageable at the revenue-concentration level, where Route D exposure may be significantly higher. But it does mean the enforcement paradigm shift claim is defensible as a characterisation of the mechanism's architecture across the WDT taxpayer population as a whole.

The cross-base fiscal externality — the Agrawal finding that wealth-tax-driven emigration generates income tax and VAT losses approximately six times the direct revenue loss — is the most serious empirical challenge the paper faces. The six-part response in (BEHAV §9.2) does not dismiss it. It argues that the WDT has more responses available than any prior wealth tax design: membrane investment that is fiscally justified at a six-to-one multiplier; a rate calibration lever that no prior wealth tax held; a timeline extension buffer that trades time for retention without systemic failure; a re-entry rule that creates an incentive to return as the envelope deepens; and a formal possibility — not a prediction — that the three features distinguishing the WDT from every prior wealth tax might, under conditions Phase One can test, produce net inward migration rather than net emigration. That possibility inverts the Agrawal ratio from threat to dividend.

Membrane calcification is the institutional drift the WDT addresses least directly. The monitoring architecture in (BEHAV §10) is the best available response within the existing institutional design, and it is stated honestly as an architecture requiring TP organisation, Allocator engagement, and DR motivation — not a self-executing guarantee. The mandatory publication cycle for membrane health observables is the residue: not a prevention mechanism, but a record that survives.

The paper does not claim the WDT will end exit. It claims that a system designed for behavioural robustness — one that functions across the full distribution of responses including exit, that offers a rational actor something no adversarial system can offer, and that is administered through a membrane designed to demonstrate rather than undermine its reciprocal character — deserves a different answer to the emigration objection than the systems that have generated the folk knowledge so far.

The folk knowledge was earned. It does not have to be permanent.

# A. The Membrane in Practice {.appendix}

This appendix illustrates the membrane concept through three institutional examples. The first demonstrates a healthy membrane functioning as designed. The second and third demonstrate two distinct failure modes that produce degraded membranes through different mechanisms and for different structural reasons.

## A.1 A Healthy Membrane — Retail Banking

A retail bank customer initiates a transfer. The transfer is processed within seconds. The customer receives confirmation of receipt, with the transferred amount, the recipient, and a transaction reference. If the transfer cannot be processed — because the recipient account details are incorrect, because a fraud flag has been triggered, or because a limit has been reached — the customer receives an immediate notification naming the specific reason and, where applicable, a single action that will resolve it. The customer knows at every point what the system has done, what it has not done, and what they need to do next.

This is a healthy membrane. Information passes clearly in both directions. The institution's response to customer action is prompt, specific, and actionable. The customer does not need professional assistance to understand their position. The cost of honest engagement — checking a balance, initiating a transfer, querying a charge — is low enough that it is never the reason a customer disengages.

The retail banking membrane is healthy primarily because competition enforces it. A bank whose notifications are opaque, whose responses are slow, and whose error messages are uninformative loses customers to banks that do better. The membrane has been refined over decades of competitive pressure into something that functions because it has to. The lesson for institutional design is not that competition is the only mechanism for membrane health. It is that membrane health requires a sustained accountability mechanism of some kind, whether competitive, regulatory, or structural. Without one, membranes degrade.

## A.2 Monopoly-Induced Degradation — Water Utilities

A household receives a bill that appears to be incorrect. The meter reading on the bill does not match the reading the household recorded on the same date. The household contacts the utility to raise the discrepancy. They are placed in a telephone queue. When they reach an operator, the operator can see the account but cannot explain the discrepancy, because the billing system and the metering system are not integrated at the level of the customer-facing interface. A review is logged. The household is told it will be resolved within a defined period. The period passes. A further bill arrives, again apparently incorrect. The process repeats.

At no point in this process does the household consider switching to a different water utility. There is no different water utility. The institution holds a legal monopoly over the service, and the household's only alternatives to continued engagement are to pay under protest or to pursue a formal complaint through a regulatory body whose processes are slower and less accessible than the institution's own. The membrane is degraded not because the institution lacks the capacity to build a better one, but because it lacks the incentive. Competitive pressure, the mechanism that enforces membrane health in retail banking, is entirely absent.

This is monopoly-induced membrane degradation. The institution's internal processes may be internally coherent. The billing system may be accurate in its own terms. But the layer through which the customer experiences those processes has not been designed with the customer's ability to engage as a constraint, because that ability is not required for the institution's continued operation.

The tax authority is structurally analogous to the water utility in one critical respect: the taxpayer cannot switch providers. That structural similarity makes the water utility's membrane degradation a relevant warning for tax system design, and it is the reason the WDT's administrative architecture must build accountability mechanisms that substitute for the competitive pressure that is unavailable.

## A.3 Interest-Aligned Degradation — Mandatory Insurance Markets

In markets where insurance is mandatory — where the consumer must hold a policy and cannot opt out of the market — a specific and more damaging form of membrane degradation is possible. The institution's financial interest and the consumer's interest in a functional membrane are directly opposed. A consumer who successfully navigates the claims process costs the institution money. A consumer who abandons a valid claim due to complexity, delay, or opacity saves it money. The membrane that is most profitable for the institution is therefore the least functional for the consumer.

The result is a membrane that is degraded by design rather than by neglect. Complexity is not a failure of the administrative process; it is a feature of it. Delay is not an operational shortcoming; it is a retention mechanism. Opacity about the basis on which claims are assessed is not an accident of poor communication; it is a strategy for reducing the proportion of consumers who successfully challenge an incorrect decision. The institution invests in the appearance of a functional membrane — customer service infrastructure, published complaints procedures, regulatory compliance documentation — while systematically ensuring that the membrane functions poorly for the consumers who most need it to function well.

This failure mode does not arise in tax administration in the same direct form, because the tax authority does not financially benefit from taxpayer confusion in the same way a mandatory insurer benefits from claimant abandonment. But the structural lesson is important: where the institution's interests and the taxpayer's interests in membrane quality diverge, membrane degradation will follow unless the administrative design contains explicit mechanisms to prevent it. The Fairness and Responsiveness Principles in (BEHAV §5) are the WDT's structural response to that risk.
