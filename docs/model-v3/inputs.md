# Model v3 input register

Brief 012r. Built up over four sessions; this file holds **stage B1** (D4 wealth and tail, D9 income and liquid assets, D1 returns), **B2** (D5, D6, D10) and **B3** (comparators M5, M6, M8) and **B4** (tier 2: D7, D8, D11; the Green Book and M2 checks). Three further research files (briefs 022r wealth, 023r tax, 024r households) are merged in the part "Added inputs" near the end; the ONS decile workbook was read on 2026-11-17.
All sources accessed 2026-11-16 unless stated. UK only; each source's coverage outside the UK is in the Coverage cell (R11).
Derivations (method and code) for the computed rows: `docs/research/012r-b1-derivations.md`, `-b2-`, `-b3-`, `-b4-derivations.md`.

**Summary of gaps (read first).** The register has 287 rows (the seven D14 run-setting rows I-D14a to I-D14g added by brief 071g on 2026-11-19; I-D4h, adults per household, added by brief 069r on 2026-11-19; the five D13 route-mix rows I-D13a to I-D13e added by brief 068g on 2026-11-19; the five I-D1 rows added by `returns-method.md` section 5 on 2026-11-18: I-D1a2, I-D1b2, I-D1a0, I-D1f, I-D1g; I-D1a to I-D1d replaced; before that 269): 93 in the stages B1 to B4 (89 as first written, plus I-D6o to I-D6q), and 177 in the added parts (I-W 45, I-T 58, I-H 74; I-H includes the ten household-decile non-payer rows I-H2a1 to I-H2a10 of brief 028r, which replaced the gap row I-H2a). The gaps below are those of B1 to B4; each added part has its own Gaps section. Weakest: (1) nothing public gives CGT, IHT or income tax by wealth band, so the M6 band rows are derived and flagged; (2) the tail of the wealth distribution depends on the Rich List (cite only) with Advani, Summers, Tarrant 2025 as the chosen fallback, and the Round 8 wealth-to-GDP conflict with the ONS balance sheet is carried as I-D4g; (3) income by wealth band (D9) and the incidence of business rates and corporation tax (D6) lean on foreign or derived evidence; (4) three wealth tax proposals' revenues are the proponents' own; (5) D7 and D8 are v2 model outputs, not independent evidence. The Green Book schedule is confirmed against the Treasury response (not the Green Book text), and the RATES 0.35% burden is not M2: it uses terminal wealth, and M2's denominator needs deciding (see the M2 check).

## Gaps and conflicts so far (B1)
- **Round 9 of the Wealth and Assets Survey is not usable.** ONS lists April 2022 to March 2024 as complete and said in February 2025 it expected release in 2026 (S11); I found no release by 2026-11-16 and did not check the ONS release calendar. The model uses **Round 8 (April 2020 to March 2022, published 24 January 2025), held in the repo.** The 2016-18 fallback is not needed.
- **Pension valuation break.** Round 8 values private pensions at £4,808bn against £6,445bn in Round 7 on the old basis, so total wealth falls from £15,221bn to £13,568bn between rounds. Earlier waves are not comparable; Resolution Foundation's own method gives about £2.9tn (17%) more than ONS's new one (S4, Box 1).
- **Wealth-to-GDP conflict, partly reconciled (B2).** The ONS national balance sheet 2026 puts household net worth at £10.8tn for 2025 (S12): produced assets £2.1tn (mainly dwellings), non-produced assets £4.5tn (mainly land) and household financial net worth £4.3tn. Against GDP of about £3.0tn (GDP for 2025 is not in the bulletin; the figure is my approximation) that is 3.6, against 5.8 to 7.5 here (row I-D4g). ONS says the two bulletins are not directly comparable (different concepts, sources and methods). My reading, not ONS's: (a) WAS Round 8 includes £1,385bn of physical wealth (vehicles, contents) that national accounts do not count as assets, about 0.6 of a GDP point; (b) WAS counts private pensions at £4,808bn on its own valuation, while the balance sheet carries funded schemes inside financial net worth and the bulletin does not say how defined-benefit schemes are treated; (c) the dates differ, 2020-22 against 2025, and the table above uses GDP for the survey years. I did not close the residual gap, so the 3.6 figure is carried as a separate row rather than folded into the range. The author may want a lower low for I-D4a.
- **Tail shares are not comparable across sources.** ABL's 51% top-10% share (2016-18, old pension basis, personal wealth) against 40.7% from the Round 8 decile table (households, new pension basis). Units and valuation differ, so the D4d range mixes definitions; flagged in the row.
- **Tail data licence unverified.** The Times terms page was not on the fetch allowlist; the Rich List 2026 figure (£784bn, 350 people, up 1.4%) was read from a High Pay Centre summary, not from the Sunday Times (S7).
- **No liquid-only split by wealth band.** The held tables give net financial wealth by band and asset-type holdings for all households, not liquid assets by band. D9 liquid rows use net financial wealth as an upper-side proxy.
- **Income by wealth band is derived, not published.** The Round 8 tables give wealth quartiles by income decile (Table 2.6); I inverted them. No joint distribution was found (S9 gives only income quintiles).
- **Returns end in 2019 or 2020.** JST R6 stops before the 2022 gilt and inflation shock; the latest JST release was not verified (page fetch failed) and the Bank of England millennium dataset's page says only that it is updated to 2016.
- **GDP vintage.** The OECD series on FRED was last updated January 2024; later Blue Book revisions are not applied (Blue Book 2026 gives £2,905bn for 2024).

- **ONS decile tax tables not reached (B2).** The Effects of taxes and benefits FYE 2024 workbook (S23) holds council tax and stamp duty by income decile, but the file would not download (WebFetch error; the shell cannot reach ons.gov.uk). Every D6 decile share is therefore a derived allocation, not a published incidence, and is flagged. If the author attaches the workbook, the council tax and stamp duty rows can be replaced with published shares.
- **No UK evidence found for business rates or corporation tax incidence by income decile, or for valuation understatement.** Foreign evidence is used and flagged (S13, S22).

## Gaps and conflicts (B3)
- **No source gives CGT, IHT or income tax by wealth band.** HMRC publishes by estate size, gain size and income. The M6 by-band rows (I-M6a to I-M6d, I-M6h, I-M6i) are published liabilities over held ONS wealth, with the allocation to bands stated and flagged. The CGT allocation to wealth decile D10 is a bound from 45% to 100%, not data. Income tax is not allocated to wealth bands: only a top-income effective rate (I-M6l, 2015-16) and an all-household ratio (I-M6m) are given. A joint wealth and tax file would settle it; I did not find a public one.
- **HMRC primary pages for CGT 2024-25 were not reachable** (gov.uk pages off the fetch allowlist). The totals (£24.2bn, £127.3bn, 584,000 payers) come from press summaries that agree with each other (S34, S36). The held IFS receipts sheet shows £19.5bn for the same year; the gap is basis (receipts against liabilities) and an old forecast vintage.
- **The 45% share from the largest gains is ambiguous.** The one press report does not say whether it is gains or liabilities (I-M6j).
- **IHT bands below £1m not read.** The HMRC commentary page read gives bands from £1m; the claim that D4 to 7 pay nothing is an assumption (I-M6d). The split of the 364 estates above £7.5m into 144 and 220 was derived by the fetch tool from a stated 220, not read as published.
- **Zucman's threshold is in dollars and the global figures are global.** The brief's "about £1bn" is a rounding; no exchange rate is set. The UK application is Tippet and Zucman 2026 (£100m threshold, income tax credited), which is a different design from the global 2% above US$1bn, and it uses the Sunday Times Rich List (S7), which is cite only.
- **Two proposals' revenue figures come from the proponents** (Tax Justice UK £24bn, Green Party £15bn); no independent estimate of either was found. IFS gives £10bn for a 1% tax above £10m before behavioural response (S30), which scales to about £20bn at 2% if linear (my arithmetic, not in the table).
- **Wealth Tax Commission report not re-read.** The one-off proposal is taken from a summary (S29); its annual-tax illustration was not found there.
- **LVT sensitivity range 1.1% to 1.7%** is on a mirror of the Neidle post, not on the primary page's first 100,000 characters (I-M8f).
- **No source costs a deemed disposal at death against carry-over**, and the alignment and uplift estimates are static and costed separately (I-M8c adds them).

## Check: M2 against the RATES paper's 0.35% (B4)
Method and numbers: `docs/research/012r-b4-derivations.md`. RATES (`source/8_Rates_and_Revenue.md` and the appendix, B.3.3 to B.3.6) read by section; the tables are the 2000 Balanced reference at N = 30. **Result: the 0.35% is not M2 as defined in `metrics.md`, and the two differ by a large factor depending on the wealth denominator.**
- **Replication.** Reading the printed burden table (4 growth tiers by 10 wealth brackets) with the printed bracket populations (34.6m taxpayers) and terminal wealth V_N: population-weighted 0.243% (paper 0.24%); wealth-weighted 0.361% (paper 0.35%). The 0.011 point gap is a proxy: the paper divides by post-settlement terminal wealth TW_settled, which I used V_N for. So the paper's 0.35% is **the wealth-weighted mean of tax over terminal wealth, weights being terminal wealth** (appendix B.3.3, formula given there). Its denominator is wealth 30 years on, not at the start of the year.
- **M2 as defined** (revenue-weighted mean of T_i/W_i, W_i the start-of-year wealth): same tax, three denominators. W = terminal V_N: 0.39%. W = geometric mean of start and terminal wealth (a crude path average): 1.24%. W = starting wealth V_0: 4.08%. These are indicative: the real path average needs the model's yearly wealth, which this check did not run.
- **Reading.** The formula in `metrics.md` and the paper's formula are different quantities, and RATES' claim that the burden is "structurally below the 1 to 2% of stock levies" holds on a terminal-wealth denominator and is not shown on a start-of-year one (about 1.2% on my crude path average). I did not change the paper or the metric. Needed from the author or guru: which denominator M2 should use, and whether RATES should say so (a brief suggestion is added to `docs/brief-suggestions.md`).

#### Gaps and conflicts
- **No UK evidence on return by wealth rank** beyond a composition-based estimate (I-W1c, financial assets, 2016-18). The Fagereng et al. figures are Norwegian; Figure 5 (returns by percentile) was not in the extracted text, so the top-1% against median gap is not given.
- **Persistence by wealth percentile**: only fixed-effect persistence and parent-child slopes were found. The rank autocorrelation of returns over ten years the request asks for is not in the part of the paper read (the last 43,000 characters, including the intergenerational section, were not returned).
- **No UK saving rate by wealth band.** The UK rows are aggregates: net saving as a share of the change in financial wealth, and active saving in total wealth gains.
- **Round 8 top-1% share is not published.** I-W5b is a fitted tail (alpha 2.1 to 2.4) and is a judgement row; it sits below the 14.4 to 23 published for 2016-18 on a per-adult basis, so the two should not be read as one series.
- **The grid above £1m (I-W6e to I-W6g) is a fit**, not data. Round 8 bands stop at £1m. Pareto alpha is set by the decile-10 aggregate, so a heavier real tail would raise the shares; the Rich List (cite only) is not used.
- **Basis break.** The ONS changed the pension valuation method between 2018-20 and 2020-22, so Round 8 does not continue the earlier shares on one basis (I-W5e).
- **Households against adults.** Deciles and bands are household-based; the per-adult figures are 2016-18 (I-W5c, I-W5d).
- **M-2 is derived from rounded two-year figures** (I-W8a, I-W8b); a four-year rank correlation (I-W4c) cross-checks the order of magnitude only.
- **Colombia and Switzerland are foreign.** The Swiss paper body was not returned (abstract only); the Colombian wealth tax is a notch design.

#### Gaps and conflicts
- **ONS decile workbook obtained and read (2026-11-17).** `etbreferencetablesfye202324final.xlsx` is now in `Research/`. I-H1k to I-H1t and I-H3k to I-H3t are filled from Tables 2b, 2c and 13. The quintile rows I-H1a to I-H1j (FYE 2023, secondary) stay as a cross-check.
- **Quintiles, not deciles; FYE 2023 not 2024** for the tax rows. They come from a Civitas reproduction of ONS Table data, so they are secondary. The Civitas text says the top quintile pays 29.5% of gross income in income tax; its table shows 22.8% (29.5% is total direct tax); the table is used.
- **Non-payers by decile** needs the ONS ad hoc 12451 workbook (FYE 2019) or the ETB decile workbook.
- **Fetch-tool arithmetic error.** The fetch summary of the ONS 2020 spending-wealth article gave spending-to-wealth ratios of 8.4 and 0.03 and spending-to-income of 0.14; dividing the published amounts gives 0.84, 0.027 and 1.17 (I-H7a to I-H7c). Units of the spending figure (annual or weekly) were not stated in the text read.
- **No UK data on leavers.** The M-4 rows use additional-rate taxpayers as a proxy group; whether they are the people most likely to leave is a model assumption.
- **Care worker pay.** ASHE 2026 care-worker median not found (only the 75th, 80th and 90th centiles); the median is the Skills for Care independent-sector figure for England.
- **JST data end in 2020** and the UK housing series starts in 1896; the 2020 equity return is -9.4%.
- **Elasticity of marginal utility equals relative risk aversion only under CRRA** preferences.
