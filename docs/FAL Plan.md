# Glossary {.unnumbered .unlisted}

**Break-even burden:** The annual WDT wealth-equivalent burden at which a representative taxpayer of a given wealth level is indifferent between WDT and the existing tax system, accounting for all economic costs of each.

**Conditional support:** An evidentiary outcome in which a hypothesis holds only within an identifiable parameter range, with the binding constraints explicitly characterised.

**Falsification:** An evidentiary outcome in which a hypothesis fails across empirically defensible parameter ranges, or survives only under assumptions that are themselves implausible.

**Hostile modelling:** Modelling conducted by researchers with every incentive and opportunity to demonstrate that a hypothesis fails, rather than by the hypothesis's proponents.

**H2a–H2e:** The five sub-hypotheses into which the macro hypothesis (H2) is decomposed. Each has an independent causal mechanism and can be tested and falsified independently of the others.

**Positive feedback loop:** The chain of mechanisms by which WDT-induced improvements in capital allocation and labour tax displacement are hypothesised to amplify one another, increasing both the WDT tax base and broader welfare over time.

**Support:** An evidentiary outcome in which a hypothesis holds across a substantial range of empirically defensible parameters.

# 1. Purpose and Methodological Commitment

## 1.1 What this paper is

FAL is a different kind of paper from the others in this series. It does not defend WDT. It identifies the propositions on which WDT most depends, states them in their strongest falsifiable form, and specifies what evidence or modelling would count against them.

The central methodological commitment is:

> WDT should be exposed to attempts to falsify its strongest claims rather than evaluated only through models constructed by its proponents.

The author does not currently possess the computational or empirical resources required to model all five questions adequately. That is not a weakness to be minimised. It is what makes this paper necessary.

## 1.2 What this paper does not claim

This paper does not establish that the hypotheses are true. It does not present new modelling. It does not summarise the existing WDT papers in defence of WDT. It takes no position on whether the hypotheses will survive hostile scrutiny.

It specifies the hypotheses so that they can be attacked.

## 1.3 Relation to the existing project

The 26-paper WDT series has established a theoretical and institutional architecture and closed three confirmed literature gaps through WFR. Six literature gaps remain open; ten empirical questions await Phase One data. The open questions register records all of these without pretending they are resolved.

FAL does not add to that architecture. It identifies the five empirical propositions that, if shown to be false, would most seriously damage the case for WDT. It then provides enough structural detail for a researcher who wishes to attack them to know where to aim.

# 2. The Research Question

> Under what conditions would the behavioural, macroeconomic, institutional, and welfare mechanisms proposed by the Wealth Delta Tax fail?

Five specific questions follow from this:

1. Would wealthy taxpayers prefer WDT to existing tax arrangements when total economic costs are compared rather than tax liability alone?
2. Would WDT's capital allocation effects, labour tax displacement, consumer demand channel, and automatic stabilisation produce a net positive macroeconomic outcome?
3. Could WDT generate and sustain a cooperative institutional equilibrium through and beyond its bootstrapping phase?
4. Does taxing changes in wealth produce lower aggregate distortion than taxing intermediate manifestations of the same economic process?
5. Is the WDT tax base structurally more resilient to automation-driven labour income decline than fiscal systems whose primary base is labour?

Each is stated as a hypothesis below. Each carries a precise falsification condition.

---

# 3. H1: Wealthy Taxpayer Preference

## 3.1 The hypothesis

> **H1:** Under plausible parameterisations, wealthy taxpayers will prefer WDT to the existing tax system when their total economic costs are considered rather than their tax liability alone.

## 3.2 The comparison that matters

The relevant comparison is not:

**WDT tax bill vs current tax bill.**

It is:

**WDT total cost vs current system total cost.**

The WDT total cost includes the 0.35% revenue-weighted annual wealth burden at reference parameters, net of expected refund value and expected institutional participation benefits.

The current system total cost includes taxes actually paid, avoidance expenditure, accounting and legal costs, compliance costs, portfolio distortion, realisation lock-in, tax uncertainty, exposure to future tax changes, migration costs, and inheritance planning costs.

The 0.35% figure is the reference scenario floor. If the current system's all-in friction cost for a very wealthy UK taxpayer is materially above 0.35%, the migration hypothesis weakens substantially. If it is materially below, the hypothesis becomes much harder to sustain.

## 3.3 The break-even burden

The most valuable single output a hostile model could produce is the break-even WDT burden: the annual wealth-equivalent burden at which a representative taxpayer at each WDT wealth percentile bracket (the P90, P95, P99, and upper tail brackets as defined in RATES and JUR) becomes indifferent between WDT and the existing system.

That is a far more useful object than "wealthy people will prefer WDT." It gives the Governing Council a calibration target keyed to the actual population distribution and gives critics a specific threshold to attack at each wealth level. Exact bracket values will be drawn from RATES and JUR when those papers are cross-referenced into the final draft.

## 3.4 The sovereign voice component

H1 is not simply a tax-cost comparison. WDT taxpayers acquire a continuing relationship with an institution whose capital partly reflects their own contributions. They receive:

- formal representation in the Governing Council (TP Chamber);
- a claim on SWF returns through the refund mechanism;
- symmetric downside protection;
- a more predictable and lower-friction fiscal regime.

The hypothesis is not that wealthy taxpayers will value these things altruistically. It is that a taxpayer with a multi-decade horizon may rationally value institutional predictability and downside participation more than the expected annual cost of holding them.

## 3.5 Falsification conditions

H1 is falsified if a heterogeneous taxpayer choice model demonstrates that:

- the current system's all-in friction cost is below the WDT reference burden at each wealth level;
- the expected value of institutional participation is negligible or negative;
- avoidance opportunities under WDT are sufficient to undermine the system at plausible evasion rates; or
- WDT reduces expected lifetime wealth enough to make migration rational for a substantial fraction of the taxpayer population.

**Current status: Hypothesis only.**

# 4. H2: Macroeconomic Effects

## 4.1 Structure of the decomposition

H2 is not a single hypothesis. The original framing — that WDT produces non-negative long-run welfare effects — contains five causally distinct claims, each requiring a different model type and each independently falsifiable. Collapsing them into one hypothesis obscures where the chain is most likely to break.

The five sub-hypotheses are stated below. H2d is the only one that requires all others to hold simultaneously.

## 4.2 H2a: Capital allocation efficiency

> **H2a:** Removing lock-in and debt-preference distortions improves the allocation of capital across investments relative to a realisation-based system, holding revenue constant.

**Mechanism:** Realisation taxation creates a switching wedge between the gross return differential and the after-tax cost of moving to a superior asset. WDT eliminates the lock-in distortion by construction (WFR §4.2). The 141–143 basis point lock-in welfare cost under CGT is a Category 1 result from WFR — a cost of an existing operating system, not a projected WDT benefit. H2a is therefore the sub-hypothesis with the strongest existing support.

**Required model:** A portfolio-choice or asset-allocation model holding macro conditions fixed, introducing the delta base in place of CGT, allowing portfolio reallocation, and measuring net productive investment change.

**Falsification condition:** Net capital misallocation is greater under WDT than under the CGT counterfactual for empirically defensible portfolio elasticity parameters.

**Current status: Partial theoretical support from WFR Category 1 results. Quantitative magnitude not established.**

## 4.3 H2b: Labour tax displacement welfare effect

> **H2b:** Replacing a portion of labour taxation with WDT revenue, through the constitutionally committed LRR accumulation, produces a net welfare gain relative to the pre-displacement position.

**Mechanism:** Bilateral NICs removal reduces the cost of labour to supply and hire simultaneously. The welfare gain depends on two conditions: (i) the deadweight loss of labour taxation exceeds the deadweight loss of WDT at the relevant margins; and (ii) the LRR accumulates fast enough to deliver meaningful displacement within a policy-relevant horizon. The ENV paper identifies bilateral NICs removal as the cleanest implementation case but does not quantify the welfare differential.

**Required model:** A labour market or public finance welfare model comparing the deadweight loss of WDT (including valuation friction and behavioural responses) against the deadweight loss of the labour taxation it displaces.

**Falsification condition:** Either (i) the deadweight loss of WDT at reference parameters exceeds the deadweight loss of the displaced labour taxation for the relevant population; or (ii) the LRR timeline is too slow to constitute a credible fiscal substitute within any realistic political window, leaving the displacement claim as a theoretical assertion with no achievable path.

**Current status: Hypothesis only.**

## 4.4 H2c: Automatic stabilisation

> **H2c:** The symmetric refund mechanism creates a state-contingent fiscal position that dampens asset-market crises more effectively than existing automatic stabilisers, specifically by reaching the leveraged and liquidation-prone population at the moment of maximum systemic risk.

**Mechanism:** A conventional income tax is procyclical on the revenue side during recessions. WDT is mechanically countercyclical: a crash produces negative wealth deltas and triggers automatic refunds to the population most likely to engage in forced selling. The SWF's accumulated reserves absorb part of the fiscal cost, and the DR chamber's pre-commitment capacity provides a second countercyclical instrument without discretionary intervention.

The opposing effect is real and must be included: large refund outflows deteriorate the government's fiscal position simultaneously, which may be contractionary elsewhere unless the SWF buffer is sufficient.

**Required model:** A financial stability or crisis dynamics model allowing simultaneous operation of (i) refund-induced household liquidity injection, (ii) government fiscal position deterioration, and (iii) SWF drawdown. The crux question is whether the SWF can plausibly accumulate enough during normal years to absorb the fiscal cost in a severe crash without triggering net contractionary effects on the broader economy.

**Falsification condition:** The combined fiscal effect of large refund outflows and SWF drawdown during a crash scenario is net contractionary — either because the SWF is insufficiently capitalised at the moment of crisis, or because government fiscal deterioration offsets the household liquidity benefit.

**Current status: Hypothesis only. No quantitative model exists.**

## 4.5 H2d: Positive feedback loop integrity

> **H2d:** The efficiency gains from H2a, the welfare gain from H2b, and the consumer demand expansion from the labour tax relief dividend combine to produce a compounding positive effect on the WDT tax base and broader fiscal capacity over time, under the condition that labour tax displacement is managed at a pace that remains below the threshold at which demand stimulus becomes net inflationary.

**Mechanism — positive chain:**

WDT → reduced lock-in (H2a) → improved capital allocation → higher productivity → higher private returns → larger WDT base → greater fiscal capacity → labour tax displacement (H2b) → higher disposable income for lower and middle earners → higher consumer spending → higher aggregate demand → higher corporate revenues → higher asset valuations → larger WDT base.

This loop has a second amplifier: wealthy taxpayers holding demand-correlated assets — equities, commercial property, consumer-sector holdings — benefit from the consumer demand channel through higher valuations. The labour tax relief dividend therefore raises the expected value of remaining in the WDT system for the very taxpayers who fund it, creating a partial connection between H2d and H3.

**Mechanism — negative chain:**

WDT → lower after-tax expected return → reduced capital formation → lower investment → lower productivity → lower wealth growth → smaller WDT base.

The modeller's task is to determine which chain dominates.

**The inflation condition:** Conditioning H2d on inflationary shocks being avoided treats the hardest problem as a given. The inflation risk from large, fast NICs displacement is an endogenous consequence of the mechanism itself if the pace is wrong — not an external event to be assumed away. The staged, constitutionally-committed LRR pacing specified in ENV is designed to keep displacement below a rate that would persistently breach the central bank inflation target. That target — 2% under the standard framework applicable to the UK reference jurisdiction — provides the concrete and externally validated reference point. The hypothesis does not require inflation to be zero. It requires that the pace of displacement remain consistent with maintaining inflation at or near the 2% target over the medium term.

H2d therefore holds conditional on:

> The rate of labour tax displacement not producing demand stimulus that pushes inflation materially and persistently above the 2% central bank target, with the constitutionally-committed LRR accumulation pace demonstrated to remain within this bound under realistic behavioural parameters.

**Required model:** A DSGE or heterogeneous-agent general equilibrium model containing WDT, symmetric refunds, SWF, labour tax replacement, capital allocation, heterogeneous returns, investment, consumption, migration, and government expenditure. This is the most demanding model in the series. The key output is not a yes/no verdict but the parameter values at which the positive loop fails to dominate the negative loop — specifically: at what capital-flight elasticity, avoidance rate, labour displacement pace, and productivity response does the feedback fail?

**Falsification condition:** H2d is falsified if the negative loop dominates for empirically defensible parameter ranges, or if constitutionally-paced LRR accumulation produces demand stimulus that persistently breaches the 2% central bank target under realistic behavioural parameters, or if the pace of displacement required to remain within the 2% bound is so slow that it fails to deliver meaningful fiscal displacement within a politically viable horizon.

**Current status: Hypothesis only. ENV identifies the transmission channels but explicitly does not constitute a general equilibrium model.**

## 4.6 H2e: Automation base robustness

*(See H5 below. H2e is promoted to a standalone hypothesis because its causal mechanism is comparative and structural rather than part of the positive feedback loop.)*

Note: H5 is not independent of H2d. If automation destroys the consumer demand channel on which H2d's feedback loop partly depends, then H5's base resilience claim and H2d's positive loop weaken simultaneously. A model that takes both seriously in the same simulation would be more informative than testing them in isolation.

# 5. H3: Cooperative Institutional Equilibrium

## 5.1 The hypothesis

> **H3:** A WDT jurisdiction can reach and maintain a stable cooperative equilibrium in which wealthy taxpayers have sufficient long-term incentives to preserve the credibility of the WDT/SWF institution rather than collectively attempting to undermine, capture, or exit it.

## 5.2 What the hypothesis is not

H3 does not claim that wealthy taxpayers will behave altruistically. It claims that the repeated-game value of preserving the institution can exceed the short-term gain from individually exploiting or capturing it.

The taxpayer is not deciding: "Do I like paying taxes?"

They are deciding: "What institutional arrangement maximises the expected net value of my wealth over a 30-year horizon?"

The WDT offers: lower friction, downside protection, institutional voice, a more predictable regime, and a contingent claim on SWF returns. Whether these are worth preserving is a question about private incentives, not public spirit.

## 5.3 The repeated-game structure

The equilibrium of interest is not a one-shot compliance decision. It is a repeated game in which each taxpayer's choice to cooperate, defect, capture, or exit is affected by what they expect others to do and what they expect the institution to be worth in future periods.

The interesting dynamic is:

**Individual incentive:** "Can I profitably manipulate this institution?"

↓

**Collective incentive:** "If everyone manipulates it, the institution loses credibility and becomes worthless to all of us."

↓

**Long-term incentive:** "I hold a large contingent claim on this institution's future functioning. Protecting its credibility protects the value of that claim."

Whether this long-term incentive is sufficient to sustain cooperation under realistic conditions is exactly what a hostile game theorist should attempt to break.

## 5.4 The bootstrapping phase

The bootstrapping problem is the period before the SWF is capitalised and before the refund mechanism has been demonstrated in practice. It is H3's most acute vulnerability window: the cooperative equilibrium requires institutional credibility, and credibility is precisely what Phase One has not yet established.

The bootstrapping problem is primarily a political design choice, not an irreducible modelling problem. The length of the vulnerability window is determined by the Phase One entry threshold. A sufficiently high threshold — restricting Phase One to a small population of very large wealth-holders — compresses the bootstrapping phase substantially, because the SRR fills faster from a small high-wealth population than from a large lower-wealth population. In the limit, a high enough threshold reduces the bootstrapping phase to near-zero: a small number of ultra-high-net-worth taxpayers whose combined contributions capitalise the SRR within one or two assessment cycles. The trade-off is that a higher threshold produces a smaller initial population, slower institutional development, and a longer path to Phase Two. The Phase One threshold is therefore a political variable, not a modelling input, and the paper should not treat the bootstrapping phase as having a fixed length requiring a parametric solution.

**Voluntary prefunding:** A further mechanism belongs in this analysis. A taxpayer who understands that Phase One is the most vulnerable period for the institution, and who has calculated that institutional survival is in their long-run interest, has a rational incentive to voluntarily prefund the SRR beyond their mechanical contribution — accelerating capitalisation and shortening the vulnerability window. This is not altruism. It is the same "buying an equilibrium" logic that underlies H1 and H3 more broadly: the taxpayer is paying now to increase the probability that the institution survives to deliver the benefits they expect. Voluntary prefunding is not assumed to occur. It is a mechanism that may occur if the H3 cooperative equilibrium logic holds sufficiently strongly, and it creates a partial self-correcting property in the bootstrapping phase — the taxpayers most exposed to the vulnerability window may have a private incentive to close it. Whether that incentive is sufficient to generate meaningful prefunding in practice is itself a question for the repeated-game model.

Two paths:

**Path A:** Phase One → threshold chosen to compress bootstrapping window → SRR capitalises rapidly → first refund demonstrated → envelope deepening → credibility accumulates → voluntary prefunding possible → taxpayer cooperation strengthens.

**Path B:** Phase One → threshold too low or voluntary prefunding insufficient → extended vulnerability window → taxpayer exit or avoidance → weak SWF → lower credibility → institutional failure.

H3 hypothesises that Path A is attainable. Whether it is attained depends on the threshold choice and on whether the cooperative equilibrium is strong enough to generate voluntary prefunding during the window that remains.

## 5.5 Required model

A repeated game or institutional model containing:

- individual and collective capture
- lobbying and corruption
- strategic exit and strategic compliance
- reputation and institutional credibility
- SWF returns and accumulated reserves
- future tax liabilities and refund claims
- political intervention capacity
- voluntary prefunding as an available action (not an assumed behaviour)
- Phase One entry threshold as a parameter affecting bootstrapping window length

The model must be given every opportunity to make cooperation collapse. If a stable cooperative equilibrium exists under these conditions, identify the parameter ranges. If it does not, that is a serious result against WDT and should be reported as such. The model should also characterise whether voluntary prefunding emerges as an equilibrium behaviour, and if so, at what cooperative stake level and under what conditions it breaks down.

## 5.6 Falsification conditions

H3 is falsified if the model demonstrates that:

- individual capture is always privately optimal for a significant fraction of the taxpayer population regardless of institutional credibility;
- cooperative behaviour cannot be sustained across any bootstrapping window length consistent with politically plausible Phase One threshold choices;
- wealthy taxpayers have stronger incentives to exit before institutional benefits materialise than to remain and accumulate cooperative stake, even at threshold levels that minimise the bootstrapping window;
- voluntary prefunding does not emerge as an equilibrium behaviour at any plausible cooperative stake level; or
- a small group can profitably destroy institutional credibility faster than it accumulates, regardless of prefunding.

**Current status: Hypothesis only.**

# 6. H4: Accumulation-Point Efficiency

## 6.1 The hypothesis

> **H4:** Conditional on a comparable comprehensive economic base and appropriately specified redistribution, taxing changes in wealth produces lower aggregate distortion than taxing intermediate manifestations of the same economic process — particularly realised gains and existing wealth stocks.

## 6.2 The mechanism

The standard tax systems intervene at intermediate points in the economic process:

**accumulation → asset holding → realisation / transaction / income event → tax**

WDT intervenes at the underlying change in economic capacity:

**accumulation → wealth increase → tax**

The hypothesis is that moving the tax point closer to the underlying change reduces the number of behavioural decisions that become tax-relevant. Every decision about when to realise, what to hold, how to structure an asset, or how to time a transaction that would otherwise be tax-motivated becomes irrelevant under accrual taxation.

The lock-in welfare cost of 141–143 basis points established in WFR is a Category 1 result that directly supports the mechanism for CGT specifically. H4 extends this to the general comparative claim across tax systems.

## 6.3 What a hostile model should measure

For equivalent revenue, a comparative welfare model should report the following across WDT, CGT, income tax, stock wealth tax, and consumption tax:

- asset allocation distortion
- realisation distortion and lock-in magnitude
- administrative complexity
- avoidance expenditure
- compliance costs
- deadweight loss
- tax-base elasticity
- welfare at comparable revenue

The WFR controlled comparison is the starting framework. H4 asks whether the H4 claim survives once valuation friction, liquidity costs, migration, avoidance, and behavioural responses are included.

## 6.4 Falsification conditions

H4 is falsified if a comparative welfare model demonstrates that, after accounting for valuation costs, liquidity constraints, migration, avoidance, administrative costs, and behavioural responses, WDT generates greater total distortion than the alternative systems across empirically defensible parameter ranges.

This is a clean falsification criterion. The model need not show that WDT is catastrophically bad. It needs only to show that the reduction in realisation distortion is offset by valuation friction, liquidity costs, and avoidance costs sufficient to leave total distortion higher.

**Current status: Partial theoretical support from WFR Category 1 results on CGT lock-in. General comparative claim not quantitatively established.**

# 7. H5: Automation Base Robustness

## 7.1 The hypothesis

> **H5:** The WDT tax base is structurally more resilient to automation-driven labour income decline than fiscal systems whose primary revenue base is labour income, because the delta base captures capital appreciation regardless of whether that appreciation was generated by human or automated production.

## 7.2 The mechanism

As automation increases the productive value of capital relative to labour, three things happen simultaneously in most existing fiscal systems: labour income falls, labour tax revenues fall, and fiscal pressure to maintain welfare expenditure increases. The result is a structural squeeze on the fiscal position.

WDT does not share this structure. Revenue tracks wealth delta regardless of whether appreciation was generated by human labour or capital deployment. As automation increases returns to capital, WDT revenue increases automatically without legislative change. This is categorically different from robot taxes, capital income taxes, or stock wealth taxes, each of which requires identifying a discrete taxable event or stock.

## 7.3 The dependency on H2d

H5 is not unconditional. The automation base resilience claim depends on automation increasing aggregate capital returns sufficiently to grow the WDT base. Under automation trajectories in which capital is highly concentrated in a small number of asset-holders who then restructure or exit, the base may not grow as predicted.

More critically: if automation destroys the consumer demand channel on which H2d's positive feedback loop partly depends — by reducing labour income and household spending across the broader economy — then H5's base resilience and H2d's positive loop weaken simultaneously. The two claims have a common vulnerability. A model testing both in the same simulation is more informative than testing each in isolation.

## 7.4 Falsification conditions

H5 is falsified if an automation scenario model demonstrates that:

- automation trajectories under which WDT revenue is not more resilient than labour-tax revenue for plausible automation pace and concentration parameters;
- automation disproportionately benefits a small number of asset-holders with sufficient avoidance capacity to prevent base growth tracking automation-driven capital appreciation;
- the avoidance capacity of the automated-capital-holding population grows faster than the WDT base under automation; or
- the destruction of the consumer demand channel under aggressive automation severs the H2d connection between labour tax relief and asset value growth, undermining the resilience claim.

**Current status: Hypothesis only. Open question register item 12 (automation and tax-base migration) is assigned to MACRO and requires Phase One data before modelling can be calibrated.**

# 8. Interactions Between the Five Hypotheses

Not all five hypotheses are independent. Several share common mechanisms and common vulnerabilities.

**H1 and H3 share the cooperative stake logic.** H1 argues that wealthy taxpayers prefer WDT on total cost grounds; H3 argues that they maintain cooperative behaviour to protect the institution. The institutional value component of H1 is partly constituted by the H3 equilibrium holding. If H3 fails, the institutional participation value that helps H1 hold is reduced.

**H2d and H3 share the demand-correlated asset channel.** Wealthy taxpayers holding demand-correlated assets benefit from the labour tax relief dividend through higher valuations. This makes the H2d positive loop a partial incentive for H3 cooperation — taxpayers who expect H2d to operate have more reason to protect the institution that delivers it.

**H2d and H5 share a common vulnerability.** If automation destroys the consumer demand channel, H2d's positive feedback loop weakens at the same time that H5's base resilience claim is under stress. A hostile model that stresses both simultaneously would be more informative than separate tests.

**H2a is the most structurally independent.** The capital allocation efficiency claim does not require H2b, H2c, H2d, or H5 to hold. It can be tested in isolation and already has partial support from WFR. A researcher wishing to maximise the value of a single modelling effort should probably start here.

**H4 is the broadest claim and the deepest.** It does not depend on WDT being implemented or any of the other hypotheses holding. It is a claim about where in the economic process taxation is least distortionary. It therefore provides a theoretical foundation that supports or undermines the others depending on its result.

# 9. What Counts as a Serious Result

This section must appear in the paper to prevent goalpost movement. Three outcome categories are defined here and should be referenced in any modelling that engages with these hypotheses.

## 9.1 Support

The hostile model finds the hypothesis holds across a substantial range of empirically defensible parameters, with the supporting parameter range characterised.

## 9.2 Conditional support

The hypothesis holds only within an identifiable parameter range, with the binding constraints explicitly stated. Examples:

- H1 holds if and only if migration elasticity is below X;
- H2b holds if and only if labour displacement pace remains below Y;
- H3 holds if and only if Phase One SRR capitalisation exceeds Q;
- H2d holds if and only if the inflationary threshold for labour displacement is above Z.

Conditional support is a substantive result. It tells the Governing Council where the live parameters need to sit and gives Phase One a calibration target.

## 9.3 Falsification

The hypothesis fails across empirically defensible parameter ranges, or survives only under assumptions that are themselves implausible.

**"The model can be made to work" does not count as support.** Any hypothesis can be defended by choosing favourable parameters. The standard is that the hypothesis holds across a substantial and realistic parameter range, not that it holds somewhere.

# 10. Minimum Requirements for Hostile Modelling

Each hypothesis requires a different model type. This section specifies the minimum requirements for a model to constitute a serious attempt at falsification rather than a superficial exercise.

| Hypothesis | Model type | Minimum requirements |
|:---|:---|:---|
| H1 | Heterogeneous taxpayer choice | WDT percentile wealth brackets (P90, P95, P99, upper tail — values from RATES/JUR); current-system all-in friction cost explicitly modelled; migration as endogenous choice; institutional participation value allowed to vary |
| H2a | Portfolio-choice / asset-allocation | Revenue-equivalent CGT baseline; endogenous portfolio reallocation; productive vs unproductive investment distinction |
| H2b | Labour market / public finance welfare | Bilateral NICs removal modelled; WDT deadweight loss including valuation friction; LRR timeline as a variable not an assumption |
| H2c | Financial stability / crisis dynamics | Simultaneous household liquidity and government fiscal position; SWF capitalisation as a variable; asset-market amplification mechanisms included |
| H2d | DSGE or heterogeneous-agent GE | Full positive and negative loops operating simultaneously; consumer demand channel included; 2% central bank target as the inflation reference; LRR accumulation pace as a variable; capital-flight elasticity and avoidance rate as inputs not assumptions |
| H3 | Repeated game / institutional | Individual and collective capture; exit and compliance as strategic choices; SWF credibility as endogenous; bootstrapping phase explicitly modelled as a distinct period |
| H4 | Comparative welfare | All six tax systems on a common revenue-equivalent basis; valuation friction, liquidity, migration, and avoidance all included |
| H5 | Automation scenario | Multiple automation pace and concentration scenarios; avoidance capacity as a variable; H2d consumer demand channel included in the same model |

# 11. What Would Constitute Evidence Against WDT

The following results, if produced by a model meeting the minimum requirements above, would be serious evidence against WDT. They are listed here so they cannot later be dismissed as unimportant.

- The current system's all-in friction cost for wealthy UK taxpayers is materially and robustly below 0.35% across representative wealth levels. *(Falsifies H1)*
- The break-even WDT burden is below 0.35% at any of the four representative wealth levels. *(Falsifies H1 at that wealth level)*
- The capital misallocation cost of WDT valuation friction exceeds the lock-in welfare cost of CGT across realistic elasticity ranges. *(Falsifies H2a and H4 simultaneously)*
- The deadweight loss of WDT at reference parameters exceeds the deadweight loss of the labour taxation it displaces, or the LRR timeline cannot deliver credible displacement within a viable political window. *(Falsifies H2b)*
- SWF capitalisation cannot plausibly absorb refund outflows in a severe crash without producing net contractionary effects elsewhere. *(Falsifies H2c)*
- The negative macroeconomic loop dominates the positive feedback loop for realistic capital-flight and avoidance parameters, or constitutionally-paced LRR accumulation persistently breaches the 2% central bank inflation target, or the pace of displacement required to remain within that bound is insufficient to deliver meaningful fiscal substitution within a politically viable horizon. *(Falsifies H2d)*
- Individual capture of the Governing Council is always privately optimal regardless of institutional credibility, or the bootstrapping phase cannot reach Path A under plausible initial conditions. *(Falsifies H3)*
- After accounting for valuation, liquidity, migration, and avoidance, WDT generates greater total distortion than CGT or an income tax across realistic parameters. *(Falsifies H4)*
- Under plausible automation scenarios, WDT base growth does not exceed labour-tax revenue decline because automation-driven capital appreciation concentrates in a population with sufficient avoidance capacity to prevent base capture. *(Falsifies H5)*

# 12. Research Challenge

The WDT research programme has developed a theoretical and institutional architecture across 26 papers. Five propositions remain insufficiently tested. They concern wealthy taxpayer behaviour, capital allocation and macroeconomic dynamics, institutional cooperation, comparative tax efficiency, and automation resilience.

The author does not currently have the computational or empirical resources required to model all five adequately.

That limitation should not be treated as evidence for WDT.

It defines a research opportunity.

Researchers who believe that WDT would produce capital flight, reduced investment, institutional capture, fiscal instability, inflationary demand stimulus, or greater welfare loss than existing systems should be able to formalise those objections and test them against these hypotheses.

Researchers who find that these objections do not overturn the proposed mechanisms should report the parameter conditions under which WDT remains viable.

The most valuable contribution to this research programme would not necessarily be another paper defending WDT. A model demonstrating that one or more of these hypotheses fail under realistic assumptions would be a substantive result.

**The objective is not to protect WDT from falsification. It is to make falsification possible.**

The conclusion should not summarise the WDT architecture or defend the design. It should point outward.

# 14. Structure and Scope Notes

## 14.1 Target length

This paper should be short. The value is in the precision of the falsification conditions, not in the accumulation of argument. A target of 8,000–12,000 words of body text is appropriate. If it runs longer, sections 3–7 should be tightened before sections 9–12.

This phrase should appear at the end of each sub-hypothesis statement (H1, H2a–H2d, H3, H4, H5). It should not be softened or hedged. Where partial theoretical support exists (H2a, H4 via WFR Category 1 results), that should be noted, but the status line should still distinguish between theoretical support for a mechanism and quantitative establishment of the hypothesis.

## 14.4 Tone

This paper is unusual in the series. It should feel uncomfortable. It should be the paper the author would least like to be wrong about. The tone is analytical and self-critical rather than defensive. Any passage that sounds like it is defending WDT against the hypotheses it is supposed to be exposing should be cut or rewritten.
