# Publication shortcodes for the hub-and-spoke series

**Status:** working naming decision, 2026-11-17  
**Context:** brief 013g, restructure of the 33-paper series  
**Purpose:** give each proposed spoke and standalone paper a short, stable human-readable code.

## 1. Naming principles

The old series used shortcodes of roughly three to five letters. The replacement should retain that convention.

- Use a recognisable abbreviation of the paper's subject, not a sequence number.
- Keep codes between three and five letters.
- Use the same code consistently in discussion, planning documents and (once agreed) publication filenames.
- Do not encode publication priority or reading order in the code. No priority order has been agreed.
- Treat the old `S1` to `S12` labels as temporary planning references, not publication shortcodes.
- Reuse an existing shortcode where the subject carries over naturally (for example, `VAL`, `CORP`, `GOV` and `POL`). Avoid near-duplicates where they could be confused with a legacy paper code. In particular, use `FISC` rather than `RATE` or `RATES` for the rates-and-revenue spoke.

These are publication shortcodes, not yet the final site IDs, URL paths, registry keys or filename rules. Those belong to stage D of 013g and must be reconciled with the site and pipeline design before implementation.

| S12-A | **UNCER** | Empirical uncertainties | What is not yet known, evidence gaps and measurable hypotheses |
| S12-B | **LIMIT** | Structural limitations | Boundaries and limitations inherent in the design, even if it is implemented as intended |
| S12-C | **FALS** | Falsification | Evidence and conditions that would count against, or require rejection of, the central claims |

The S11 division distinguishes direct or first-order effects from wider transmission effects. It is not a claim that the former are necessarily easy to model or that the latter are merely speculative.

The S12 division separates three different questions:
- **UNCER:** What do we not know empirically?
- **LIMIT:** What can the design not do, even when it works as intended?
- **FALS:** What evidence would show that a central claim is wrong?

A limitation is not automatically a falsification. For example, dependence on functioning democratic institutions may be a structural boundary; evidence that a central behavioural assumption is false may undermine the proposal itself.

## 4. Boundaries between neighbouring papers

The shortcodes should make the publication structure easier to navigate, but they do not remove the need for clear editorial boundaries.

- **FAIR / LIT:** FAIR makes the normative case. LIT reviews prior work and identifies what remains unresolved. LIT should not become a second fairness argument.
- **MECH / other spokes:** MECH is the canonical account of how the mechanism operates. Other papers should refer back to it rather than independently re-explaining the whole system.
- **PAY / BEHAV / WELF:** PAY covers who bears the tax and relevant taxpayer circumstances; BEHAV covers responses to taxation; WELF compares welfare outcomes.
- **FIRST / WIDER:** FIRST covers direct effects and relatively short causal chains. WIDER covers effects transmitted through longer chains, including institutional and social consequences. Neither should duplicate the detailed treatment of liquidity (PAY), behavioural responses (BEHAV), welfare comparisons (WELF), fund operation (FUND) or corporate treatment (CORP).
- **UNCER / LIMIT / FALS:** empirical gaps, inherent design boundaries and disconfirming evidence are distinct categories. FALS should state meaningful tests of the proposal, not merely list objections and replies.

## 5. Ordering and publication status

No numeric priority or publication sequence is encoded in these codes. The order shown in the tables follows the current planning map for ease of comparison only.

The split of S11 into FIRST and WIDER, and of S12 into UNCER, LIMIT and FALS, records the conceptual divisions agreed in the 2026-11-17 discussion. The exact presentation as separate pages or as separately navigable parts of a larger publication can be reconciled with the stage D site design.

The codes are working publication names. They should be changed only deliberately and consistently if later author decisions or implementation constraints require it.
