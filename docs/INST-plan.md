**Working title:** Fiscal Architecture and Institutional Selection: The Wealth Delta Tax as a Missing Instrument of Democratic Competitive Fitness

## Section Structure

### §1 Abstract

Opens with the gap, not the solution.

The academic literature on institutional fitness and democratic resilience treats fiscal architecture as a fixed parameter — one governance system or another operates within whatever tax instruments happen to exist. This paper argues that assumption is wrong in a specific and consequential way: a fiscal instrument now exists whose stable implementation is constitutionally compatible only with democratic governance, and which unlocks a pool of private capital that authoritarian systems are structurally excluded from accessing. If the Wealth Delta Tax's properties hold under implementation, democracies may have been competing against authoritarian systems while leaving their most significant potential fiscal advantage entirely unmobilised — not through choice, but through the prior absence of a workable mechanism.

The paper does not demonstrate that advantage. It establishes the preconditions for it, identifies the structural mechanisms through which it would operate, and proposes the empirical programme that could confirm or falsify it. The central claim is a hypothesis, not a conclusion.

### §2 The Persistent Dominance of Authoritarian Governance

**Purpose:** Establish the empirical baseline. Authoritarianism is not a historical aberration being steadily displaced — it is the persistent equilibrium. The democratic interlude of 1945–2005 looks, on the longer view, like a fluctuation rather than a trend.

**Content:**

Five-tier governance classification (V-Dem Regimes of the World as the methodological anchor):
- Tier 1: Liberal democracy — free elections, rule of law, constitutional executive constraint
- Tier 2: Electoral democracy — competitive elections, basic rights, weaker institutional checks
- Tier 3: Hybrid / developmental — elections present but contested; developmental authoritarian states sit here
- Tier 4: Electoral autocracy — multiparty facade over concentrated power
- Tier 5: Closed autocracy — no meaningful competitive elections

Population by tier, seven snapshots 1800–2025 (order-of-magnitude estimates from V-Dem, Polity IV, Our World in Data). Key observations:

Tiers 1 and 2 combined never exceeded ~35% of world population even at the 2005 democratic peak. The peak was shallow and brief. The V-Dem 2026 report places current Tier 4 population at approximately 3.8 billion — 46% of humanity — with Tier 1 having contracted from its ~1.1 billion peak to roughly 600 million. The post-1989 democratic wave is now substantially reversed.

The structural reason for authoritarian persistence is not simply state violence, though that is a mechanism. Authoritarian systems have two genuine competitive advantages over democratic ones. First, they can impose costs on populations without electoral accountability — a demonstrated capacity to run populations at lower welfare thresholds during external pressure or internal hardship. Second, the "not knowing a better alternative exists" equilibrium: information suppression and restriction of comparison stabilises populations at welfare levels that would be politically unsustainable under free information. Both advantages are real and should not be minimised.

Authoritarian systems trade individual instance longevity for system-type persistence. No authoritarian state has a particularly long continuous record — dynasties collapse, regimes fall — but authoritarianism as a system type re-emerges persistently. Democratic systems theoretically trade short-run flexibility for long-run institutional resilience; the empirical record does not yet cleanly confirm this, partly because the liberal democratic form is historically recent and geographically narrow.

**Tier A:** V-Dem data, Polity IV, Our World in Data population figures.
**Tier B:** The characterisation of authoritarian competitive advantages as structural rather than contingent.

### §3 The Untaxed Pool: Private Wealth Concentration by Governance Tier

**Purpose:** Establish the size and governance distribution of the capital pool at the centre of the argument. Make the evolutionary pressure visible as a quantity.

**Content:**

Total global household wealth: approximately $570 trillion in 2025 (McKinsey Global Wealth Watch, August 2026), a fourfold increase from roughly $130 trillion in 2000. The growth rate of private wealth has materially outpaced the growth of state fiscal capacity in both democratic and authoritarian systems.

Distribution by governance tier (order-of-magnitude estimates, country-level aggregation mapped against V-Dem 2025/2026 classifications):

| Tier | Population | Wealth | Wealth/capita | WDT accessible? |
|------|-----------|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| T1 Liberal democracy | ~600M / 7% | ~$171T / 30% | ~$285k | Yes — fully |
| T2 Electoral democracy | ~1.0B / 12% | ~$57T / 10% | ~$57k | Partial |
| T3 Hybrid / developmental | ~800M / 10% | ~$57T / 10% | ~$71k | Fiscal yes, governance no |
| T4 Electoral autocracy | ~3.8B / 46% | ~$200T / 35% | ~$53k | No — self-defeating |
| T5 Closed autocracy | ~2.0B / 24% | ~$86T / 15% | ~$43k | No — structurally blocked |

The inversion: Tier 1 holds 30% of global wealth with 7% of population. Tier 4 holds 35% of global wealth with 46% of population. Neither system type is currently mobilising the upper-tail portion of its wealth pool at scale — conventional wealth taxes have failed systemically across both (POL §2 documents the OECD abolition record).

The crossover: WDT-inaccessible wealth (T4+T5, ~$286T) overtook WDT-accessible wealth (T1+T2, ~$228T) around 2013, driven primarily by Chinese private wealth accumulation. The gap is widening. This is the evolutionary pressure the paper analyses — a growing pool of private capital that neither system type is efficiently mobilising, but whose governance accessibility is asymmetric.

**Tier A:** McKinsey, UBS, Credit Suisse wealth data; V-Dem governance classifications.
**Tier B:** The governance accessibility column — derived from WDT mechanism analysis, not directly empirical.

### §4 Why Prior Mechanisms Failed: The Three Structural Failure Modes

**Purpose:** Establish why the untaxed pool has remained untaxed in democratic systems. The failure is not political will — it is mechanism failure. This section is load-bearing for the paper's central claim.

**Content:**

Twelve OECD countries levied individual net wealth taxes by 1990. Most had abolished them by 2020. Three structural failure mechanisms (POL §2):

**Legitimacy collapse.** Taxpayers experienced the tax as extraction without visible reciprocity. No mechanism connected their payment to a benefit they could observe or defend. Political support eroded once the distributional novelty wore off.

**Organised opposition advantage.** The taxed population — small, concentrated, wealthy, professionally advised — could mobilise political resistance at a cost far below what the diffuse majority could organise to defend. The structural asymmetry of political organisation, not the wealth tax itself, determined the outcome.

**Institutional hollowing.** Individually defensible exemptions accumulated invisibly into structural erosion. Each exemption had a legitimate-seeming rationale; their cumulative effect was a mechanism that taxed increasingly little of what it was nominally designed to tax.

All three failure modes are independent. A mechanism can survive one and fall to the others. Every prior OECD wealth tax fell to at least one; most fell to combinations. These are not failures of implementation — they are structural properties of the instrument designs that existed before the WDT.

The WDT addresses all three at the design level rather than the political management level (POL §3). The symmetric refund makes the cooperative character of the arrangement visible in the loss year rather than invisible in the gain year. The valuation architecture and Route C/D settlement rules make organised avoidance self-defeating rather than merely difficult. The ten enumerated structural clauses and three-chamber governance structure build constitutional resistance to hollowing into the mechanism rather than relying on political will to defend it. Whether those design responses are sufficient is a Phase One empirical question (PHASE1 §5). That they address the right targets is established by the failure analysis.

**Tier A:** POL §2–§3, GOV §5.2.
**Tier B:** The claim that WDT addresses all three failure modes at the design level.

### §5 The Four Conditions and Why Only Democratic Governance Satisfies Them

**Purpose:** The load-bearing structural argument. The WDT is the first proposed mechanism that plausibly satisfies four conditions simultaneously — and satisfying all four requires a specific governance architecture that is constitutionally incompatible with authoritarian systems.

**Content:**

The four conditions for a stable large-scale wealth tax:

**Condition 1: No accurate external valuation required.** The mechanism must not depend on the state certifying asset values it cannot directly observe. The WDT satisfies this through self-declaration routes — a declared value becomes the legally operative basis; tax liability flows from subsequent deltas (VAL §1). This condition is governance-neutral: authoritarian states could in principle implement it.

**Condition 2: Cooperation must be the path of least resistance.** The mechanism must make voluntary compliance more attractive than avoidance across a wide range of declaration strategies, without requiring enforcement of individual asset values. The WDT satisfies this through the tolerant zone (α ≈ 0.8–1.5 producing outcomes close to honest declaration), the Route C must-transfer rule, and the refund-protection asymmetry that gives risk-averse taxpayers under valuation uncertainty a modest incentive to declare accurately (VAL §7.1, BEHAV §8.11). This condition is partially governance-neutral: it requires the taxpayer to believe the refund guarantee is credible.

**Condition 3: The refund guarantee must be constitutionally protected against regime discretion.** The symmetric refund is the mechanism's cooperative character made concrete. If the guarantee can be suspended by the government of the day, the entire valuation architecture collapses — rational taxpayers will declare conservatively or not at all, anticipating that loss-year refunds will not be paid (GOV §4.2). This is the condition that breaks for authoritarian governance systems. Constitutional protection of the refund drawdown against governance discretion requires institutional constraints on executive power that are definitionally incompatible with concentrated authority. A government that can suspend the refund when convenient — which any government without binding constitutional constraints can do — destroys the cooperative mechanism.

**Condition 4: The taxable population must not be the governing coalition.** The WDT's support coalition is the majority — the Dividend Recipient chamber's 50% share is derived from the anti-collusion guarantee, filled by monthly lottery from the general population (GOV §3). The taxpayer chamber has formal standing but cannot dominate: unanimous DR opposition defeats any TP/FS joint proposal. This separation — taxable population as subject, majority population as governing coalition — is the condition that makes the three POL failure modes addressable. In authoritarian systems, the wealthy population and the governing coalition are fused by definition in predatory cases, or structurally entangled in developmental cases. The separation the WDT requires is, in varying degrees, what authoritarian governance cannot permit.

The developmental authoritarian case examined specifically. Developmental authoritarian states — the canonical cases being Park Chung-hee's Korea, Lee Kuan Yew's Singapore, Taiwan under the KMT, Deng-era China — partially satisfy Condition 4 through deliberate separation of economic and political power: the wealthy class may accumulate but may not convert wealth into political faction. This creates more WDT-compatible conditions than predatory authoritarianism. However, Condition 3 is not satisfiable without constitutional constraints on executive power that developmental authoritarian systems cannot accept without ceasing to be authoritarian in the relevant sense. A credible refund guarantee requires a mechanism the governing coalition cannot override. That requirement is the institutional boundary.

**Tier A:** VAL §1, §7.1; GOV §3, §4.2; BEHAV §8.11; POL §2–§3.
**Tier B:** The four-condition framework and the governance compatibility analysis.

### §6 The Structural Exclusion of Authoritarian Systems

**Purpose:** Synthesise the self-defeating mechanism and explain why it applies across the full range of authoritarian governance types, with developmental authoritarianism as the hardest case.

**Content:**

Three distinct exclusion mechanisms, each applying to a different part of the authoritarian spectrum:

**Predatory authoritarian exclusion (T5, most T4).** The wealth-holding population and the governing coalition are fused. Taxing the former is taxing the latter. The WDT's cooperative mechanism requires that the taxable population bear a burden that the governing coalition does not share; in predatory authoritarian states, this condition is simply not met. Any attempt to implement the mechanism faces immediate coalition defection — the people best positioned to bear the burden are the people whose continued support keeps the regime in power. This is not a political difficulty of the kind democratic wealth taxes face (organised opposition, lobbying); it is a structural impossibility.

**Electoral authoritarian exclusion (most T4).** Electoral autocracies run multiparty elections as legitimating facades while concentrating power. Their governing coalitions are narrower than democratic majorities and more dependent on elite support networks. The refund guarantee cannot be constitutionally protected because no independent institution with genuine authority to enforce it against the executive exists — the judiciary, legislature, and constitutional bodies that would need to function as enforcers are the very institutions the autocratic architecture keeps subordinate. Condition 3 therefore fails.

**Developmental authoritarian exclusion (T3 borderline cases).** The most interesting and analytically demanding case. The governing coalition and the wealthy class are partially separated; credible institutional commitments are possible in some domains (Singapore's institutional record on economic commitments is strong). The fiscal architecture — self-declaration routes, delta base, cooperative compliance logic — is potentially implementable. What is not implementable is the governance architecture: the three-chamber structure with constitutionally guaranteed refund drawdown and mandatory public transparency creates formal institutional checks on state power that developmental authoritarian systems cannot accept without beginning the transition toward democratic accountability they have historically managed to defer.

The pressure this creates is worth naming precisely. A developmental authoritarian state that wants the fiscal benefits of WDT implementation faces a choice: accept the governance constraints and begin an institutional trajectory toward Tier 2, or forgo the mechanism and accept growing fiscal disadvantage relative to states that implement it. This is not a moral argument for democracy — it is a structural pressure operating through fiscal self-interest. Its strength as a pressure depends entirely on how visible and large the fiscal advantage of WDT-implementing democratic states becomes in practice.

**Tier B throughout.** The exclusion mechanisms are derived from the WDT's design requirements and the structural properties of authoritarian governance. They are not empirically confirmed.

### §7 The Three Competitive Advantages

**Purpose:** State what democratic systems with WDT would gain, as precisely as the available evidence supports. Three distinct advantages, all conditional on Phase One demonstration.

**Content:**

**Advantage 1: The resource pool.** RATES establishes that at canonical parameters, the pre-behavioural combined estimate for a UK-scale jurisdiction (individual WDT ~£874b, corporate levy ~£172b, UHNW tail ~£27b) approaches near-parity with total managed expenditure of ~£1,157b (RATES §10). The revenue-weighted annual wealth burden is 0.35% of net worth — structurally below the 1–2% stock levy of conventional wealth tax proposals. At a rate equivalent to twice the canonical parameter — roughly 1% stock-equivalent — the arithmetic suggests a revenue roughly double current government expenditure at the median historical return scenario. The exact figures are not asserted here; the order of magnitude is. A democratic state with WDT access to this pool does not need to match authoritarian concentration ratios to achieve competitive resource parity. It needs sufficient absolute resources that the concentration advantage becomes less decisive than the total available.

**Advantage 2: The welfare floor.** Democratic systems have historically funded state capacity through three mechanisms — labour and consumption taxation, borrowing, and inflation — each of which imposes costs on the majority population. The WDT draws instead on the annual delta of the top wealth percentiles. The labour tax relief dividend (ENV §3) actively increases majority welfare simultaneously with increasing state fiscal capacity. There is no prior fiscal architecture that moves both variables in the same direction. The authoritarian endurance advantage — the demonstrated capacity to run populations at lower welfare thresholds — is eroded precisely in the scenario where it matters most: resource mobilisation for crisis response. A WDT-equipped democracy mobilising resources for an external challenge draws on wealth appreciation rather than majority purchasing power; a conventional democratic system mobilising for the same challenge reaches for borrowing and inflation, imposing costs the majority feels directly. The endurance asymmetry narrows.

**Advantage 3: Pre-built crisis capacity.** The SRR and LRR architecture (RATES §4–§6) provides pre-accumulated fiscal reserves that compound over the system's operating life. A democracy that has operated WDT for a decade before a crisis that tests it has a qualitatively different mobilisation position than one starting from zero. The bootstrapping problem — Phase One as the vulnerability window (POL §4, PHASE1 §7) — means this advantage is path-dependent. It accrues only to systems that commit early and maintain the mechanism through the accumulation period. It cannot be constructed rapidly in response to a recognised threat.

**Tier A:** RATES §10, ENV §3.
**Tier B:** The competitive advantage framing and the comparison to authoritarian endurance capacity.
**Tier C:** The claim that these advantages would be decisive in actual institutional competition.

### §8 Memetic Diffusion and the Race Condition

**Purpose:** The final and most speculative section. What happens when the structural argument is recognised by governments operating under competitive pressure? Four response options; their dynamics and costs.

**Content:**

The FM paper establishes a binary for any jurisdiction concluding the WDT's properties are likely true: commit or suppress, with no stable middle (FM §2). The present paper extends that binary to the systemic level: what happens when multiple governance systems simultaneously recognise that one system type has acquired or is acquiring a durable structural advantage?

Four response options:

**Option A — Adopt.** Implement the WDT and its governance architecture, accepting the democratic constraints that requires. Available in principle to Tier 2 and Tier 3 states; requires genuine institutional reform in most cases. The governance constraints are not negotiable components — partial adoption that implements the fiscal architecture without the constitutional refund guarantee does not produce the cooperative compliance dynamic and is likely to collapse through the same three POL failure modes prior wealth taxes encountered.

**Option B — Suppress.** Prevent recognition of the advantage through information control, counter-narrative, and isolation from jurisdictions demonstrating it. This is the option Tier 4 and Tier 5 systems are most structurally equipped to attempt. Its viability is time-limited: suppression of an idea is substantially easier than suppression of its observable consequences. Once WDT-implementing democratic states exhibit materially higher welfare, lower labour taxation, and larger crisis response capacity than comparable systems, the information to suppress is no longer an argument but an outcome.

**Option C — Race.** Attempt to implement WDT-compatible institutions rapidly enough to capture the advantage before early adopters accumulate insurmountable reserve differentials. The race, if it occurs, is not symmetric: the lifetime contribution envelope deepens with every year of system membership; the cooperative compliance norm strengthens with every assessed cycle; the membrane investment (BEHAV §5–§6) pays returns that a late entrant has not yet made. Strong path dependence advantages early movers. The FM paper's point that partial adoption is not available also applies here: a state that attempts the fiscal architecture without the governance architecture is likely to produce an unstable hybrid rather than a durable mechanism.

**Option D — Conflict.** Attempt to deny the advantage to existing adopters through active disruption before the reserve differential becomes insurmountable. This option is historically available and has precedent: periods of interstate conflict have partly been driven by recognition that a rival's institutional advantages were becoming durable. The deterrent against it is the accumulated reserve differential itself — a WDT-equipped democracy that has operated the mechanism for a decade enters conflict with a fiscal mobilisation capacity that cannot be rapidly replicated.

The diffusion pattern under competitive pressure is likely to be non-linear: fastest adoption at the Tier 2/3 boundary, where institutional proximity to the WDT's requirements is greatest and oligarchic opposition least entrenched; slowest or blocked at the Tier 4/5 core, where suppression is institutionally feasible and adoption structurally self-defeating. This means the advantage concentrates in the systems already closest to Tier 1, deepening the differential rather than closing it.

The paper's final hypothesis: the WDT may be the missing tool that democracies have not yet realised they are missing. Its significance, if that is correct, is not primarily fiscal. It is that changing the fiscal architecture changes the incentives that determine which political institutions can sustain themselves over time. Whether that change is large enough to alter the long-run balance between governance system types cannot be established from desk research. It requires Phase One implementation, a demonstrated track record, and eventually the comparative evidence on economic performance, political institutions, and state capacity that only a live system can generate.

**Tier B:** Options A–D and their structural dynamics.
**Tier C:** The diffusion pattern, the path-dependence claim, and the final hypothesis.

### §9 Failure Conditions

**Purpose:** Protect the argument by naming what would falsify it. Not a pro-forma caveat section — a genuine engagement with the conditions under which the thesis is wrong.

**Content:** Twelve conditions under which the predicted equilibrium would not materialise, grouped into three categories.

**Mechanism failures (the WDT does not perform as designed):**
1. Phase One does not generate cooperative compliance at scale — the tolerant zone and refund-protection asymmetry do not produce the predicted declaration behaviour.
2. The valuation architecture is captured — Route D auction fails to achieve deterrent credibility; professional valuation routes (A/B) are systematically gamed.
3. The SRR cannot sustain prolonged negative return periods — the RATES worst-case (2000 reference scenario) does not adequately represent the full range of stress conditions.
4. The membrane fails — legitimacy friction compounds from first contact; the cooperative norm is not established during Phase One.

**Competitive mechanism failures (the advantages do not translate into competitive fitness):**
5. Lower labour taxation does not materially increase output or majority welfare — the ENV displacement channel does not produce the predicted effects at scale.
6. Higher private wealth does not reduce welfare-state demand — the feedback loop from private resilience to reduced required expenditure does not operate at meaningful magnitude.
7. Authoritarian systems develop alternative mechanisms for accessing the upper-tail wealth pool — the structural exclusion argument holds only for current institutional forms, not for adaptive variants.
8. The cross-base migration externality (BEHAV §9.2, open question #5) is large enough to substantially erode the revenue base before the reserve differential accumulates.

**Evolutionary mechanism failures (the diffusion dynamics do not follow the predicted pattern):**
9. Democratic systems that implement WDT do not demonstrate materially superior outcomes on timescales that drive institutional imitation.
10. Option D (conflict) is chosen before the reserve differential becomes deterrent — the vulnerability window is exploited before it closes.
11. The bootstrapping problem is not solved — no jurisdiction commits to Phase One during a period of sufficient political stability.
12. The premise is wrong: the capital pool is not as large or as accessible as the WDT mechanism papers establish.

### §10 The Research Programme

**Purpose:** Convert the hypothesis into a set of testable propositions. The paper's contribution is not proof but a well-specified research programme.

**Content:**

Five empirical clusters, each with a primary observable:

**Cluster 1 — Mechanism performance.** Does Phase One generate the predicted cooperative compliance distribution? Observable: declaration ratio distribution (α) versus SWEEPS predictions; refund uptake in loss years; Route adoption distribution. Primary Phase One deliverable; cannot be advanced by further desk research (open questions #4, #13, #18, #29).

**Cluster 2 — Welfare effects.** Does labour tax relief dividend produce measurable majority welfare improvement? Observable: disposable income by decile, private wealth accumulation rate below the WDT threshold, welfare-state demand indicators. Phase One and Phase Two deliverables; some advance modelling possible via ENV framework.

**Cluster 3 — Fiscal capacity differential.** Does a WDT-equipped democracy accumulate a reserve differential large enough to be competitively significant? Observable: SRR and LRR trajectories versus RATES predictions; effective mobilisation capacity in the first stress scenario encountered. Phase One deliverable; RATES establishes the pre-behavioural baseline.

**Cluster 4 — Governance compatibility.** Do Tier 3 hybrid states that attempt WDT implementation face the predicted institutional pressure toward democratic accountability? Observable: institutional reform trajectories in early-adopter jurisdictions; cases where fiscal architecture is attempted without governance architecture. Comparative political economy; requires case study depth outside the WDT project's scope.

**Cluster 5 — Diffusion dynamics.** Does the predicted adoption pattern — fastest at Tier 2/3 boundary, slowest at Tier 4/5 core — emerge under competitive pressure? Observable: adoption sequencing across governance tiers; counter-narrative investment by non-adopting states. Long-run observable; cannot be tested before Phase One generates demonstrable outcomes.

## Notable Gaps in Cross-Reference Coverage

Three claims in the plan currently lack precise WDT paper anchors and will need either external literature or explicit labelling as Tier C:

1. **Failure condition 6** — "Higher private wealth does not reduce welfare-state demand." No ENV or PHASE1 measurement design currently addresses this directly. Either needs to be added to ENV's Phase One agenda or explicitly assigned to MACRO.

2. **Failure condition 7** — "Authoritarian systems develop alternative mechanisms for accessing the upper-tail wealth pool." This is pure Tier C — no WDT paper addresses adaptive authoritarian responses. The paper must be explicit that this failure mode is the one where the structural exclusion argument is most vulnerable to future developments.

3. **The 'not knowing a better alternative exists' equilibrium** (§2) — this is a claim about information suppression dynamics that has no WDT paper anchor. It will need external literature (communication economics, authoritarian information control) or explicit Tier C labelling.
