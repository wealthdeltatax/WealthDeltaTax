**§7.5 Portfolio Composition as a Structural Constraint on Declaration Deviation**

The preceding sections analyse declaration strategy through the lens of the C.1 metric and the tolerant zone. A critic might read those results and ask: if the zone runs from α = 0.8 to α = 1.5 with relatively modest consequences within it, what prevents a sophisticated taxpayer from systematically anchoring at the lower edge and extracting a persistent, if small, advantage? The answer lies in a constraint the simulation tables do not make explicit: α is a portfolio-level aggregate, and for the population the WDT primarily addresses, a large share of that portfolio is structurally locked to α = 1 regardless of declaration intent.

Routes A and B subject assets to independent professional valuation. The declared value for these assets is not the taxpayer's number — it is the valuator's determination, which the mechanism treats as the operative basis. A taxpayer cannot self-declare a listed equity or a professionally appraised property at a fraction of its independently established value without triggering immediate inconsistency detectable by the Administrator. For all practical purposes, Route A and B assets carry an imposed α = 1. They contribute no leverage to a declaration strategy directed at the portfolio aggregate.

This matters because portfolio α is a weighted average. A taxpayer with proportion *p* of their wealth on Routes A or B and proportion (1 − *p*) on Routes C or D has:

α\_portfolio = p · 1 + (1 − p) · α\_C/D

where α\_C/D is the declaration ratio applied to their self-declared assets. To achieve a target portfolio α, the required C/D declaration ratio is:

α\_C/D = (α\_target − p) / (1 − p)

The compounding is immediate. A taxpayer with 60% of wealth on Routes A/B who wants a portfolio α of 0.8 must declare their C/D assets at α = 0.5. To reach a portfolio α of 1.5 they need their C/D assets at α = 2.25. The structural anchor does not merely limit deviation — it amplifies the required C/D declaration to achieve any given deviation, making extreme portfolio positions require increasingly implausible asset-level declarations.

The following table illustrates the required C/D declaration ratio for a range of A/B share levels and target portfolio α values:

| A/B share | Target α = 0.7 | Target α = 0.8 | Target α = 1.2 | Target α = 1.5 |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 30% | 0.57 | 0.71 | 1.29 | 1.71 |
| 50% | 0.40 | 0.60 | 1.40 | 2.00 |
| 60% | 0.25 | 0.50 | 1.50 | 2.25 |
| 75% | 0.00 | 0.20 | 1.60 | 2.50 (impossible if C/D assets are honestly worth less) |
| 80% | — | 0.00 | 1.50 | 2.50 |

A taxpayer at the 80% A/B level cannot achieve a portfolio α below 0.8 without declaring their C/D assets at values approaching zero — a declaration that triggers the Route D auction's corrective pathway or, for Route C assets, an implausible must-transfer price that the mechanism enforces directly. At the upper tail, the C/D declaration required for portfolio α = 1.5 at 60% A/B exposure is 2.25 — more than double the asset's true value — which faces the full set of aggressive overstatement penalties analysed in §7.3 and (SWEEPS §2.2).

The constraint is strongest precisely where the fiscal stakes are highest. BEHAV.A §A.2–§A.5 establishes the route distribution by asset class, placing Route A assets (listed equities, liquid financial instruments, pension wealth) at approximately 40–55% of total WDT-taxable wealth at the population level — and concentrated disproportionately at the top of the distribution, where pension wealth, listed shareholdings, and professionally valued financial assets dominate. The ultra-high-net-worth population with V₀ above £100m — the bracket (SWEEPS Figure 11c) shows generates the sharpest incentive landscape — is also the bracket with the highest structural A/B exposure. The taxpayers for whom the revenue implications of declaration strategy matter most face the strongest structural constraint on how far their portfolio α can deviate from 1.0.

This does not eliminate declaration risk at the portfolio level. Route D assets — privately held non-fungible positions — retain genuine flexibility at entry, and the inception basis vulnerability noted in §14.4 applies here with full force. But the vulnerability is correctly characterised as concentrated in the C/D fraction of the portfolio, not distributed across the whole. A taxpayer whose wealth is 70% professionally valued has 30% of their net worth available for declaration strategy; a 12% shortfall on that 30% is a 3.6% shortfall on the total portfolio — within the tolerant zone's ±2% outcome range, and bounded by the costs that §7.2 and §7.3 establish apply on both sides of honest declaration.

The practical implication for enforcement is that the residual declaration risk sits in a specific and identifiable place: C/D assets, particularly Route D, particularly at entry. It does not require wholesale enforcement of every declared value. It requires attention to the distribution of entry declarations at inception and at inheritance — the two moments where Route D basis is most exposed — and to the pattern of declared values relative to realisation prices as Phase One data accumulates. That measurement agenda is specified in (PHASE1 §5.4), which should be read alongside this section.
