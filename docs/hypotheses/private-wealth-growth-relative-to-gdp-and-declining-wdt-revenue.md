# Hypothesis: private wealth can grow while WDT revenue falls relative to GDP

**Status:** Hypothesis for investigation; not an established model finding  
**Origin:** Review of the 055c range-runner results, 20 November 2026  
**Scope:** Model v3 revenue dynamics, especially the decline in post-fill fiscal relief as a share of GDP

## 1. The question

The 055c run found no feasible schedule on its tested 27-point grid. Short-window F1 was only 4.0% to 11.2%, against the 75% target. The accompanying constant-return illustration showed post-fill relief falling from about 5.7% to 2.9% of GDP over 100 years, while retired receipts remained at 4.74% of GDP.

Why should WDT revenue decline as a share of GDP if private wealth is assumed to keep increasing?

The key distinction is between:

- private wealth increasing in absolute real terms; and
- private wealth increasing as fast as GDP.

The first does not imply the second. A growing private-wealth stock can become smaller relative to the economy. If the taxable wealth base, or the annual wealth increments generated by it, shrink relative to GDP, WDT receipts can fall as a share of GDP even while private wealth continues to rise in pounds.

This is a plausible mechanism to investigate, not yet a demonstrated explanation of the 055c result.

## 2. The model assumptions that motivate the hypothesis

The current central register values include:

| Input | Register ID | Central value | Meaning |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Mean real arithmetic total return on wealth | I-D1a | 4.74% p.a. | Arithmetic return input |
| Mean real geometric total return | I-D1a2 | 4.42% p.a. | Geometric wealth-path return |
| Annual consumption share of net worth | I-D14c | 3.5% p.a. | Retained v2 setting, not a UK measurement |
| Historical real GDP-per-head growth | `returns.csv` | about 1.49% p.a. | Geometric growth over 1896–2019 |

The distinction between I-D1a and I-D1a2 matters. The 4.74% arithmetic mean is not the same thing as the 4.42% geometric mean used to describe a compounded wealth path. The model's relevant return convention must be checked carefully in each calculation.

The consumption parameter is especially important. I-D14c is explicitly a retained v2 run setting, not a measured UK consumption rate. It should therefore be treated as a modelling assumption to test, not as an empirical fact about how much wealth households consume.

## 3. A simple illustration

The model's wealth-growth step is represented in the current implementation as:

\[
W_{t+1}=W_t(1+r_t)(1-c)-\text{WDT paid}_t
\]

Before WDT is deducted, using the central arithmetic return and consumption settings gives:

\[
(1+0.0474)(1-0.035)-1 \approx 1.08\%
\]

So wealth still grows in real terms by about 1.08% per year under this simplified calculation, before WDT.

If real GDP per head grows at approximately 1.49% per year, the wealth-to-GDP ratio falls:

\[
\frac{W_{t+1}/GDP_{t+1}}{W_t/GDP_t}
=\frac{1.0108}{1.0149}
\approx 0.996
\]

Compounded over 100 years, this illustrative ratio falls to roughly two-thirds of its starting value. Private wealth has almost tripled in real terms, but GDP has grown more quickly.

This is an illustration of the arithmetic, not a forecast. It combines a historical GDP growth estimate with model settings, assumes constant rates, and excludes WDT itself, asset-composition changes, demographic changes and behavioural responses. The return convention also needs to be aligned with the actual kernel before this is used as a model result.

## 4. Proposed mechanism

The hypothesis is that the model's consumption and tax mechanics can cause private wealth to grow more slowly than GDP, reducing the WDT base relative to the economy.

A possible chain is:

1. Investment returns increase private wealth.
2. Consumption removes a material share of wealth each year.
3. WDT removes a further share of positive wealth increments.
4. If the resulting net growth rate of private wealth is below GDP growth, private wealth falls as a ratio of GDP.
5. If the minimum taxable wealth threshold is indexed to GDP per head, that threshold rises faster than the wealth of some taxpayers.
6. The taxable population, taxable wealth share, or taxable annual increments may consequently decline relative to GDP.
7. WDT revenue as a share of GDP can therefore fall, even though aggregate private wealth continues to increase in real terms.

The mechanism may be reinforced by the interaction between consumption, WDT deductions and progressive thresholds. It should not be assumed that every link operates in the current model without checking the relevant outputs.

## 5. Why this could matter for 055c

The 055c result is a fiscal-closure failure: the tested schedules do not maintain enough relief relative to the receipts from the taxes being retired.

If WDT's revenue-generating base grows more slowly than GDP while the retired tax receipts are held constant as a share of GDP, the model contains a structural mismatch. WDT relief would tend to decline relative to the fiscal obligation it is intended to replace.

This would not necessarily mean that WDT receipts decline in real currency terms. It means that receipts may decline relative to GDP, which is the unit used for the fiscal-closure comparison.

Nor would it establish that WDT cannot replace the retired taxes under any design. The result could depend on the consumption assumption, return assumptions, threshold indexation, tax schedule, supplementary revenue streams and the design of the sovereign wealth fund.

## 6. Tests required

The hypothesis should be tested by decomposing the revenue path, not by changing assumptions until F1 improves.

### A. Isolate consumption

Run otherwise identical paths with:

- zero consumption;
- the current 3.5% central consumption setting;
- a range of lower and higher consumption settings, including the registered low and high values.

Record private wealth, private wealth/GDP, taxable wealth, WDT receipts and total fiscal relief. The zero-consumption case is a diagnostic counterfactual, not a recommended assumption.

### B. Isolate WDT's own effect on compounding

Compare:

- returns only;
- returns plus consumption;
- returns plus consumption and WDT.

This separates the effect of consumption from the additional compounding effect of WDT deductions.

### C. Separate absolute growth from relative growth

For each scenario, report both real currency levels and GDP ratios for:

- aggregate private wealth;
- wealth above the taxable threshold;
- annual positive taxable wealth increments;
- WDT receipts;
- total fiscal relief.

This prevents a growing stock in pounds from being mistaken for a growing tax base relative to GDP.

### D. Test threshold indexation

Compare the current GDP-per-head-indexed threshold with a fixed real threshold and, if useful, alternative indexation rules. Report changes in taxpayer counts, taxable wealth and receipts. This identifies whether threshold drift is a material part of the effect.

### E. Decompose fiscal relief

Separate individual WDT receipts, corporate levy receipts, sovereign wealth fund income, reserve accumulation and any other components of total relief. The observed fall in total relief cannot be attributed to the individual WDT base without this decomposition.

### F. Check return conventions

Ensure the diagnostic uses the return measure actually used by the wealth-growth kernel. I-D1a (4.74% arithmetic) and I-D1a2 (4.42% geometric) are distinct inputs with different purposes. The earlier CK1 diagnostic also had a recorded mean-return input mix-up; any comparison using CK1 should use the corrected input.

## 7. What would support or weaken the hypothesis?

**Support:** The no-consumption scenario materially slows or removes the decline in wealth/GDP and WDT receipts/GDP; adding consumption recreates it; adding WDT deductions makes it worse. A falling taxable-wealth/GDP ratio and/or threshold-driven contraction explains a material share of the revenue decline.

**Weaken:** Private wealth/GDP and taxable wealth/GDP remain stable or rise, while WDT receipts/GDP still decline. That would direct attention to the tax schedule, revenue calculation, asset returns and composition, behavioural responses, fund mechanics or other components of fiscal relief.

**Mixed result:** Consumption explains part of the decline, but the remaining decline is caused by other model mechanics. This is entirely plausible and should be reported as a decomposition rather than forced into a single explanation.

## 8. Interpretation and limits

This hypothesis does not say that private wealth must grow more slowly than GDP in reality. It says that the current combination of model assumptions may imply that outcome, despite positive real wealth growth.

It also does not say that consumption is inherently a flaw in WDT. Consumption is a real use of wealth and should not be removed merely to improve fiscal results. The question is whether the chosen consumption parameter is defensible, whether its treatment is internally consistent, and what its implications are for the tax base over long horizons.

The central empirical question is therefore:

> Does the model's assumed net accumulation of private wealth, after consumption and WDT, keep pace with GDP over the long run?

Until the counterfactuals and revenue decomposition are run, this remains a testable explanation for the 055c decline, not a conclusion about its cause.
