## What the paper measures
- **Question:** do households with higher *lifetime* income save a larger share of income? Two UK routes, plus a US comparison (HRS).
- **Route 1, annual saving rates (LCFS, 1978-2009, 5,000 to 7,000 households a year).** Saving = current income less current spending, as a share of income; this is **active saving only** (no holding gains). Panel A counts mortgage capital repayment as saving, panel B does not.
- **Route 2, wealth at retirement against lifetime earnings (ELSA linked to National Insurance records from 1948; 1,073 couples, aged 60 to 75, England).** The ratio W/LR is accumulated wealth over lifetime resources. It mixes saving, returns and inheritance, and excludes gifts and inheritances received (a caveat the authors state).

## What it identifies, and what it does not
- **Identifies:** a **direction** (saving and wealth accumulation rise with permanent income in the UK, so cells defined by income should not share one saving rate) and an **order of magnitude** for a saving gap between the most-educated and others (about 4 points of income, median, active saving only) and for private wealth against lifetime earnings by decile (0.13 to 0.30, England, ages 60 to 75).
- **Does not identify:** saving by **wealth** band (ranking is by lifetime earnings or education); anything for the very wealthy (the authors say their sample does not over-sample them and cite Carroll 2010 that top US wealth is not explained by standard life-cycle models); gifts and inheritances; holding gains (route 1) or a split of wealth into saving and returns (route 2); drawdown in retirement beyond the age dummies; anything after 2009.
- **Mismatch to the model's cells:** WDT bands are by wealth, the paper's by lifetime income. High lifetime earners are not the same households as high-wealth households (the paper's own dispersion finding).

## Use for 096r cells (Scenario B)
- Supports giving the **working-saver** and **low-saving** cells different income-side saving, with the direction fixed by this paper and the **size left as a labelled judgement scenario** (a gap of the order of the 4-point median difference is the nearest UK bound, not a calibration).
- A wealth-to-lifetime-earnings ratio is a **validation moment** for the working-age to retirement path (private wealth about 0.13 to 0.30 of lifetime earnings at retirement by decile), not a parameter.
- Not usable for the **high-wealth cell**: the paper says it cannot speak to it. That cell stays unset or illustrative.
