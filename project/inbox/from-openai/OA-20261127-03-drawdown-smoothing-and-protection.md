## Answer

The most durable examples combine a published fiscal rule with a buffer and a mechanism for adjusting withdrawals gradually: Norway links spending over time to expected real returns but allows countercyclical deviations and multi-year smoothing; Alaska uses a five-year market-value average and a 5% draw ceiling; Chile's structural-balance framework used independent expert panels and accumulated assets before the global financial crisis, but its target was revised repeatedly and the rule suspended in 2010. The protection record is mixed. Alaska's constitutional principal and citizen-facing dividend create political visibility, but the earnings reserve remains appropriable and the constitution can be amended. Alberta demonstrates that a fund without equivalent constitutional protection can have inflows stopped and earnings diverted for decades. Botswana's Pula Fund has a legal basis but the IMF notes that it is not legally separated from foreign-exchange reserves and has no statutory deposit or drawdown rules.

### What is weak or conflicting

- Norway is rule-based but includes significant discretionary judgement over the cycle and after large market movements. It survived shocks as a framework, not by mechanically enforcing a fixed annual payout.
- Chile accumulated fiscal space before the global financial crisis, but IMF records revisions and suspension. It is evidence for expert inputs and transparent escape clauses, not proof that a numerical rule cannot change.
- Alaska's constitutional principal is a genuine legal barrier, but the spendable ERA and annual appropriation channel remain exposed to ordinary politics. A popular dividend creates political support but does not lock the money.
- Alberta's history is a counterexample to assuming that statutory designation, investment governance or institutional longevity is enough to prevent diversion.
- IMF's 2024 Botswana diagnosis is staff advice, not a judicial finding. It identifies a specific weakness: no legal separation and no binding deposit/withdrawal rule.
- No controlled empirical estimate was found for how much each protection reduced reversal risk. “Worked” means documented arrangement and observed survival, not causal estimate.

## Relevance to our design

For the **long reserve** and **trigger**, Norway and Alaska provide distinct smoothing choices: a multi-year market-value average with a hard percentage ceiling, versus a long-run expected-return anchor with cyclical discretion and gradual adjustment. Chile provides a structural-revenue trigger based on independent estimates, plus a warning that emergency flexibility needs an explicit route back to the normal rule. For the **short reserve**, the question is whether a hard ring-fence can be paired with a published restoration mechanism rather than discretionary replenishment. For the **cap** and **excess release**, Alaska and Alberta show why legal status matters: constitutional protection can shield principal, while statutory funds can have inflows stopped and earnings diverted. For **excess release**, Norway is not a stock-cap precedent: its fiscal rule allocates withdrawals to the budget over time rather than automatically sweeping excess above a balance ceiling. The repo's aim of making the SWF economically essential is a political-economy hypothesis; visible beneficiaries, transparent rules and institutional separation may help, but none makes reversal impossible.

## What I could not check

- Full statutory governance and investment mandates for Norges Bank and Alaska Permanent Fund Corporation.
- Primary-source time series measuring the causal effect of Alaska's dividend on political support.
- Full Chilean PRF withdrawal regulation issued after the 2024 amendment and its performance under a shock.
- Detailed history of fund capture/reversal across a wider sample of failed sovereign funds.
- Exact legal steps and voting threshold required to amend Alaska's constitutional principal protection.

## Suggested next step

Separate three design questions: (1) whether short-reserve assets are legally ring-fenced; (2) whether long-reserve withdrawal trigger is formulaic or discretionary; and (3) whether excess release is automatic or can be suspended by a budget vote. For any escape clause, specify declaration authority, publication requirement, duration and return-to-rule procedure.
