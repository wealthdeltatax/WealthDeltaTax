## Answer

Completed research notes for all four questions in brief 185r: fund-size caps and excess release; reserve sizing against outflows; rule-based drawdown and smoothing; and institutional protection against reversal or capture. The notes cover the named candidate families, distinguish caps from payout ceilings and reserve targets, and identify where a primary-source rule or figure could not be established.

## Evidence

- OA-20261127-01-fund-caps-and-excess-release.md: Alberta, Alaska, Chile, Norway, FDIC, Botswana and New Zealand. Main distinction: stock cap versus annual draw ceiling versus reserve floor / target.
- OA-20261127-02-reserve-sizing-and-outflow-cover.md: FDIC, OASDI, CPP, US unemployment insurance and Botswana, with limits of comparing contingent insurance claims to earned or scheduled outflows.
- OA-20261127-03-drawdown-smoothing-and-protection.md: Norway, Alaska, Chile, New Zealand, Botswana and Alberta, including documented examples of rules flexed, funds diverted or legal separation lacking.
- The repository's 2026-11-25 decision entry defines the WDT short reserve as the ring-fenced refund reserve, long reserve as stabiliser, cap as short plus long, excess release as accelerated tax replacement, and replacement time as headline outcome.

## What I could not check

I did not inspect every fund's consolidated legislation or annual reports, and did not build a complete global dataset. Chile's PRF size-unit wording and post-2024 withdrawal regulation need primary legal verification. Current US state UI reserve multiples were not collected. No causal estimate of political protection or capture resistance was found. All web claims remain marked verified: unverified.

## Suggested next step

The guru should spot-check primary sources and decide which mechanisms merit a formal design comparison. The notes make no recommendation and change no repository decision.
