6. CONCLUSION
This article has studied the impact of housing transaction taxes based on administrative stamp
dutyrecordsandquasi-experimentalvariationfromnotchesandstimulus.Wehaveshownthat
the housing market responds very strongly and quickly to transaction taxes across a range
margins, making such taxes very distortionary compared to standard recurrent taxes. We have
interpretedourresultsinthecontextofahousingmodelwithdownpaymentconstraints(building
onStein,1995),arguingthattheliquidityandleveragechannelsamplifybehaviouralresponses
totransactiontaxesandprovidesanaturalexplanationforthelargebehavioralresponseswefind.
Ourmostimportantandnovelfindingsarethoserelatedtothe2008–09stampdutyholiday.
These findings contribute to the large macro literature on fiscal stimulus, presenting some of
thefirstevidenceontheeffectivenessofusingtemporarytaxchangestostimulatethehousing
marketduringeconomicdownturns.Ourstimulusanalysiscarriestwoqualitativelessons.First,
